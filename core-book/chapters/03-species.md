# Species

::: epigraph The System
Welcome to the Identity Market. Everything here is your size, within reason, and none of it is returnable. Please do not touch the merchandise. The merchandise is you.
:::

Every Crawler walked into the Dungeon wearing the body they were born in. That body has been quietly edited. Your species, which the System calls your **Latent Biology**, is chosen at level 1 like any fifth edition species, and its traits work from your very first session. For two floors your body keeps the secret. On Floor 3 the secret comes out, and the System offers you a chance to become something else entirely.

This chapter covers what your species means in the Crawl, what happens at the Floor 3 **Identity Market**, the rules for rebuilding a Crawler there, and the seventeen **oddities**: the strange new species the System can offer you. Chapter 2 has the kiosk names, the trait list and the 24 lineages for the nine starting species; this chapter does not reprint them.

::: sidebar Where the Rules Live
Species traits come from the **SRD 5.2** and are named, not reprinted. The oddities are ours: every number in their entries was written for this game. Where a rule here and the SRD disagree on the floor, this book wins for Crawl overlays (saferooms, the Offer, rebuilds).
:::

## Latent Biology

You pick your species at level 1, for balance, and **every trait works from the start**. Darkvision sees in the dark on Floor 1. A Halfling's Luck rerolls a 1 in the tutorial. A Dragonborn's Breath Weapon clears a hallway of rats before anyone has learned the word "saferoom." Nothing is held back.

What is held back is the *look*. On Floors 1 and 2 everyone looks human. The System files your traits as **quirks**, each with a mundane-sounding excuse ("your pupils do a thing"), and toasts the first one that fires as a snark-only **Biology Anomaly (Logged)**. It never explains. If you ask why you can see in the dark, your file is under review. {{HOUSE}}

### Small Tells

You look human, but not *perfectly* human. Now and then the GM describes a **small tell** of your real species: a detail, never a feature. No horns, tails, scales, wings or fur appear on camera, and your height never changes. Tells are subtle and rare, once or twice a floor, and the camera never lingers on them. {{HOUSE}}

| Starting species | Example tells |
|---|---|
| Welsh (Dragonborn) | Breath that fogs in a warm room; a faint scale pattern on the knuckles in the cold |
| Vietnamese (Dwarf) | Eyes that catch the light in the dark; the scale says you weigh 40 lb more than you look |
| Icelandic (Elf) | Ear tips a touch too pointed; ears that twitch at small sounds; you never quite yawn |
| Polish (Gnome) | Eyes a shade too bright; a laugh pitched where dogs notice |
| Samoan (Goliath) | Grey mottling on the shoulders like stone; every doorframe feels low |
| Belgian (Halfling) | Bare feet that never get cold; you always spot the coin on the floor |
| Canadian (Human) | None. That is the tell. The System finds it suspicious. |
| Azerbaijani (Orc) | A too-sharp lower canine; bruises gone by morning |
| Brazilian (Tiefling) | Warm eyes in the dark; a horn-shaped shadow that isn't there when you turn |

- **Tells have no mechanics.** A tell gives no bonus and no penalty. NPCs don't react to one beyond a glance.
- **Size is still real.** A Halfling or Gnome is Small in the rules from level 1. The System's chyron calls them "compact."
- **Mirrors flicker.** Reflective surfaces in the Orientation Pit show a frame of the truth (an ear, a horn, a tail-shaped shadow) and the camera cuts away.
- **Your Look stays human.** When you write your Look at intake (Chapter 2), leave species features out, or wear them as a costume. The GM handles the tells.
- **Comfort settings apply.** If you opted out of body humor, your tells stay plain and nobody jokes about them.

## The Nine Species in the Crawl

Your starting species is one of the nine in the SRD, shown at the kiosk as **Our Name (5e Name)**. The nationality is the System's opinion of you; the parenthesized name is the species whose rules you use. Chapter 2 lists each species' size, speed and traits, and the lineage or ancestry choices for five of them. Below is how each one tends to play once the doors lock.

**Welsh (Dragonborn).** Your Breath Weapon replaces one of your attacks and fills a line or a cone, and Dungeon corridors were practically drawn for it. Pick an ancestry whose damage type the floor's mobs don't shrug off, and enjoy resisting the same type when the traps use it. Darkvision means you're the one who sees what's in the vent.

**Vietnamese (Dwarf).** Darkvision to 120 feet makes you the party's flashlight. Stonecunning turns any stone floor into an early-warning system: a Bonus Action of tremorsense and you know what's coming around the corner. Dwarven Toughness adds a hit point every level, and you resist poison, which matters more than you'd think in a place with vending machines.

**Icelandic (Elf).** Trance lets you finish a Long Rest in four hours of meditation and keeps magic from putting you to sleep. You still need a saferoom to rest, but you're up first, every time. Keen Senses gives you Insight, Perception or Survival, and Fey Ancestry shrugs off charm. Your lineage (Drow, High Elf, Wood Elf) decides whether you're the scout, the trickster or the runner.

**Polish (Gnome).** Gnomish Cunning gives you Advantage on Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma saving throws, and the Dungeon throws a *lot* of those at you. You're Small, so Heavy weapons are a bad idea. Your lineage hands you cantrips that are better at problem-solving than they look.

**Samoan (Goliath).** Powerful Build lets you carry like a forklift and slip a grapple easily. Your giant ancestry gives you a few uses per Long Rest of something loud: a teleport, extra fire or cold, a knockdown, a damage soak. From level 5, **Large Form** makes you Large for 10 minutes at a time; use the Large rules later in this chapter while it lasts.

**Belgian (Halfling).** Luck rerolls every natural 1 on a d20 test, and in a game where the System rolls in the open, that ding is the sweetest sound on the floor. Brave helps against fear, Nimbleness lets you walk through bigger creatures' spaces, and you can hide behind your own party. Small: no Heavy weapons.

**Canadian (Human).** Resourceful gives you Heroic Inspiration after every Long Rest, Skillful adds a skill, and Versatile adds a second Origin feat. Humans are the most flexible starting species and the only one with **no tells at all**. A Human who keeps Human at Floor 3 gains **Unbroken Original**, described below.

**Azerbaijani (Orc).** Relentless Endurance drops you to 1 hit point instead of 0 once per Long Rest. It is a species trait and works as written; it isn't one of the System's near-death buffers. Adrenaline Rush lets you Dash as a Bonus Action for temporary hit points, which is exactly what a collapsing floor wants from you. Darkvision reaches 120 feet.

**Brazilian (Tiefling).** Your Fiendish Legacy gives you a damage resistance and a cantrip at level 1 and more spells at levels 3 and 5. Otherworldly Presence adds Thaumaturgy, which is mostly for theater, and theater is how the Crawl keeps score.

::: sidebar Pick for the Floor You're On
Your starting species will carry you through Floors 1 and 2, and you can keep it forever. Choose the one that fits the Crawler you want to play now. Floor 3 offers changes; it never requires them.
:::

## Floor 3: The Identity Market

On Floor 3 the Crawl reaches the **Kingdom Continuum**, and the System decides your file is ready. Every Crawler gets **one** chance to keep what they are, or to be dissolved, reorganized and reissued as something new. In the source series, the System recommends a handful of races out of hundreds at this point. Our version is smaller, private, and comes with a background and class option attached. {{INSPIRED}}

### When It Opens

The Identity Market opens in the **first saferoom of Floor 3**, during **Floor Onboarding**, right after you reach level 3. You can pick your level 3 subclass then, or wait to see your Offer first.

- **It opens before your first Long Rest on Floor 3 finishes.** The rebuild itself is not a rest and doesn't count as one.
- **It stays open until the party leaves Floor 3** by the stairwell. Take your time, ask questions, sleep on it.
- **An absent player's Offer waits** for them. A Crawler running on Standing Orders never accepts an Offer.
- **An Offer still unanswered at the Floor 3 stairwell counts as declined.** You keep everything as it is, and your starting species manifests.

The System makes one public announcement to the whole party. Then each Crawler gets their own card.

::: system
Congratulations, Crawlers. Your Latent Biology file is out of review. Each of you will be shown three options. You may keep what you walked in with, or you may be reissued in a new body at no charge. One time. No refunds, no exchanges, no screaming. Your faces are about to stop lying.
:::

### Your Offer Card

Your **Offer Card** is private. Only you see it, though you may show it to anyone you like. It has three parts, and each part is free, optional and used once.

| Part | Your choices |
|---|---|
| **Species** | **A. Keep** your starting species (always offered, always free). **B** or **C**: one of **two oddities** the System picked for you. |
| **Background** | Keep your Prior Employment, or **redo** it under the same rules you used at level 1. |
| **Class** | Keep your class and pick any subclass as normal, or take one of **two class options** built on your class (see below). |

**The System picks your two oddities.** It picks the ones it thinks suit you, and it won't tell you why; the card's rationale line usually just says *"The System noticed things."* The System is petty, but it **never offers a trap**: both oddities are real trade-offs, and the card states every cost plainly.

**The card shows its math.** Each oddity lists what you **gain** at your level, what you **lose** from your starting species, what it **costs** (its drawback), and any **warnings**: a gate you'd need to meet, a Small body with a Heavy weapon in your hands, a Large body on a floor full of narrow doors. If you're Human, option A shows Unbroken Original. **Asking questions and previewing are free.**

### Choosing

You give three answers, privately if you like: **species** (A, B or C), **background** (keep or redo), and **class** (keep, option 1 or option 2). You may change your mind until the transformation starts. After that, every choice is locked.

- **Keep everything** and nothing changes on your sheet, except that a Human who keeps Human gains Unbroken Original. Your starting species **manifests**: the horns, ears, scales, tail or stone-grey shoulders arrive on camera, and the System has a line about it.
- **Take anything else** and you rebuild (see "Rebuilding" below), then the transformation scene runs.
- **The result is announced.** Everyone learns your new species and your Official Class Title. Nobody learns what you turned down unless you tell them.

### Class Options

Your Offer Card also shows **two class options** for the class you already have. A class option is a named Crawl class built on **your same class**: it sets your subclass and adds one small **Perk** and one small **Negative**. It is free and optional, like the species rebuild, and you can take it once. Your level, Hit Point Dice, maximum hit points, class features, spells and equipment stay. If you had already picked a different level 3 subclass, it is swapped for the option's subclass. The option's name becomes your **Official Class Title**. There is no swap to a different class. {{HOUSE}}

**Built on.** The first option of each class uses an SRD subclass so every table can run it. The second uses a 2024 Player's Handbook subclass when the table owns the book; otherwise it uses the SRD fallback in brackets, with the same Perk and Negative. Names are locked from the display table. {{INSPIRED}}

If a Perk gives a proficiency you already have, pick another of the same kind (skill for skill, tool for tool). A Crawler below level 3 who locks an option in gets the Perk and Negative at once; the subclass arrives at level 3.

**Anger-Management Dropout (Barbarian)**

- **Football Hooligan (Barbarian).** Built on Path of the Berserker. **Terrace Chant:** when you start a Rage, one ally within 30 feet who can hear you gains Temporary Hit Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus. **Red Card:** you can't take the Disengage action while Raging.
- **Feral Cat Berserker (Barbarian).** Built on Path of the Wild Heart (SRD fallback: Berserker). **Alley Climber:** while Raging you have a Climb Speed equal to your Speed. **Hates Baths:** Disadvantage on Strength (Athletics) checks to swim.

**Open-Mic Menace (Bard)**

- **Song Bard (Bard).** Built on College of Lore. **Encore:** once per Long Rest, when a creature rolls your Bardic Inspiration die and still fails, that use isn't spent. **Can't Stop Humming:** Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks to move quietly.
- **Legendary Diva (Bard).** Built on College of Glamour (SRD fallback: Lore). **Stage Presence:** Advantage on Charisma (Performance) checks in front of three or more watchers. **Sung Spells:** every spell you cast must be sung; you can't cast while you can't sing (silenced, gagged, underwater).

**Urgent-Care Chaplain (Cleric)**

- **Physicker (Cleric).** Built on Life Domain. **Triage:** proficiency with the Herbalism Kit, and you can stabilize a creature at 0 HP as a Bonus Action (Wisdom (Medicine) as normal). **Bedside Manner:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Intimidation) checks.
- **Fire Spiritualist (Cleric).** Built on Light Domain (SRD fallback: Life). **Juiced:** once per Long Rest, as a Bonus Action, one ally within 30 feet has Advantage on its next attack roll before the end of its next turn. **Pilot Light:** you glow faintly; Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks in darkness.

**Community-Garden Zealot (Druid)**

- **Banana Farmer (Druid).** Built on Circle of the Land. **Banana Seed:** proficiency with the Herbalism Kit; after each Long Rest you grow one banana that heals 1d4 HP as a Bonus Action. It rots at your next Long Rest and can't be sold or traded. **Fruit Flies:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks indoors with strangers.
- **Earth Mover (Druid).** Built on Circle of the Moon (SRD fallback: Land). **Stone Feet:** Advantage on saving throws against being knocked Prone or moved against your will while you stand on stone or earth. **Heavy Tread:** Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks on hard floors.

**Off-Duty Bouncer (Fighter)**

- **Pit Fighter (Fighter).** Built on Champion. **Crowd Work:** proficiency in Intimidation or Performance. **Showboat:** Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks to hide during combat.
- **Boring Ol' Fighter (Fighter).** Built on Battle Master (SRD fallback: Champion). **Basics Done Right:** one extra Fighting Style feat. **No Magic:** you can't cast spells from any source (class, species, feat, scroll or item); spell scrolls are sell-only for you. Magic weapons, armor and potions still work. Not offered if you already have species or feat spells.

**Barefoot Hot-Yoga Brawler (Monk)**

- **Prizefighter (Monk).** Built on Warrior of the Open Hand. **Pugilist:** proficiency in Athletics. **Punch-Drunk:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks in formal settings (the GM judges).
- **Street Monk (Monk).** Built on Warrior of Shadow (SRD fallback: Open Hand). **Parkour:** climbing walls, buildings and scaffolding costs you no extra movement. **No Fixed Address:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks with officials on first meeting.

**HOA Enforcement Crusader (Paladin)**

- **Sacred Paladin (Paladin).** Built on Oath of Devotion. **Liturgy:** proficiency in Religion, and you know the Light cantrip (Charisma). **Can't Lie:** you can't tell a flat lie; a Deception check that needs one fails. Misleading truths are fine.
- **Black Inquisitor General (Paladin).** Built on Oath of Vengeance (SRD fallback: Devotion). **Mage Training:** you know one Wizard cantrip (Charisma). **Dread Reputation:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks on first meeting with clergy and lawkeepers.

**Seasonal Park Ranger (Ranger)**

- **Pest Exterminator (Ranger).** Built on Hunter. **Tent the House:** once per Long Rest you can cast Fog Cloud without a spell slot (Wisdom). **Bug-Spray Smell:** Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks against creatures that hunt by scent.
- **Archer (Ranger).** Built on Gloom Stalker (SRD fallback: Hunter). **Archery Training:** the Archery Fighting Style feat (or another Fighting Style if you already have it). **Arrow Snob:** Disadvantage on attack rolls with thrown and improvised weapons.

**Five-Finger Discount Specialist (Rogue)**

- **Fugitive (Rogue).** Built on Thief. **Slippery:** Advantage on checks to escape a grapple or nonmagical restraints. **Wanted:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Persuasion) checks with guards and militia.
- **Spy (Rogue).** Built on Assassin (SRD fallback: Thief). **Tradecraft:** proficiency with the Disguise Kit. **Burned Cover:** Disadvantage on Charisma (Deception) checks against anyone who has caught you in a lie on this floor.

**Leaky Power-Bank (Sorcerer)**

- **Blizzardmancer (Sorcerer).** Built on Draconic Sorcery (cold ancestry). **Cold Snap:** you know Ray of Frost; it doesn't count against your cantrips. **Ice Queen:** Disadvantage on saving throws against extreme heat.
- **Glass Cannon (Sorcerer).** Built on Wild Magic (SRD fallback: Draconic). **Overclocked:** your Sorcery Point maximum is higher by your Proficiency Bonus. **Fragile:** your Hit Point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to your level.

### Once Only

The Offer happens **once per Crawler**. The rebuild is **irreversible**. Nothing in the Crawl, not a box, a sponsor, a potion or a favor, ever reopens the Offer or rebuilds you a second time. If you keep your species, you can't take an oddity later. If you take one, you can't go back. **No reissue, ever.**

## Staying Human: Unbroken Original

About the only thing rarer than an oddity in the Dungeon is a Crawler who looks at a menu of wings, fangs and extra heads and says *"No thanks, I'm good."* The System respects that, in its way. {{HOUSE}}

**Who gets it.** A Crawler whose starting species is **Human** and who picks **option A (Keep)** at the Floor 3 Offer. A Crawler created as Human after Floor 3 also gets it (see "Replacement Crawlers" at the end of this chapter). Redoing your background or taking a class option doesn't affect it.

**What you keep.** Resourceful, Skillful and Versatile, exactly as the SRD gives them, including the bonus Origin feat from Versatile.

**What you gain: Unbroken Original.**

- **Crowd Favorite.** **Once per floor**, when the crowd would love it (a stunt, a stand, a perfect line; the GM decides), you gain **Heroic Inspiration**. If you already have it, you can give it to another Crawler who doesn't.
- **Original Certificate.** At the Floor 3 Offer you gain **one extra Origin feat** you don't already have. A Repeatable feat can be taken again as its text allows. In this book, pick from the four SRD Origin feats: Alert, Magic Initiate, Savage Attacker or Skilled.

**Your Versatile feat and your Original Certificate feat are species feats**, not background feats, so redoing your background never removes them.

**Leaving Human.** A Human who takes an oddity loses Resourceful, Skillful and Versatile, **including the Versatile bonus Origin feat**, and never gets Unbroken Original. The Offer Card says so on its "loses" line.

**Manifesting.** You manifest as exactly yourself, with no tells. The System hands you a laminated "Original" certificate. It is cosmetic. It is also laminated, which is more than most Crawlers get.

## Rebuilding

A rebuild has **three parts**, and you may take any, all or none of them, once each: a **species swap** (option B or C), a **background redo**, and a **class option**. It happens only at the Floor 3 Offer, or when a Crawler is created after Floor 3. {{HOUSE}}

### What a Species Swap Changes

Everything that came from your old species goes. Everything from your new one arrives at your **current level**.

| Area | What happens |
|---|---|
| Creature type | You're Humanoid before and after. Every oddity is Humanoid. |
| Size and speed | Replaced by the oddity's. Large Crawlers have their own rules below. |
| Senses | Darkvision, tremorsense and the like are replaced. |
| Resistances and save bonuses | Replaced. |
| Species skills and tools | The old ones go, unless your class or background also gives them. If the new species gives one you already have, pick a different skill or tool of the same kind. |
| Species spells | The old ones go. New species spells use Intelligence, Wisdom or Charisma, your choice when you take them. Class spells are untouched. |
| Species feats | The old ones go, including a Human's Versatile feat. |
| Limited-use traits | Old ones disappear. New ones start **fully available**. |
| Languages | Unchanged. Your languages come from your background. |

### What Stays

Your level, class, Hit Point Dice, class spells and slots, class equipment, backstory, Broadcast Name, achievements, credits, loot boxes, conditions and Exhaustion. Your background stays unless you redo it. Your subclass stays unless you take a class option.

**Ability scores never change from a species swap.** In these rules your ability increases come from your background, not your species. Your **base scores** (from the standard array, point buy or the Roll method) never change at all.

**Hit points.** Your class hit points and Constitution bonus stay as they are. The only species hit-point trait among the nine is Dwarven Toughness: **a Dwarf who takes an oddity loses 1 hit point of maximum per level.** No oddity adds any. Your current hit points stay where they are, capped at your new maximum, and the rebuild never drops you below 1.

Recalculate **Armor Class, initiative and saving throws** from your new traits. Your Proficiency Bonus comes from your level and doesn't change.

### Redoing Your Background

You may redo your **Prior Employment (background)** once, with or without a species swap.

- **Out:** the old background's ability score increases, its two skills, its tool and its Origin feat, along with anything that feat gave you.
- **In:** the new background's, chosen exactly as at level 1. Apply **+2 and +1**, or **+1 to all three**, among its three listed abilities. No score can go above 20.
- **Base scores stay.** Only the background increases move.
- **Knock-on effects.** If your Constitution changes, recalculate your maximum hit points; your current hit points are capped at the new maximum and never drop below 1. Recalculate Armor Class, saves, skill bonuses, spell save DC and attack bonuses.
- **Gear.** You keep the old background's starting equipment. The new background gives no equipment and no credits.
- **Same list.** The same backgrounds you could pick at level 1 (Chapter 2), shown under their Prior Employment names.

### Resources, Gear and Attunement

- **The transformation is not a rest.** It restores no hit points, spell slots or Hit Point Dice. Traits from your **new** species start full, a one-time gift of the new body; spent uses of old traits are simply gone.
- **Your gear fits.** The System retailors your clothing, armor and normal gear to the new body (*"Wardrobe Adjustment, no charge, no dignity"*). Magic items resize as fifth edition allows. You may re-equip freely at the market.
- **Small bodies and Heavy weapons.** If you become Small, you have Disadvantage on attack rolls with Heavy weapons, as the SRD says. The card warns you.
- **Attunement stays at three.** If an item needs attunement by a species or creature type you no longer are, the attunement ends and the slot is free; attune to something else at your next Short Rest.
- **Curses stay.** If a curse names your old species, the GM adapts it to the new one.

### Natural Weapons

Many oddities grow claws, fangs, horns, jaws or a tongue. A **natural weapon** is an **Unarmed Strike** that deals the listed damage die and type instead of the usual 1 plus your Strength modifier.

- You're proficient with it.
- Use the **higher of your Strength or Dexterity** modifier for its attack and damage rolls.
- It doesn't use Weapon Mastery, and weapon enchantments don't apply to it. Magic items that improve Unarmed Strikes (wraps, bracers) do.
- A Monk's Martial Arts die replaces the listed die if the Monk's die is higher.

### The Transformation

The System dissolves you, reorganizes you and reissues you, all in a few lines of commentary, and the GM adds one concrete detail about the new body (*"your voice drops half an octave," "your left hand has opinions"*). If you opted out of body-transformation humor, you get the short version: two lines, no jokes about your body. Fade to black on request, as always.

::: system
Dissolving. Please keep all limbs inside the vat until the vat says otherwise. You will feel warm, then wrong, then warm again. Done. Please check your teeth. Check them again. There are more of them now.

### Large Crawlers

One oddity, the **American (Sasquatch)**, is Large all the time, and a Goliath's Large Form uses these rules while it lasts. The core rules are the SRD's; the lines marked {{HOUSE}} are ours.

- **Space.** You take up a **10-by-10-foot** space. You can move through an ally's space, an Incapacitated creature's space, and the space of a Tiny creature.
- **Squeezing.** {{HOUSE}} You can move through a gap at least **5 feet** wide. While you're in a space narrower than 10 feet, each foot of movement costs **1 extra foot**, you have **Disadvantage on attack rolls and Dexterity saving throws**, and attack rolls against you have **Advantage**. You can't fit through a gap narrower than 5 feet. Most Crawl corridors are 10 feet wide. Many doors, vents and stairwells are not.
- **Saferoom doors.** {{HOUSE}} An honest saferoom's door, and your Personal Space door, always fit every Crawler in the party; the System widens them with a sigh. Inside a saferoom you never squeeze. The furniture is another matter.
- **Carrying.** You carry Strength × 30 lb and can drag, lift or push Strength × 60 lb.
- **Food.** You need 4 lb of food a day. {{HOUSE}} A saferoom meal counts as a full day's food for you; the System upsizes it at no charge.
- **Grapple and Shove.** You can Grapple or Shove a creature up to **Huge**.
- **Mounts.** A mount must be Huge or bigger. In practice, you walk.
- **Gear.** {{HOUSE}} After the rebuild, armor and clothing you find or buy must be tailored at a saferoom counter: free, 1 hour, and it can happen during a rest. Magic armor and clothing resize themselves. **Weapons need no change**: you use normal weapons for normal damage, and you have no penalty with Heavy weapons.
- **Hiding.** You need cover big enough to hide behind, which the GM decides. There's no other size penalty to Stealth.
- **Spells and effects** that care about size (*Enlarge/Reduce*, nets, grapples, some traps) treat you as Large.

## The Oddities

An **oddity** is a species you can only get from the Identity Market (or by joining after Floor 3). There are seventeen. Most are shaped by species from the source series, which we mark {{INSPIRED}}; one, the Raptorkin, is our own invention {{HOUSE}}. **Every number in every entry is ours.** None of the oddities is strictly better than the species you started with. Each one trades something.

### Reading an Oddity

Every entry uses the same layout: the kiosk name, a line of flavor, a stat line (size, speed, senses and any **gate** you must meet to be offered it), its **traits**, what it gains **at levels 3 and 5**, its **drawback**, and a word from the System. A few common rules apply to all of them:

- **Creature type:** Humanoid.
- **Species DC** = 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + the ability modifier the trait names.
- **"Once per Long Rest," "PB times per Long Rest"** and similar limits refresh as they say. PB means your Proficiency Bonus.
- **Level means character level.** Crawlers reach Floor 3 at level 3, so level 3 traits are active the moment you take the oddity.
- **Natural weapons** follow the rules above (higher of Strength or Dexterity).
- **Variants are cosmetic.** Where an oddity lists forms, breeds or head counts, they change your look and your kiosk name, never your rules.

**Level 3: Primal Rebound.** Once per Long Rest, when you fail a Strength, Dexterity or Constitution saving throw, you can reroll it, and you must use the new roll.

**Level 5: Blank Slate.** Choose one skill you're proficient in. You gain **Expertise** in it. That skill also counts as your species' own skill family: System skills tied to it can train to **rank 20** instead of 15 (Chapter 5).

**Drawback: Plain Wrapper.** No signature power, no flashy body. The chyron is unimpressed and sponsors look at someone else first. There's no mechanical drawback, which is why the traits are modest.

**Tanzanian (Primal) or Canadian (Human)?** Canadian (Human) is feats and luck. Tanzanian (Primal) is body and instinct: senses, speed, saves and recovery. A Canadian (Human) who becomes Tanzanian (Primal) loses Resourceful, Skillful and Versatile, as with any oddity.

> **System:** "Primal. You paid nothing, changed nothing, and somehow got an upgrade. Viewers are filing complaints. We are framing them."

## Replacement Crawlers

A Crawler who joins **after the Floor 3 Offer** never sees the Identity Market. Instead, they pick **any Offer option at creation**: {{HOUSE}}

- **Species:** any of the nine starting species, **Human with Unbroken Original** (including its extra Origin feat), or **any oddity** in this chapter. Gates still apply, and the GM may waive them as usual.
- **Background:** as normal, from Chapter 2.
- **Class:** keep the plain class, or take either class option for that class (the locked list above).

No Offer runs for them later; they made their choice at the kiosk. The same applies to a **benched Crawler who missed the Floor 3 Offer**: they pick any Offer option the first time they become active after Floor 3. A replacement still arrives at the party's lowest level with the usual late-entry gear and Silver loot box (Chapter 1). A Crawler who is **revived** keeps their species; revival is not a rebuild.

Welcome, late arrival. Everyone else had to earn their weird. You get to order yours off the menu. The others will be bitter about it, and the System will be broadcasting that.
:::
