Rosetta Thagra is an alien, a former Crawler, and an anarchist, best known as the author of the ninth edition of the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook. Her marginal notes, addressed throughout to "comrades," lean heavily toward demolitions and practical bomb-craft.[3|13]

==Description==
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=By Zev's account, Rosetta crawled a great deal like Carl does, with a real enthusiasm for blowing things up, which is why her edition of the cookbook carries so many explosives recipes. Her notes grow steadily more emotional across the editions, culminating in furious, repeated vows that the galaxy will one day answer for how it treats crawlers.[6|12][6|13]
Behind the cookbook byline is a nervous, meek-seeming woman who hosts the interview program Shadow Boxer. She is not a natural interviewer and warms up only once a conversation begins to flow on its own, but underneath the timidity is a passionate anarchist consumed by the injustice done to crawlers. She is habitually anxious and habitually aware of the nearest way out.[6|12][6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=In person she is far more commanding than her television persona; Donut remarks that she has gone from meek to blunt and self-assured. She runs on a short fuse and a deep, weary rage, addresses her allies as "comrade," and reveals a razor-sharp tactical mind beneath the exhaustion. She smiles rarely, and rarely wastes courtesy on those she blames for the crawl.[7|11][7|16][7|73][7|74]
Her fatalism is bone-deep. She holds that everyone who enters the Dungeon dies in the dungeon, and treats her own survival as beside the point next to the message she believes the Princess Posse has already sent. What keeps her fighting is not hope of victory but loyalty to her comrades, chief among them Justice Light and Tipid, whom she guards with fierce, exasperated protectiveness.[7|32][7|78][7|Rosetta]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Her manner stays brisk and unsentimental, but she is visibly grieved by Tipid's memory loss and by Justice Light's disappearance.[8|26][8|34][8|69]}}

==Appearance==
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Rosetta is a Crest: pale, pretty, and outwardly appearing to be in her early twenties, though her voice carries hints of a much greater, rejuvenation-masked age. As a Crest she has no eyebrows, and her otherwise bald head sports a strange, movable blob of hair that she can shift from side to side and recolor from faded blond to red, resembling anything from a toupee to a hat. A long, flowery tattoo circles up her right leg. She favors professional pantsuits and short dresses, and gives away her nerves by rubbing that thigh with her right hand.[6|12]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=By the Ninth Floor she has traded her interview wardrobe for a fighter's kit: black shorts, boots, and a plain unenchanted leather top, with nothing on her bald head. The floral tattoo on her right leg now glows, and its tendrils reach up her stomach and chest and onto her left arm. Her pale, almost sickly skin stands in sharp contrast to the leathery, sun-worn look of her fellow Crest, Tipid. The habit of absently rubbing that tattooed leg follows her from the studio to the battlefield.[7|11][7|Rosetta]}}

==History==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Before her crawl, Rosetta was a fledgling journalist, one of the most dangerous and subversive professions in her country, and she grew up in a world where kindness was read as weakness. She entered the Dungeon alongside her mother, who was killed by goblins on the very first day; her mother's last words were a plea to avenge them.[7|Rosetta]
She reached level 91 before taking a deal at the start of her own Eleventh Floor. Her indentureship put her to work as a Game Guide and as a Desperado Club merchant renting out mercenaries, occasionally handling distribution of the dungeon newsletter, a stretch of cycles that forced her to serve the same Faction Wars combatants who had slaughtered her comrades, including a then-Prince Rust who did not recognize her.[7|Rosetta]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Only recently released from that contract, Rosetta's first act upon leaving was to make a documentary about her home world, The Other Side of the Glass. It was well received and entertaining, though the Syndicate audience largely missed its point. On the strength of that success, the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network funded her "raw" weekly program Shadow Boxer, which tells Earth's story through interviews staged to look like casual conversation and which has been growing in popularity.[6|12]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=It was Porthus who gave her the money and backing for that documentary, and who drew her into the Crawler Project and the Pacifist Network. When the authorities began accusing the organization of terrorism, its people scattered; Rosetta eventually returned to the dungeon aboard the Homecoming Queen to fight for the Princess Posse, noting that once inside the game there was nothing anyone could do but wait to arrest her on the way out.[7|11][7|Rosetta]}}

==Story==
===Book 3===
Carl first encounters Rosetta not in person but in text, reading her ninth-edition notes while studying the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook on the Fourth Floor. Her entry on keeping explosives stable inside an equipped backpack gives him the idea for the improvised rig he builds so that Katia can carry more than a ton of extra mass for her Doppelgänger race.[3|13]

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=During the first phase of the Eighth Floor, Zev arranges for Carl and Donut to appear on Shadow Boxer, with the liaison Harbinger present to watch for foul play. Rosetta is visibly nervous and the interview is stilted until Donut warms to her. Ostensibly she is interviewing Donut about Earth television; in fact every screencap she shows is set in Miami, steering the Royal Court toward that location for the floor's second phase.[6|12]
She then turns to Carl and, indelicately and over his objections, walks him through his own childhood. That line of questioning is cover for a second hidden message, a staged photograph and an exchange about milk, potatoes, and root vegetables that links Milk's cookbook entries to the toraline. Harbinger recognizes the coded exchange, cuts the interview short, and bans Rosetta from all future crawler interviews.[6|13]
Her network later routes Carl an emergency Benefactor Box containing a flyer for a Christmas breakfast at the Shepherd's House in Homestead, Florida. The flyer is bait for something far larger: the Pacifist Network has staked everything on putting Carl within earshot of a Residual agent of the Apothecary. The gambit fails. The box is seized, the entire charity vanishes, and Rosetta and her colleagues are left fugitives with their assets frozen and Carl's sponsorship spot revoked.[6|49][6|51][6|Epilogue]
She is not gone at all. In the season's final hours, Rosetta is revealed alive aboard the Homecoming Queen in Earth orbit, a converted garbage freighter, working alongside fellow Crest Tipid under the plan of Porthus. Hidden inside the ship are fifty thousand former crawlers who intend to enter the dungeon as troopers for the Princess Posse; Rosetta and Tipid also arranged the Celestial box sent to Donut.[6|Epilogue]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Arrival
On the Ninth Floor, during Faction Wars, Rosetta and Tipid step out of the Meadowlark saferoom in person as Crest Barn Burners, the vanguard of roughly fifty thousand former crawlers who chose to re-enter the dungeon to fight their old captors. Rosetta arrives as a Colonel, and greets Carl with a comradely smack to the forehead before pulling him into a hug.[7|11]
She takes charge of the crawlers' most delicate problems, collecting Carl's remaining toraline and the tattoo ink of Shi Maria to brew skill-sharing tattoos, a humane alternative to the crawler-farming Ring of Divine Suffering and Necklace of Indelible Woe, and overseeing the Glory Bound treatment that frees Carl of the spider's presence and clears Katia's addiction.[7|11][7|16][7|32]
King Rust
Rosetta has her own score to settle. Her comrade Brooks was casually killed by a then-Prince Rust at the end of her own Faction Wars, lassoed through the portal glass and cut in half while the orcs laughed. When King Rust unexpectedly enters the Earth crawl to sue for peace, Rosetta bursts up out of a dwarven tunnel and beheads him, then uses a datapad and a looted credit chit to drain his accounts, a heist brokered with adjutant Victory to bankroll the Pacifist Network's escape plan. She sobs with relief once the transfer clears.[7|32][7|35][7|Volteeg][7|Rosetta]
The price of that bank access is ugly. Before the Posse ever boarded, Rosetta asked the tenodera York point-blank whether he could destroy an orc yacht carrying children, including the new heir to the Skull Empire. She feels ill about it afterward, but she does not call it off.[7|Rosetta][7|Epilogue]
Strategist
With Tipid she reconstructs the Blood Sultanate's line of succession and cracks the naga sultana's decades-old vanishing trick, tracing it to the Temple of Khepri inside Club Vanquisher and building Operation: Snake Pit around it.[7|46][7|48][7|49] For the climactic Operation: Ruin she serves as designated Theater Commander, coordinating all four simultaneous offensives.[7|59][7|65] When the war mages flood Larracos with generated zarogoths, she recognizes the mob generator from her own crawl and talks Donut through hunting the soul-crystal antenna feeding it.[7|69]
In the closing hours she hauls Edgar up the Nest tower strapped to her back, then shaves a patch on Donut's flank with a pink disposable razor and talks the reluctant cat through accepting a tattoo, pairing a borrowed Bijanbi spell with War Crime to strip the enemy out of their rolling battle formation and drop them into a ring of fire.[7|73][7|74][7|75]
Aftermath
It falls to Rosetta to explain that Tipid's mind, patched together with Viceroy repairs that the blast destroyed, has reverted to the last day of his indentureship; she sits with him and begins telling him the story of the bravest Crest she has ever met.[7|82]
She assists in the improvised surgery that cuts Epitome Tagg out of Louis's chest, and she is the one who tells the team where the stairwells will generate once Faction Wars is won. She stays behind with Carl and Donut through the final minutes of the floor.[7|84][7|86]
She also directs the pre-recorded broadcast in which Porthus announces a new militant organization and issues an ultimatum to the Syndicate, timed to air the moment the crawlers claim victory.[7|Epilogue]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Rosetta continues on to the Tenth Floor with the crawlers, one of the handful of former crawlers who stay, and becomes the guild's fixer and reference desk. She fields questions on dungeon lore, runs the shop interface, scouts the town while the others race, buys claw caps to blunt Donut's newly celestial claws, tracks down a fleshmancer, hires undead drivers and the three-crawler mercenary squad Helix, and warily manages Mordecai through the glitches of his new shadow mimic form.[8|7][8|8][8|9][8|26][8|34]
She designs the indicator that lets the team track Li Na after she is smuggled out of the dungeon inside a cleaner bot, having Li Na cast a dread trap on a potted cactus whose glow, blooms, or collapse into dust will report her fate.[8|61]
When Carl finally asks about the Scavenger storyline, Rosetta lays out the myth of Nekhebit, the old gods, the all-tree, and the contested identity of the Scavenger's Daughter, noting dryly that religions are usually reverse-engineered from phenomena nobody understood. She also helps vet who can safely attempt the Pineapple Cabaret escape, ruling out fairies, flying classes, and anyone whose body depends on dungeon magic.[8|69][8|79]}}

==Progression==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=As a crawler, Rosetta reached a top level of 91 as a Gazetteer, keeping her birth race, Crest, and took an exit deal at the beginning of her own Eleventh Floor.[7|Rosetta]
Re-entering the dungeon with the Princess Posse under the standard mercenary package, she is reset to level 60 as a Barn Burner, carrying only basic, unenchanted gear while retaining the experience and instincts of a veteran who has already survived Faction Wars.[7|11][7|Rosetta]
Like other former crawlers serving as mercenaries, she is subject to the blood bar and must periodically leave the guild to kill something in order to refill it.[7|32]}}

==Cookbook Entries==
The notes Rosetta left in the ninth edition of the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook.

===On Explosives===

Comrades, you know the high-level madness satchels, the bronze tier ones with the big yield? They are not as useless as they look. Because they randomly fall into rapid de-stability and explode, they are most dangerous unless they are in your inventory, of course. But with the 15-second waiting period after removing it, it makes them almost impossible to use because they can (and do) explode during those fifteen seconds. I have discovered a solution. If you can find or build an equippable backpack, they will not lose stability as long as they are in the backpack and the backpack is equipped. This also works for other party members if you wish to give them explosives to utilize. Use with caution.[3|13]

===On Mercenaries===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=
Never trust mercenaries.[5|33]}}

===On Fairies and Sprites===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=th Edition>
As fragile as they look, fairies and sprites can be difficult to kill. They’re fast. They’re magical. They’re always on alert. They say luck is not a real stat in this place, but I think it is. The luck of these little monsters... it’s unnatural. Something crazy always happens when I try to fight one. Be wary, comrades.
But if you must fight them, pull their wings off. They come off easily. That’s where they store their magic.[6|31]}}

===On Stairwells and Faction Wars===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=th Edition>
The only way for a crawler to move from one floor to the next is via a stairwell. That’s it. It is an unbreakable rule. There is no skipping floors. At the end of Faction Wars, after the viceroys pulled out their last minute victory, a portal appeared, and several of the remaining fucks stepped within. It was all of them. The orcs, the viceroys, the operatics, even the nagas, all together, laughing as if they weren’t just bathing in each other’s entrails the day before. It appeared it was taking them to another part of the dungeon. Some sort of nightclub on a different floor for a victory party. They dragged several unwilling NPCs with them, including the librarian elves from the college, whose help was instrumental in our survival. A crawler tried to jump through the portal to save the NPCs, and he was stopped like it was a wall of glass. He tried casting a spell through the portal, but it did not work, though the attempt did catch the attention of the orc prince. The orc stepped back through the glass into the smoldering remains of Larracos, lassoed the crawler, and returned to the portal. The rope pulled itself within, and it cut the crawler in half. We watched as the orc and his friends laughed, just there on the other side of the glass. It was the last crawler they killed, of the thousands who fell during that pointless game.
Tonight, I will dream of watching them burn. Tonight, I will pretend I was smart enough to find a way to lance them from afar.
Comrades, I hope you help me realize my dream one day.[6|61]}}

==Trivia==
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Rosetta's crawl ran roughly two seasons before Mordecai's, under a System AI she describes as a passionless one.[6|12]During her indentureship she was fond of the program Little Settler Miss.[6|12]She is a Crest, a hairless race that turned up as a common hunter type on the Seventh Floor.[6|12]Nobody, not even Carl, can tell whether the blob on her head is real hair, a wig, or a hat.[6|12]Her cookbook comrade Milk owned the sixth edition, and the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network is believed to employ several former cookbook owners.[6|13]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Her fellow cookbook author Justice Light affectionately calls her "Rosie."[7|59]Her original crawler class, Gazetteer, fits her later life as a journalist and mapmaker far better than the demolition-flavored Barn Burner class she draws as a Princess Posse mercenary.[7|Rosetta]She loathes the ninth floor's teleport ban chiefly because of how much running it forces on her, and proposes that the next tower the Posse builds include an elevator.[7|73]Carl at one point recalls her as the author of the cookbook's seventh edition, though every other reference, including her own character card, names her the ninth-edition author; the seventh edition actually belongs to the gargoyle crawler Volteeg.[7|11][7|Volteeg][7|Rosetta]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Rosetta buys Donut red claw caps on the theory that a translucent red will mute the blue glow, while admitting she does not know whether felines perceive the same color spectrum as a Crest.[8|9]Her own culture told of an ocean goddess with sapient menstrual blood, which she notes was really a red algae bloom that had evolved to move onto land.[8|69]She is the one who realizes that a guild's secondary add-on rooms, such as the pet stables, are a blind spot where the gods cannot overhear what is said.[8|69]}}

==Speculation==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Rosetta professes a bleak fatalism, insisting no one who enters the dungeon leaves it alive and that her mission is complete once the stolen funds reach Porthus. Yet she keeps choosing her comrades over her own exhaustion, leaving open whether loyalty or revenge is the stronger engine.[7|Rosetta]
She also voices deep distrust of the Apothecary, suspecting the collective's motives are unknowable and that Porthus is being played. Nothing in the text yet confirms or refutes her suspicion, though it sits oddly against the Shepherd's House operation, in which her own network staked everything on delivering Carl to one of the Apothecary's agents. Whether Rosetta knew at the time what that speech was meant to accomplish is never stated.[6|51][7|Rosetta]}}
