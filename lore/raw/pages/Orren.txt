{{Quote|Not quite a sheriff. Not quite an attorney. Not quite a judge.|Orren[4|19]}}
Orren is a Syndicate Liaison, an independent fact finder called in when the showrunners and the System AI cannot agree on what happened.[4|19]

==Description==
Orren is a parasitic worm and a citizen of the Valtay.[4|19] He describes himself as an independent consultant retained by the Syndicate rather than an employee of Borant, working in concert with the showrunners, the Syndicate government, and the controlling System AI.[4|19] When the kua-tin and the AI file contradictory or inconclusive reports on an incident, a liaison investigates and recommends a resolution; crawlers would not normally meet one.[4|19]

He is dry, unhurried, and hard to rattle, speaking with the authority of a British professor and grunting instead of laughing.[4|19] Carl describes him as a vice principal.[4|19] He is not a company man, and agrees without hesitation when Carl blames the season's admin death toll on Borant running the crawl as cheaply as possible.[4|19]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Orren's race is the gondii.[5|1] Liaisons are selected for a genuine passion for the game's success and, unadvertised, for a fundamental disagreement with their own nation's goals.[5|59] Orren credits his seniority and his vocal criticism of his own people for keeping his post after the Valtay took control of Borant.[5|59] He likes Carl and Donut and is entertained by their habit of breaking things never meant to be broken, while still considering them dangerous.[5|59]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=A liaison's second and more vital duty is negotiating with the System AI should it take control of the show.[6|48]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Orren states that his first priority is safety.[7|37] By the ninth floor his role has shifted from investigating Carl's actions to mediating between the showrunners and the AI.[7|36][7|38]}}

==Appearance==
Orren's body is a worm living in a bowl of fluid mounted atop a tall humanoid frame, the bowl supernaturally obscured by a dark hood; Carl describes him as grim reaper-like.[4|19] His hands, normally the only flesh visible, are a purple dark enough to read as black, with elongated fingers that do not match his height.[4|19] He is heavier than he looks, his chair groaning whenever he shifts, and his voice carries a slight distortion.[4|19] When the hood slips, the darkness resolves into a face-shaped bowl of liquid engineered to look like shadow, the worm inside visible only as a glimpse of light.[4|19]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The glint under the hood is glass.[5|1] He wears gloves over his hands.[5|59]}}

==History==
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Unlike most of his people, Orren does not ride a host body. Quasar treats this as a warning sign, holding that a Valtay who is not wearing someone usually has something wrong with them.[5|3]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Inside the dungeon his posting is disguised. His cover is manager of the Desperado Club, working alongside co-manager Astrid.[6|48] His office sits in the employees-only hallway at the back of the club's top floor.[6|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Orren comes from a place he calls the Knot. He left saying he wanted to make a difference; in truth he wanted to be an actor, and wanted to remain pure, unwilling to rely on someone else's death to make his mark.[7|39] He expects history to remember him as the one who let the apocalypse happen.[7|39]}}

==Story==
===Book 4===
Orren's first meeting with Carl is an investigation into the death of Loita, for which he has Carl and Donut teleported into his office at the Desperado Club rather than to their personal space.[4|19] He reconstructs Carl's actions largely accurately, calls it one of the most brilliant assassinations he has seen a crawler execute, and offers first a blank confession and then a Legendary box in exchange for the identity of Carl's outside source; Carl refuses both.[4|19]

Unable to establish consensus, he recommends that the Syndicate close the matter, but bars third-party teleportation of crawlers for the rest of the floor as a penalty against Borant.[4|19] He warns Carl that whoever is helping him is not doing it for his benefit, and admits he would have had Carl removed had the decision been his alone.[4|19] The meeting consumed five days of crawl time.[4|19]

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=At the start of the sixth floor Orren summons Carl over the Gate of the Feral Gods and offers three options: a floor-wide protection spell stripping all portal access, acceleration, or surrender of the winding box against a receipt redeemable on the ninth floor.[5|1] He also tries to trick Carl into touching the gate pieces while they are under administrative lock, which would have deleted the gate.[5|3]
When Carl retains Quasar, Orren refuses to leave his own office and sits through the negotiation with his auditory receptors switched off.[5|3] He objects only to an emergency secondary program license, conceding it with a twenty percent surcharge payable to Borant.[5|13]
Near the end of the floor he has Astrid bring Carl and Donut to him a final time, announces that he is leaving at the floor's collapse and will not return until the ninth, and warns that liaisons are like sheriffs in the old west, some good and some bad.[5|59] He also circumvents the freeze on crawl litigation to let Quasar speak with Carl directly, on the condition that he remain in the room.[5|59]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Orren's office sits empty for most of the eighth floor,[6|22] which makes the passage behind it useful to Carl.[6|32]
When the System AI seizes control of the crawl from Borant, Orren appears alongside Cascadia in a special announcement as the designated negotiator, explains to the crawlers how the system AIs are built and how Borant lost control, and reports a settlement returning most of the seized controls to Borant in exchange for concessions. The disclosure was itself one of the AI's demands.[6|48]
He later pulls Carl out of the memory of Paulie, a Residual, and moves to seize the bin Carl looted there, saying he likely saved Carl's life by acting before one of his colleagues could and that the Open Intellect Pacifist Network engineered the encounter as a trap.[6|51] Carl gives the bin up in trade instead, receiving a wand recharge scroll, three Cracker Jack scrolls, and the use of the four cretin mercenaries through the ninth floor.[6|51] Orren refuses to release the Gate of the Feral Gods early, since it would let Carl skip the floor's final phase.[6|51]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Carl is pulled into the Desperado Club immediately after the death of King Rust, though Orren says the heist is not why he is there; what he wants is the reasoning behind Carl's certainty that pulling the Fail Safe would not work.[7|36] He reveals that the liaisons traced Carl back to the hidden conversation with Paulie, that they know Carl carries an illegal non-Valtay implant but not what it does, and that Harbinger already ordered him killed only for the AI to intervene.[7|36] He then hangs up on the prime minister, mutes the council, and triggers a custom-engineered ball that generates a zero zone around the office for roughly ten unmonitored minutes, observing that he has probably lost his job.[7|36]
An avatar of the system AI wearing the body of Growler Gary cuts the interrogation short, and Orren deduces from the demonstration that the failsafe is already disabled.[7|37] He asks whether the crawl's other agreements still stand and how the liaisons can be of further assistance.[7|38] Afterward he asks Carl whether he helped break the AI out of containment, and whether he did it deliberately; Carl does not answer.[7|39] He asks them not to repeat the conversation, warning that mass panic would hurt the weak and the innocent first.[7|39]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=During Carl and Donut's judgment before the leadership of the Plenty, Orren messages in alongside Prime Minister Victory and asks Carl to put a carefully worded question to the caprids about the integrity of the tunneling system.[8|28] He explains that many unsponsored gods have escaped the dungeon's controls, that the AI cannot rein them in either, and that the checks built to stop an insane AI from weaponizing deities are broken.[8|28] Carl asks his own question instead, live on air.[8|28] Orren concedes afterward that the liaisons knew the outcome was possible and proceeded anyway, having no other choice.[8|29]
By the eleventh floor Orren is blocked from messaging crawlers directly and routes his questions through Zev.[8|88] He remains locked out after the crawlers descend to the twelfth floor; the AI tells him the restriction is for his own safety, because he is being hunted and because only the AI is permitted to kill him.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Prime Minister Victory]}}

==Speculation==
When Donut is attacked on the Desperado Club dance floor during the Fourth Floor, a tall, shadowy figure emerges from the unmarked door at the back of the room and disappears into the club's offices.[3|9] The figure is never identified, but the description matches Orren, whose office lies at the back of the club.[4|19]

Orren's hands are described as flesh, yet no host body is ever confirmed beneath the cloak. His height, the creaking of his chair, and the distortion in his voice would all suit a constructed body carrying the containment helmet, though the books never say.[4|19]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Orren's account of leaving the Knot, in which he says he refused to rely on someone else's death to make his mark, suggests his lack of a host body may be an ethical choice rather than the defect Quasar takes it for.[7|39]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The System AI's stated reason for locking Orren out could indicate protection, a claim of ownership, or both. Whoever is hunting him is never identified.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Prime Minister Victory]}}
