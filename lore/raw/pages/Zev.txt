Zev is a Kua-Tin and low-ranked employee in the Borant Corporation Communications Department. After Carl and Donut's first appearance on Dungeon Crawler After Hours with Odette, she was appointed as the Royal Court of Princess Donut's "Outreach Associate."[1|36]

== AI Description ==
Zev - Borant Corporation Assistant Communications Representative.This is a Dungeon Admin.[1|36]

== Description ==
Zev works in the Borant Corporation Communications Department as a low-ranked liaison between her company and the privately-produced shows that want to feature crawlers.[1|36] She monitors her clients, coordinates their appearances, hosts focus groups to gather viewer opinions, and has the authority to sign official documents on their behalf.[1|37][2|11] As a Kua-Tin outside the Borant System's controlling political party, the Bloom, she struggles to advance her career and answers to a Bloom-member boss; by her own admission, a single wrong word from her could see her whole family wiped out. She casts herself as genuinely on her crawlers' side, since the better they do, the better she does.[1|36]

Donut and Zev bond over their shared love of scripted Earth television and chat constantly,[1|36][1|39] planning an intergalactic version of Gossip Girl to write together once Donut leaves the Dungeon.[2|23]

== Appearance ==
Zev enters the Dungeon with a pop and a small splash of cold water.[1|36][2|16] For her first three floors, where staff receive no protections, she kits herself out in full-body armor: a round glass helmet of bubbling water piped from a bulky backpack, worn over a white mesh "spacesuit."[1|36][2|15] Even armored, she wears a badge indicating that she is not a member of the Bloom.[1|36] It is her first-ever foray into a dungeon and she over-prepares out of caution; Mordecai remarks that most kua-tin make do with a rebreather worn around the neck rather than a deep-diving water suit he hasn't seen in centuries.[1|36]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Although admins are supposed to receive protections starting on the Fourth Floor, Zev keeps wearing the suit because Hinter, the company that normally rents out the integration equipment, refused to work with Borant at the last minute.[3|25]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The first time Carl sees her out of the suit, Zev has traded the bulky helmet for a simple rebreather worn around her throat, revealing a surprisingly thin frame.[5|17]}}

== History ==
Zev spent fifteen years in Earth's orbit with nothing to watch but Earth programs, becoming particularly fond of Gossip Girl and Riverdale.[1|36] She once tried writing a Gossip Girl fan fiction, but it didn't get many hits.[1|44]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Zev belongs to a younger generation of kua-tin that has soured on the Bloom's doctrine of "system strength," "True Unity," and the "Great Consensus." The Bloom was especially invested in "converting" her because of her age and skills, but she refused to join until the party committed unspeakable acts against her mother and her aunts. Only then did she take the party badge.[4|18]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=By Zev's own account, her family was slaughtered after she refused to wear a Bloom pin. With the kua-tin home system gone dark, she and the others who stayed on after "the change" do not know whether they will be welcomed home or executed as traitors when the crawl ends.[5|32]}}

== Story ==
=== Book 1 ===
Zev is assigned to Carl and Donut as their PR agent through the Crawler Assisted Outreach Program after the team's early popularity triggers a flood of interview requests.[1|36] She throws herself into managing their image, relaying focus-group reactions and coaching Carl's on-camera delivery.[1|37]

She then breaks the rules to warn Carl over chat to run for a Safe Room moments before the Rage Elemental manifests,[1|37] drops off chat immediately afterward, and is suspended and censured by the System AI for "cheating."[1|39] During the suspension a callous stand-in admin, Mukta, overrides her booking and sends the team onto the abusive Death Watch Extreme Dungeon Mayhem.[1|41] Her representative wins the appeal on a technicality; Zev clears her record, apologizes for the Death Watch booking (Borant is not permitted to turn down Syndicate-member production companies), and cautions that once the AI takes notice of someone, it rarely lets up.[1|44] She later badgers Carl to trade his "boring" slingshot for dynamite, advice he flatly ignores.[1|47]

=== Book 2 ===
Zev makes contact soon after the team reaches the Third Floor, reporting strong reception, steering them away from the boring local crawlers nearby, and noting that she has taken on extra staff just to handle the flood of interview requests.[2|4] When Carl asks her to intervene with the producers of Vengeance of the Daughter, she insists she can't interfere, but carries the request to them anyway and runs it past the Syndicate AI referee, which forbids any help whatsoever.[2|9] She relays Carl's verbatim pitch under protest, then signs the resulting deal on the team's behalf.[2|11] Barred from revealing anything about the wider universe the team doesn't already know, she still feeds them everything the rules allow, explaining that no Kua-Tin outfit may produce serials during the game and booking the team onto Danger Zone with Ripper Wonton.[2|12] When that appearance is cut short by a pulse attack she checks frantically that they are unharmed,[2|19] then reunites with them in the Safe Room to explain that the assassins knew which production trailer to target because rental companies publish their booking schedules openly.[2|20]

=== Book 3 ===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=On the Fourth Floor, Zev raises the problem of Katia Grim: audiences find Katia boring, and fewer viewers mean fewer fan-box and sponsor prizes. She suggests the team either ditch Katia or make her more marketable, and notes she still isn't permitted to message Katia directly.[3|6]
She books the team onto the narration-based culture program Planet Beautiful,[3|13] nags them toward a Safe Room and threatens to teleport them away mid-task,[3|18] grants the six-hour delay Carl talks her into,[3|19] then pushes the taping back further still.[3|20] During the Vermillion Line Rescue she cuts into a private conversation to remind Carl that half his exchanges are invisible to viewers and that he needs to start having these moments out loud.[3|21]
With three days left on the floor, Zev teleports into the Royal Court's space in person to congratulate the team on their standings and break bad news: a rival representative, Loita, is angling to take over the account, her claim backed by the Bloom status Zev lacks. Zev also warns that the team's private chats will be made public the next day, alludes to something having happened back home, and tells them that, in case something happens to her, she appreciates how hard they have worked.[3|25]
Within thirty-six hours, Zev is sent to a "temporary reeducation retreat" following a "personal tragedy." Loita becomes the team's primary PR agent and says Zev will return only as their social media manager, implying the camps don't take long for the "weak-minded."[3|Epilogue]}}

=== Book 4 ===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=As social media manager, Zev can comment on the team's numbers and reception but no longer controls their appearances, and she can't be messaged directly unless she opens the chat herself. Her replies to Donut are wooden and canned, signed off with a rote "kill, kill, kill."[4|12]
Her obedience starts to slip when the crawlers complain about the Veriluxx RealPet Companions: Zev quietly agrees that the toy is generating memes and that its voice doesn't sound like Donut, then, through a roundabout Gossip Girl reference, signals to Donut that her own out-of-character behavior is an act.[4|15]
When Donut's purchase of the Social Media Board accidentally makes her redundant, Zev says goodbye to the crawlers, noting she will stay on as Loita's behind-the-scenes assistant.[4|17] After Carl engineers Loita's death,[4|18] Zev is reassigned as the Royal Court's temporary PR agent until things get sorted out.[4|19] She still speaks in a flat, robotic "Stepford Wife" voice, but Donut greets her warmly and no longer seems worried, a sign the two are quietly communicating.[4|24]
Zev quietly coaches Carl on how to word the conversation in which he first lays out his Larracos plan.[4|28] When he prepares to spring it, she relays a failed last-minute legal challenge and a message from the Borant politburo: they approve, but future game-breaking antics affecting sponsors will not be tolerated. Once the Gate of the Feral Gods floods Larracos, Zev drops her "good little citizen of the Party" persona entirely, giddy and barely able to contain it as she buries a stream of valuable strategic information inside a flurry of seemingly outraged messages.[4|34]}}

=== Book 5 ===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Having resumed handling the team as their PR agent, Zev makes her first contact of the sixth floor soon after Carl and Donut are ferried out of Zockau, urging them to stick close to Prepotente and refrain from insulting him and deflecting Donut's question about whether the arrangement is now permanent.[5|6] She and Donut keep communicating in secret through the Social Media Board; when Donut suffers a panic attack mid-conversation, Zev's uncharacteristic chat interruption serves as a quiet signal for Carl to drop a sensitive subject before he says something dangerous on an open channel.[5|12]
Zev is physically present, out of her suit, when Odette reveals that Carl's ex-girlfriend Beatrice is alive. Having learned the news herself only moments earlier, she discloses that Odette is using Bea to blackmail Borant and warns that the fan box Carl just won will whisk him away alone to an event where a rival program planned to ambush him with the story. Uncharacteristically rattled, she urges him to set his own feelings aside because "Donut is going to need you."[5|17]
She appears to be central to the unfolding Borant System revolution: when the Valtay Corporation abruptly seizes 51% of Borant, Donut whispers "Viva la revolución," making clear the two had a hand in it,[5|19] and Donut later lets slip that she had smuggled a group of Valtay agents to Zev and the Borant Emancipation Front.[5|20]
Zev stops just short of warning Carl about Lucia Mar's ambush in Alucarda, cutting herself off mid-message before she says too much,[5|29] then arranges to keep him at CrawlCon between events rather than send him straight back into it.[5|30] Asked how she is holding up, she admits she is doing "about the best anyone can expect."[5|32] Promoted over the course of the floor, she now bosses the Popov Brothers' outreach associate and brings Carl together with them in person,[5|32][5|40] and presents his replacement sponsor, The Plenty, cautioning him not to insult the new benefactors.[5|41] After Carl stabs the naga reporter Nihit with a pen, Zev debriefs him on the resulting assault charge and explains that Nihit's grudge is part of a small but growing conspiracy that casts Carl and Donut as apocalyptic figures.[5|42]
In the lead-up to the Butcher's Masquerade, she warns Carl in no uncertain terms not to smuggle the elite Signet into the party, and is exasperated when Signet appears in the ballroom anyway.[5|64][5|65] When Carl presses her about the children he discovers inside Lucia Mar, she shuts the subject down, telling him he doesn't have to be involved in everything.[5|69] As Carl uses the Faction Wars sponsorship reveal to publicly campaign against the rival teams, Zev mutes him and warns that the feed is being cut, calling him crazy; Carl reminds her she still has a job thanks to the risks that were taken.[5|75]}}

=== Book 6 ===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Early on the eighth floor, Zev books Carl and Donut onto Shadow Boxer, a documentary-style program about their lives before the crawl. She turns down a competing offer from The Plenty on the team's behalf and warns that, because the show is owned by their sponsor, the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network, a liaison will be required on set.[6|7] At the taping she is caught between the attorney Quasar (who nicknames her "Fish Sticks") and the liaison Harbinger, insisting she has no control over which liaison the office sends.[6|12] When Harbinger abruptly ends the interview, Zev rounds on him furiously, the angriest Carl has heard her, protesting that a liaison holds no such authority. Stretched thin since the Valtay takeover left her "doing the work of five," she flatly refuses to discuss how Harbinger still has a job after the Skull Empire bribed him.[6|13]
Zev keeps a constant watch on the team's broadcast feed. When Carl drops off it during a private sacrament inside Club Vanquisher, she frets over the apparent glitch, relieved once the backup playback resumes and dismayed to realize a cleric had just licked his foot.[6|18]
She opens a secured sponsor conversation to brief the team on the role of adjutant, a neutral, protected referee, advising against accepting Odette for fear that Mordecai would attack her on the battlefield and get himself permanently removed, jeopardizing the orphaned Changeling children he secretly fosters; in the same breath she reveals she knows about that fostering, a story she finds endearing.[6|21]
Zev calls the team back for their appearance on Odette's show, warning that Harbinger will again observe virtually,[6|30] and attends in person as a holo, having begged them not to choose Odette as their adjutant.[6|35] As the team transfers into Phase Two she relays a last-minute twist before being pulled away by an all-hands security alert,[6|37] then apologizes afterward for a transfer glitch that scrambled crawler destinations.[6|38] During the floor's memory-simulation gauntlet she confirms that the brutal deathbed scene Carl is forced to witness is a genuine recording of the real day, while refusing to explain how Borant obtained it.[6|45] When the System AI seizes control for the climactic boss battle, Zev and the rest of the production are locked out of their consoles entirely.[6|46][6|47]
When Carl slips away from the team's Christmas celebration to chase a tip, Zev quietly covers for him and urges caution, warning that his sponsor and the entire charity behind the box have abruptly vanished. She admits she has no idea what the Syndicate's "failsafe" actually does, only that the system is under quarantine, and she ties the strange events to the mysterious Residuals.[6|49][6|50]}}

=== Book 7 ===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=When Donut demands answers about the bootleg "Donut Holes" fan-club logo, Zev calmly informs her that the group paid its license fee and is a perfectly legitimate brand, then walks her through the economics: reaching the Tenth Floor earns a crawler a stipend and the right to negotiate a small cut of licensing revenue, with an automatic percentage kicking in only above a threshold so high nobody has ever come close.[7|7]
Zev passes along an urgent warning from the Syndicate Council, delivered personally by Prime Minister Glory, ordering Carl not to interact with the residual Agatha, though she admits she has no idea why; Residuals turn up every season and are normally harmless, so the sudden alarm baffles her. She notes she is not giving the same warning to Lucia Mar, who no longer answers messages, and recalls with a shudder how one of Lucia's dogs once devoured the kua-tin Mukta.[7|28]
Brought to the production facility for the Faction Wars warlord council, Zev pops into the room in her usual splash of water and reveals, sourly, an unexpected prior acquaintance with Ferdinand. She confirms the production expected Formidable to trigger the system failsafe, then breaks even more frightening news: the very nanosecond the orcs pressed the button, the Aryl system, site of the previous crawl, went dark and its tunnel node vanished, something she stresses has never happened in recorded history.[7|41]
During the chaotic battle for Larracos, Zev finally explains the truth Borant had been hiding about Lucia Mar: her presence began as a glitch the company chose to conceal rather than correct, and she has effectively become a living dead man's switch tied to roughly 120,000 of Earth's children, whom Borant now fears will die if Lucia dies. In the same conversation Zev confirms that the Eulogist is another name for the AI of the center system and, accused of nearly letting Carl kill Lucia, can only answer that the matter is out of her hands.[7|73]
After Glory's covert attempt to assassinate Carl is overridden by the System AI, Zev reports that the ejection request did not come from Borant, that warships in orbit have opened fire on one another, and that the Valtay showrunners have countermanded a Syndicate order to self-destruct the production headquarters. Unsure whether the mercenaries will side with the production or the Syndicate, she urges Carl to hurry and get his revelation out.[7|81]}}

=== Book 8 ===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=As the Tenth Floor opens, Zev dives for the loudspeaker to deliver the racing floor's introduction after Cascadia breaks down mid-announcement, relaying the System AI's instructions while awkwardly softening its profanity.[8|2] She stays in steady contact with Donut throughout the floor, early on fielding her complaints about bootleg merchandise.[8|11] With Cascadia incapacitated, Zev is later made to read the AI's scripted announcements aloud, editorializing and correcting the script as the AI rewrites it mid-sentence, all while it audibly tortures Cascadia in the background; she keeps her composure and reassures Donut that she is okay.[8|23]
With the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network's insurgents controlling orbit and most surviving Borant staff barely functioning, Zev ends up running nearly the entire production on her own, with Lexis loaned in from Odette's show to assist her,[8|27] and she handles the floor's running race-rule announcements between heats.[8|40][8|47] When Cascadia "accidentally" overdoses and an unknown goddess manifests inside the submerged headquarters, Zev is placed on lockdown while a surface satellite office takes over the production and announcements pass to Damien.[8|78]
By her own admission terrified but still doing her job, Zev relays a message from a comms-blocked Orren, cheers Donut's television-themed parade float ahead of the Eleventh Floor parade, and confirms that the mysterious mandatory broadcast is playing on every screen outside the center system, though she has no idea what it is; she also notes that Gilmore Girls and Gossip Girl have taken the galactic net by storm.[8|88][8|89]}}

== Progression ==
Zev is a non-combatant Dungeon Admin with no crawler levels, stats, or gear of note; Mordecai observes that she is about as low-level as one can be and still be an Admin.[1|36][1|41]

== Trivia ==
* When the AI first displayed Zev's information, it pointedly skipped its usual snarky flavor text, giving only her title and a flat note that she is a Dungeon Admin.[1|36]
* The dungeon's cameras cut out whenever a Kua-Tin is present, so viewers, and the crawlers' own interface, go dark during Zev's in-person visits, which is how Carl manages to speak candidly with Mordecai while she is in the room.[1|35][1|36] The blackout isn't foolproof, though: when Zev reached a Safe Room ahead of the team on the Third Floor, their HUD stayed on.[2|20]
* According to Matt and Jeff, Jeff's voice for Zev is a [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GiGJ-ySE1DY B-tier voice].

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Zev's boss is Cascadia, the notorious "kill, kill, kill" announcer, an older kua-tin with the long, droopy whiskers that Zev lacks.[6|21]
Zev serves as a casual source of wider-galaxy lore for the team; she had once mentioned that cat girls exist, to Donut's scandalized disbelief.[6|5]}}
