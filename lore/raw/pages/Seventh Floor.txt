{{Quote|We call it the Great Race. It is an engineering marvel.|System Announcement[5|56]}}

The Great Race is the Seventh Floor of the World Dungeon, a sealed circuit of glass tunnels that crawlers were meant to race through toward a stack of exit portals rather than a landscape they could explore. A last-minute, Valtay-funded replacement for the level originally planned for the slot, it was structurally compromised and shattered into fine dust by Prepotente minutes after it opened. No crawler ever entered the maze, and no mob, NPC, or boss was ever encountered.[5|Epilogue]

==Introductory Announcement==
Hello, Crawlers!
Welcome to the seventh floor! The countdown is on!
Rules. The moment this message ends, each crawler pod will slide to the starting chute and open. This will happen in order. Once opened, you will be pulled through the tunnel and into the maze. All paths lead to the exit. There are no dead ends. The exit portals you see behind you are physically pulling you in, but in order to get to them, you must first negotiate the maze paths, which constitute several hundred kilometers of twisting and turning tubes. Each path leads to a node which in turn leads to multiple branches. In some cases, there are literally hundreds of possible paths. Pitstop saferooms exist in these nodes. You cannot backtrack. Some paths are more dangerous than others. Some require the solving of math equations or puzzles to proceed. All tunnels are passable. The danger level of each branch is marked, oftentimes describing what you’ll find along the way and the distance to the next node. Most major nodes contain a zero tube, or a path with no danger. You must choose your path. Once a path has been passed through by 10 crawlers or a single party, it closes for an amount of time based on difficulty. All zero tubes may only be passed once. So those of you in the back might want...[5|Epilogue]

The announcement was cut off partway through its final rule when the level was destroyed.[5|Epilogue]

==Description==
The Seventh Floor was a late substitution. Midway through the previous floor, the showrunners announced that crawler resilience had prompted a complete redesign of the seventh.[5|37] The level originally intended for the slot was pushed back to become the Eighth Floor with its difficulty raised to match, and the Great Race was announced in its place, credited to the Valtay Corporation for the capital and expertise needed to finish on schedule.[5|56] Time to level collapse was set at twenty days.[5|Epilogue]

Both the level's name and its construction were drawn from a fairytale called the Great Race, a story widely known across the universe that blends elements of several Earth folktales, including those of Achilles, Sisyphus, and Wu Gang. In the tale, a boy is trapped in a maze where each tunnel seals behind him; presented with thousands of paths, he repeatedly chooses one that leads to danger, is killed, is resurrected by a god, and starts over. Prepotente concluded that the engineers had built the floor to behave like a [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Rupert%27s_drop Prince Rupert's drop] on an enormous scale: the whole structure held in tension, and liable to disintegrate entirely if broken at the right point.[5|Epilogue]

==Locations==
===Pole Position Chamber===
Crawlers materialized in individual starting pods, featureless rooms of clear glass roughly the size of a small apartment living room and resembling fish tanks: three solid walls, and a fourth made of thick vertical glass bars facing a round tunnel that curved away and downward like a waterslide. A gentle pull dragged everything toward the bars, the glass floors were slick enough to slide on, and inventory, magic, and chat were all suppressed inside the pods until the race began. A countdown ran above the bars.[5|Epilogue]

The pods were arranged in a corkscrew, stacked vertically and spiraling upward around a central shaft, and were to slide to a starting chute and open in order. The pod at the very bottom held the crawler who had purchased the Seventh Floor Pole Position boon.[5|Epilogue]

===Stairwells===
The exits filled the open shaft at the center of the spiral with glowing light, stacked one above another like a string of beads directly behind the rear wall of each pod. These were not ordinary stairwells: they had no stairs and no doors, and could be entered from any direction, a configuration common enough to be documented in the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook.[5|Epilogue]

===The Maze===
The tunnel network was never observed directly, since the floor collapsed before the starting chutes opened, and everything recorded about it comes from the rules read out at the start of the race. Several hundred kilometers of twisting tubes ran without dead ends, every path eventually reaching an exit, though the portals themselves exerted a physical pull on crawlers throughout. Paths fed into nodes that branched again, in some cases into hundreds of options, with pitstop safe rooms at the nodes and no backtracking. Each branch carried a marked danger rating, often with a description of what waited along the way and the distance to the next node, and some required solving math equations or puzzles to pass. Most major nodes offered a zero tube, a route carrying no danger at all and usable only once. A path closed for a period based on its difficulty once ten crawlers or a single party had passed through it.[5|Epilogue]

==Story==
In the hours before the Masquerade party, an unusual number of sponsor gifts were routed to Donut through other crawlers, and two of them proved to be tools rather than presents. The Enchanted Hammer of Fast Forward, delivered by Imani, was described as enchanted despite doing nothing magical; the Enchanted Stick of Cascadia's Screams, delivered by Dmitri Popov, was a plain aluminum rod described as not a wand but capable of something magical when used correctly. Carl found a faint impression of the number seven pressed into each one.[5|62]

At the prize counter of the party, Prepotente spent thirty of his thirty-three hunter hands on a Future Floor Boon called Seventh Floor Pole Position, listed as limited to one and marked sold out immediately afterward.[5|67]

Carl, Donut, and Prepotente arrived together in the lowest pod of the spiral. A cascade of debuffs struck them the moment they materialized, including peace bonding, a freeze, a paralysis, a slow, a teleport, and an Administrative Hold, each negated in turn by AI resolution, and four armed gnolls appeared and were removed just as quickly.[5|Epilogue]

While the countdown ran, Prepotente worked at the rear wall, knocking on the glass and checking it with a device resembling a thermometer to locate the structural weak point. He had confirmed the floor's fairytale basis in advance from an unusually long item description and from a broadcast interview he gave alongside Cascadia. He left the party, splashed a clear potion onto the glass, then pushed the metal stick through the softened wall and tapped it once with the hammer, just as the floor's introductory announcement was being read. A lightning pattern of fissures spread across the glass, and the entire level shattered into fine dust. Cascadia was heard screaming over the dungeon's announcement system as the level came apart, and every crawler was pulled into the exit portals at the same moment, bypassing the seventh floor entirely.[5|Epilogue]

Crawlers were set down in a round marble chamber holding a car-sized holographic globe of Earth, the same style of room used between the fourth and fifth floors. Squad leaders were given ninety seconds to select a starting location for the Eighth Floor, and parties larger than five were split into squads that could not be broken for the duration of that floor.[5|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The floor was open for roughly ten minutes.[6|2]
Mordecai laid the blame on Borant's engineers, who had reproduced the fairytale so faithfully that the same trick was available to anyone who knew the story, even though the boy in the tale had needed thousands of years to work it out. The Apothecary and the Plenty, who had supplied the means, were beyond the showrunners' reach, and the whole affair earned those two sponsors a great deal at the expense of the Valtay and the Syndicate as a whole, since the enormous fees paid in advance for tunnel access to livestream the crawl are not refunded when a floor is skipped.[6|2]
Zev identified the level as Cascadia's own project, one the Valtay had pushed to see built, and said that its loss, combined with the end of the sixth floor, prompted the Valtay to step back from the production's daily operation.[6|21]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The war mage Akuma later tied the floor's destruction to a wider structural problem. Crawler numbers were already higher than the dungeon had planned for, and breaking the seventh floor forced the eighth to be populated in a hurry, further depleting the deep reserves of stored mobs and NPCs.[8|38]
Donut never received the spellbook of the floor that crawlers are normally granted for the seventh, and was still raising it as a grievance on the eleventh floor.[8|88]}}

==Trivia==
*The Seventh Floor was referenced long before it was reached. The patronage rules announced at the start of the second floor stated that benefactors could transfer their patronage to other parties beginning on the seventh floor.[1|31]
*Previous iterations of the seventh floor bore no resemblance to the Great Race. One season's version was built entirely around the Guild of Suffering attempting to raise their demigod, the Night Wyrm,[5|8] and crawler Allister devoted his cookbook note on vampires to the ones he met on his own seventh floor.[5|45] Denise, the Feral Goose Mother, was plucked directly from a seventh floor when her grandfather brought his people into the bubble.[4|16]
*The AI's description of the Ursine refers in passing to the Gingers of the seventh floor, a race that never appeared during this season's version of the level.[5|10]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Volteeg, a pet turned crawler, lost his owner on his own seventh floor, which ran as an ordinary, survivable level.[7|Volteeg]
Donut has since claimed she held the number one leaderboard position for the entirety of the Seventh Floor.[7|4]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=At least two storylines written for the seventh floor went unused and were later repurposed on the tenth floor. The ginger cannibal tribes of the jungle race were lifted wholesale from it,[8|43] and Penelope, a pig magically protected by Taranis, came out of a planned succubus storyline.[8|19]}}

==Speculation==
*Prepotente purchased the Seventh Floor Pole Position boon at the prize counter, where it was listed as limited to one,[5|67] and then began the floor in the pod at the very bottom of the spiral, closest to the portals.[5|Epilogue] The text never connects the two directly, but the placement suggests the boon set starting order rather than granting any advantage inside the maze.
*The matched sevens pressed into the hammer and the stick, and the fact that both were routed to a single crawler through separate intermediaries, suggest the sponsors involved knew the floor's structural weakness well in advance and equipped for it deliberately rather than reacting to events.[5|62]

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Later references describe storylines lifted from a never-realized seventh floor and reused elsewhere.[8|43] That may point to the Great Race, or to the original seventh floor design that was pushed back and rebuilt as the Eighth Floor. The ginger material was already being referenced as seventh floor content before the substitution was announced,[5|10] which favors the second reading.}}
