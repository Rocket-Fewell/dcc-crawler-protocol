{{Quote|They're one of the most powerful entities in the universe.|Odette[1|Epilogue]}}

The Valtay Corporation is a corporate system government within the Syndicate, at once a business and a nation. Its people are a parasitic species that pilots the bodies of other races, and the corporation holds the debt of the Borant System.[1|Epilogue]

==Description==
The Valtay are a parasitic lifeform rather than a conventional humanoid race. They can and do use human bodies, but their homeworld is aquatic and they prefer water-based hosts such as the Kua-tin or the Gleeners. Their fleet is large enough to foreclose on an entire star system.[1|Epilogue]

Publicly the corporation is best known for its technology, and the versions of Dungeon Crawler World it runs favor android death machines and pulse rifles over goblins and trolls.[1|Epilogue] Its hardware reaches the Dungeon as tech-based gear such as the Mag 3040 Valtay Corp Pulse Pistol and the Valtay Perso-Shield Platinum Edition.[1|47] Entertainment nonetheless earns considerably more than technology, and part of that portfolio outlives the performer: the singer Manasa's career continued after her death, with a worm driving her body ever since.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The corporation also markets neural enhancement products that interface with a Crawler's existing wetware. Each installs on hardware the corporation did not build, and the accompanying notice directs recipients to a Valtay Corporate Outreach Center for upgrade options under the corporate motto: "Keeping the Best of You Alive."[3|13]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Mordecai identifies the species as a type of creature called a Gondii, which reactivate dead bodies so long as they enter within hours of death, read the host's old memories, and keep that body running for centuries.[4|5] Unlike the Intellect Hunters, whose stolen bodies rot almost immediately, the Valtay secrete a liquid that preserves the corpse.[4|14] A worm not currently riding a body appears as a mote of light suspended in a face-shaped bowl of dark liquid.[4|19]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Biology alone does not make a worm Valtay. The agent known as Agatha takes bodies the same way and is biologically similar, but belongs to neither the Valtay nor the Apothecary.[6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The Valtay charter permits a worm to take over a brain only if the host was willing. Outlaw gondii who ignore that law hire themselves out as mercenaries, hopping from host to host and overclocking bodies past their normal limits, and are reputed to be among the most expensive and dangerous hired guns in the galaxy.[7|30]}}

==History==
The corporation is the principal creditor of the Borant System. When Earth was collected, a Valtay fleet sat parked outside the system, hours away from a full-scale collection action, until the Kua-tin stopped them by starting the season early. The season following Earth's is scheduled to be run by the Valtay.[1|Epilogue] The corporation also held a long-standing alliance with the Skull Empire going into the crawl.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=In the early days of the Syndicate, the Valtay and the orcs together wiped out the H'lene.[3|30]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The Valtay routinely enter into life contracts with citizens across the galaxy: the signer agrees to let a worm take over their body upon death, which keeps the persona, and any career attached to it, running afterward. The moon elf Drick is one such signer.[5|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Valtay commercial reach extends well past the crawl. The corporation operates leased manufacturing facilities across the galaxy,[8|28] and ordinary businesses operate as its subsidiaries: one publishing contract withholds an author's royalties for the entirety of their natural life, payable to heirs only after they prove the author was of good moral character, with the option to accelerate those payments if the author signs a Valtay life contract.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Sydnee Iglacia]}}

==Story==
===Book 1===
Valtay-manufactured gear surfaces in Carl's inventory on the Second Floor, and the system flags the Trans Tunnel 7C Orator Relay among it as a non-sanctioned communications device that will not function inside the Dungeon.[1|47] Odette warns Carl to be very cautious if the corporation is trying to reach him.[1|Epilogue]

===Book 2===
The alliance with the Skull Empire shatters when a botched assassination attempt on Carl and Donut kills Manasa instead; a Valtay dreadnaught in orbit retaliates by destroying the royal yacht, and panel commentators immediately observe that the rupture weakens the Valtay position in the bankruptcy action.[2|19]

===Book 3===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=After Carl kills Gore-Gore on the fourth floor, the Valtay become his first sponsor.[3|11] Their first gift is a Bronze Benefactor Box containing Neural Enhancer #544, a pill that permanently alters the brain and grants the ability to identify and analyze subspace portals.[3|13]}}

===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=A second Bronze Benefactor Box on the fifth floor holds Neural Enhancer #275.[4|8] The Syndicate liaison who questions Carl over the death of Administrator Loita turns out to be a Valtay worm floating beneath his hood: Orren.[4|19] At the end of the floor, a Valtay attorney helps Borant defeat a last-minute legal challenge from the Skull Empire on a question of venue.[4|34]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=A Syndicate court declares the Borant Corporation an independent non-governmental entity, and that ruling automatically executes a previously filed judgment granting the Valtay 51% ownership of the company, making them the new principal owners of the Earth crawl. The regency change costs Carl his sponsor on a declared conflict of interest.[5|19] Lexis explains that the Valtay had sponsored him because they knew he would be a thorn in the mudskippers' side, and warns that the new owners will want a top ten of their own choosing.[5|20]
Security around the crawl tightens under their control. Rental trailers are kicked off system following an escape attempt, and interviews are moved to a Valtay landing vessel parked on the ocean floor,[5|30] patrolled by gnoll mercenaries and staffed by Valtay Corporation porter bots.[5|32]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Valtay had pushed to get the seventh floor built and funded; after Prepotente breaks that floor, they step back from the production's day-to-day operations, hoping to preserve investor confidence for the following crawl, which they are also scheduled to run.[6|21]
Once the system AI seizes control and the quarantine traps the galaxy's elite in orbit, the Valtay advocate for an extinction event to end the crawl as quickly as possible so the quarantine can be lifted. They negotiate to send tens of thousands of armed mercenaries into Faction Wars, distributed evenly across the sponsored teams, and begin recalling the guilds that had been protecting their surface assets.[6|Epilogue]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Gondii worms take part in the assault on the Princess Posse stronghold on the ninth floor, puppeting several of the mages who maintain the castle's protective spells in order to switch off the interior wall protections from outside.[7|30]
When the Syndicate Council orders the Borant headquarters to initiate its self-destruct, the Valtay showrunners countermand the order.[7|81]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=During the tenth floor, the Emperator of the Valtay fleet brings a system-busting bomb through the tunnel gate while the system AI is ostensibly distracted. The AI stops it, then calls the Emperator out by title in a floor-wide announcement.[8|23]}}

==Pop Culture==
The corporation's effort to collect on the Borant System debt is a recurring topic on Syndicate talk shows, where panelists line up on either side of the bankruptcy question. Individuals driven by a Valtay worm are commonly called "worm heads," a term used even affectionately.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=A prior season of Dungeon Crawler World run by the corporation is remembered above all for the death of the crawler Hoon, who reached the elevator to the twelfth floor and found his own five children repurposed as sentinel hunter killers guarding the exit. The use of collected children and pregnant women was banned afterward.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Carl's attorney Quasar calls most Valtay tricksy and reserves particular contempt for the naked ones, arguing that a worm riding nothing is usually a sign that something is wrong with it.[5|3]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=A popular tunnel drama called The Maggot Squad follows a crew of outlaw gondii mercenaries. The Valtay government hates the show, which has done nothing to slow its ratings.[7|30]}}
