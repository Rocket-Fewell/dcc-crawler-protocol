{{Quote|Elves worship the Oak Mother, the mother of all gods. Her name is Apito.|Mordecai[2|17]}}

Apito, the Oak Mother, is a goddess of the Dungeon's in-game pantheon, revered as the mother of all gods and worshipped by elves, most fervently by the doomsday cult known as the 201st Security Group.[2|17]

== Description ==
Apito's mythology is a tangle of conflicting and apocryphal stories: her angels, the skyfowl, must remain free and alive to keep the path to heaven open; she calls them "bless-ed"; and in one apocryphal strain, all violence against them must stop or Apito will destroy every world.[2|17] It is that last, doomsday reading that her militia of city elves clings to, awaiting the day the tree goddess unmakes the universe.[2|17]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=She is no mere figure of elven folklore. The crawler cookbook ranks her with Eris among the pantheon's "big" deities, invulnerable except on the twelfth floor and above.[3|32] She also bears the title Oak Goddess.[3|24] In the deep past the elf mothers abandoned the vulture goddess Nekhebit to worship her instead; the enraged Nekhebit blamed the male-dominated high elf court for letting her worshippers stray and cursed their seed, creating the Fae Diaspora.[3|24]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=By the sixth floor her place atop the divine hierarchy is clear: her sprawling family forever vies for control,[5|65] but Apito is the one truly in charge.[5|62] The high elves hail her as the Blessed Oak Mother and the most perfect of Mothers, a peacemaker who wants nothing more than for her squabbling children to get along.[5|65]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=She keeps that peace sternly, threatening the goddess Ysalte with banishment to the Nothing and sending her fleeing to Sheol.[6|66] Her oldest rival has stirred as well: the primordial Nekhebit has returned to the Halls bearing an ancient grudge against Apito's whole family, and against Apito above all.[6|38]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The dungeon's deep mythology recasts her entirely. Apito is no eldest god but the one who ended the old order, bringing down Nekhebit, last of the wild elder deities who came before the present pantheon, and absorbing the vulture goddess's remaining worshippers while her own children expelled, banished, or murdered the rest.[8|69] The myth credits her with a terrible power to match, said to be shared only by the fabled Scavenger's Daughter: the ability to erase the present pantheon and start again.[8|69]}}

== Family ==
Apito sits at the root of the dungeon's divine family tree, and the title Mother of All Gods is the first thing said of her.[2|17]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=She is the mother of the war god Grull, borne to the deity Taranis; their son is reckoned one of the few trueborn heirs to the Celestial Ascendency.[3|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Taranis is also her husband.[5|65] Her wider brood is vast and loosely drawn: the shapeshifting nature goddess Diwata counts among it without being a direct child,[5|62] as does the lust-god Yarilo, Taranis's own child.[5|65]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The fire-god Emberus is named as her son, which makes his murdered son Geyrun her grandson; Taranis's younger brother Hellik is her brother-in-law, as (in the pantheon's tangled way) is Emberus.[6|18]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Hellik puts it most baldly, naming Apito "our mother and sister," at once parent and sibling to himself and Emberus.[7|54]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The deepest roots invert everything. Apito is the daughter of Nekhebit, the elder vulture goddess and "Scavenger Mother of Mothers," alongside the centipede goddess Scolopendra and a shadowy son, the three of them sometimes described as one divine being split three ways.[8|69] Her own children, conceived either through the All Tree or by Apito impregnating herself, became the present pantheon.[8|69] Taranis is at once her son and her husband,[8|19] a pairing his sister, the chaos goddess Eris, throws in his face during the final battle.[8|97] Emberus, Hellik, and Eris are his siblings and her children.[8|19] Yarilo counts Apito as both grandmother and stepmother.[8|70]}}

== Story ==
=== Book 2 ===
Apito never appears in person; on the Third Floor her influence reaches Carl and Donut entirely through her devotees.[2|20] Within the Medium Skyfowl Settlement, the 201st Security Group runs the Apito Education Center, a recruitment front flagged on the minimap as a corrupt temple, where robed wand-bearers press passersby to hear the Oak Mother's "good news."[2|20] Taking the settlement's skyfowl for living angels, the cult branded Donut the "Oak Fell," Apito's foretold antichrist and the great beast who would unravel the heavens and kill all the gods, a conclusion sealed by the Enchanted Anklet of the Fallen Oak she wore.[2|20]

=== Book 5 ===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=On the sixth floor her influence returns in force through the Butcher's Masquerade, one of her holiest sacraments, a spell only the most divine may cast and only once in a lifetime.[5|65] The first was held in the Halls of Ascendency, hosted by Apito and Taranis as a night of respite for their feuding family; when the lust-god Yarilo broke that peace she banished him to the Nothing, and the ensuing brawl slaughtered every mortal and demigod present, leaving her in tears.[5|65] Ever after, the party became a sacred rite of non-violence, rewarded with a boon from the goddess if the evening ends without bloodshed.[5|65]
When Queen Imogen of the high elves cast the spell, styling herself a "Chosen Daughter of Apito,"[5|73] Apito formed the guest list herself, deliberately seeding it with enemies to sow discord and test the host.[5|65] She appeared in person to the half-naiad Signet, who found herself invited and then abruptly standing in the ballroom.[5|67] The instant the celestial peace seal shattered, the system announced that Apito had turned her back: the masquerade failed to cast, and the wards fell.[5|74]
The ball's grand prize was a memorial crystal, put up because the butcher spell requires the host to offer her most valuable item.[5|69] Nobody could afford it, so it returned to Imogen; after her death it was looted from her body and identified as the Memorial Crystal: Apito, a strange artifact given that Apito is not believed to be dead.[5|77]}}

=== Book 6 ===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Apito does not appear on the eighth floor, but the mystery of her death deepens there. The high cleric Pater Coal states plainly that memorial crystals form only when a god has died, yet a crystal made by Apito exists and Apito lives; Emberus himself, he says, worries that something may have happened to his mother.[6|18]}}

=== Book 7 ===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=On the ninth floor the mystery finally takes shape, and it is far worse than a quiet death. During the raid on Club Vanquisher, the god Hellik sits down beside a battered Carl and gives him a message for Emberus: Apito has been corrupted, "there is a malevolent presence about her," and the All Tree is beginning to rot.[7|54] He names Nekhebit, newly returned, as the hand behind it all, believes his nephew Geyrun had learned of the corruption, and pleads for the estranged brothers to work together.[7|54]
When the banished Yarilo stepped back out of the Nothing, the Memorial Crystal: Apito flared like a beacon, and Carl put the riddle bluntly: Apito is "both dead and not dead."[7|64]}}

=== Book 8 ===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Across the tenth and eleventh floors the riddle of the memorial crystal breaks open. Taranis tears the crystal from Prepotente's neck, and Carl reckons the god wanted it because it holds Apito's memories and spells.[8|98] The chaos goddess Eris mocks him for believing their mother merely missing: Apito has been broken into five pieces, four of them present in the room.[8|97] Taranis professes not to grasp what she means, then seizes two of them anyway, the boss Krakaren and the sentient sex doll Samantha, before he vanishes.[8|97]
In the book's closing interlude, the effort to restore her moves to Sheol, where Taranis and Krakaren gather at a temple to make whole the being who is at once his mother and his wife. Samantha, who hitched a ride and then slipped away, reports that it will not work: a piece is still missing.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Louis Santiago 2]}}

== Trivia ==
* Apito's iconography is the tree: her recruitment front bears a tree symbol, and her fallen devotees are said to "blossom on Her tree."[2|20]
* Items made of oakwood, such as the Enchanted Anklet of the Fallen Oak, terrify her followers, a curious aversion for worshippers of the Oak Mother.[2|20]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Apito's reply to her son Grull, that the only masks at a maskless masquerade are the smiles and goodwill the guests wear for one another, is considered sacred gospel among the elves.[5|65]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The Varg Vikernes Achievement banned Carl from every temple but those of his own god and of deities who had named him a friend of the church. Apito, to his surprise, was on that list, a tie he admits he does not yet understand.[7|57]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Despite being the daughter of Nekhebit, Apito is not the fabled "Scavenger's Daughter": that title belongs to a separate, even more powerful entity said to share her pantheon-erasing power. Carl makes exactly this mix-up before Rosetta sets him straight.[8|69]}}

== Speculation ==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=That the All Tree rots alongside Apito's corruption ties the cosmic tree directly to the Oak Mother, strong support for the idea that her decay and its own are the same wound.[7|54]If Nekhebit is the hand behind that rot, her elevation of Donut, branded the Oak Fell on the third floor and now styled "Champion of Nekhebit," looks less like coincidence than like a piece on the same board: Apito's foretold destroyer, claimed by the very rival poisoning Apito's tree.[7|78]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The five pieces revelation is the long-awaited key to "both dead and not dead": Apito truly died, hence the memorial crystal, but was shattered rather than ended.[8|97] If the crystal holding her memories and spells is one fragment,[8|98] and Krakaren and Samantha are two more,[8|97] then three of the five are already accounted for, and the hunt for the missing piece may shape the endgame.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Louis Santiago 2]The AI's boast that the Ascendency's winner might kill it "the way Apito did in the legends" squares neatly with her toppling of Nekhebit and the elder gods; the dungeon's master intelligence plainly treats it as the precedent for one god killing another.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Prime Minister Victory]}}
