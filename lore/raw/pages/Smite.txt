Smite is a severe and often lethal Divine punishment inflicted upon crawlers or NPCs who have provoked the ire of the Gods in Dungeon Crawler World.[4|30]

==Description==
A Smite is a targeted retribution from a deity that can manifest in various painful forms, though it typically results in the target's immediate death.[8|35] It does not always kill, however; in one case a smite instead made an afflicted crawler's existing blindness debuff permanent.[4|30] Smites are provoked by angering a god or by violating the strict rules of a worshipped faith.

Known causes for receiving a smite include voluntarily leaving a faith or abandoning a god, which carries an automatic smite[8|95][4|30]; failing to complete a god's assigned quest within its specified time limit or floor constraint[8|35][4|32]; or missing the required daily worship ritual. Skipping worship for a single day causes the deity to "turn her back" and withdraw all benefits, two days incurs a debuff, and three consecutive days strips the adherent of their faith entirely and carries a 50% chance of a smite.[4|30]

{{Spoiler | book = 6 | spoiler = Certain spells and actions can also provoke a smite. Casting the Black Nimbus spell—which strips a target of their deity's blessing—carries a chance of forcing a smite upon them that grows at higher spell levels; crawlers are also cautioned against casting it on members of their own religion more than two or three times in a row.[6|3] Before a smite is fully executed, the victim may receive a "Shunned marker" floating over their head, such as a flaming anvil, signaling that the smiting is imminent.[6|15]}}

{{Spoiler | book = 7 | spoiler = If a crawler is named a Champion of a deity, the god can order them to undertake tasks under the direct threat of a smite for disobedience.[7|79]}}

{{Spoiler | book = 8 | spoiler = Some restricted or hidden areas, such as the Pineapple Cabaret (Location) escape route, are rumored to trigger an automatic smite if entered by a crawler who actively worships a god—though the crawlers were never able to confirm whether this was true or merely a deterrent meant to keep certain crawlers out.[8|58]}}

==Story==
Smites play a central role in Carl's volatile relationship with the pantheon, particularly with Emberus, the God of Sun and Ash. After becoming an adherent of Emberus on the Fifth Floor, Carl is saddled with two near-impossible church quests, each carrying a smite as the penalty for failure: to kill the god Hellik before reaching the 12th floor, and to solve the murder of Emberus's son, Geyrun, before exiting the 18th floor.[4|32]

{{Spoiler | book = 6 | spoiler = During a Celestial Thorn Room event on the Eighth Floor, crawlers Anton and Paz receive a flaming-anvil shunned marker over their heads—signaling an imminent smite—after the group repairs Yemaya's shrine instead of Ogun's.[6|15]}}

{{Spoiler | book = 7 | spoiler = Donut's status as the Champion of the goddess Nekhebit likewise means she could face a smite should she disobey the goddess's direct orders.[7|79]}}

{{Spoiler | book = 8 | spoiler = Carl ultimately finds himself boxed in: a smite awaits him whether he fails Emberus's Hellik quest or formally abandons it, while Emberus has privately vowed to kill him outright should he actually succeed in killing Hellik. This leaves killing Emberus himself as his only real escape.[8|35]To outmaneuver the gods during the confrontation inside Club Scolopendra, Carl deliberately abandons his faith in Emberus. The act instantly triggers a smite: the twin sun-disk tattoos on the backs of his hands burst into searing flames.[8|95]Smites are not limited to crawlers. The mantaur Corcunda, whose devotion to the war god Grull lapsed when he split into his two halves, is marked for a smite and ultimately gives his life to satisfy Grull's punishment during a race.[8|66]}}
