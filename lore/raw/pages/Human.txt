Humans are one of the six basic starter species the Syndicate seeds across the galaxy for Dungeon Crawler World, and the indigenous species of Earth.[1|4] A crawler may choose "Human" during Third Floor race selection to keep their current form in exchange for a small boost to stat points and skill training.[2|2]

== AI Description (Third Floor Race Selection) ==
You're already a human. I'm going to go out on a limb here and guess you don't need a description. If you choose this, nothing will change. Except for these racial benefits:
*+2 to all base stats.
*Adaptability. +2% faster skill progression.
Choosing this race unlocks multiple exclusive, earth-based classes.[2|2]

== Description ==
Humans are a seeded race, sown by the Syndicate onto compatible worlds and left to grow unattended for thousands of years. Once a civilization takes hold — and as long as it has a working government — the Syndicate stages "First Contact," a legal milestone that clears it to return a couple thousand years later and strip the planet of its resources.[1|4]

The average adult human enters the World Dungeon with three to five points in each of the five core Player Stats (Strength, Intelligence, Constitution, Dexterity, and Charisma), one to eight levels across various Skills, nonmagical gear, and no Spells.[1|4] Everyone is assigned the Human race by default on entry[1|2] and may keep or change it at third-floor race selection. A human who stays human undergoes no dramatic physical change but gains the Adaptability trait, a +2 bonus to all base stats, and a +2% boost to skill progression.[2|2] Roughly 80% of crawlers on Dungeon Crawler World: Earth chose to remain human after reaching the third floor.[2|7]

== Appearance ==
Earth humans keep their ordinary appearance, varying widely in height, weight, skin tone, and hair color. During race selection the System AI cheekily declines to describe them at all, noting that the crawler is "already a human" and surely doesn't need the rundown.[2|2]

Humans are spread across the galaxy, and — possibly because of genetic drift and differing gravity — Syndicate humans come in a range of shapes and sizes. The most common type appears narrower than an Earth human (though not necessarily thinner), with a head roughly three-quarters the width of an Earth human's and eyes set noticeably close together.[1|27][4|Epilogue]

== Subraces ==
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Gingers are classified by the dungeon as a human subrace produced by ancient alien interference — much like the octopus. The origin of the corruption is unknown, though the Nullians are suspected of meddling in Earth's development to create them.[8|43]
Ginger men are described as sexually deviant to a fault, while the women are reputed to be "extra spicy." Both possess a whacked-out pain tolerance that leaves them more susceptible to heat and cold but immune to electrical and acid attacks. The dungeon most often deploys them as cannibalistic jungle mobs, and their chieftains can wield a dreaded Soul Suck attack that drains a victim into a helpless fugue until a cleric restores them.[8|43]}}

== Subclasses ==
Choosing or retaining the Human race — or choosing Primal — unlocks a set of exclusive, Earth-based classes that non-Earth races can't access.[2|2] A few notable examples include:
* Bomb Squad Tech: An explosive-focused class that boosts explosive and trap skills, keeps explosives from degrading in the user's hands, and grants the Limb Regeneration Benefit — at the cost of Intelligence.[2|2]
* Prizefighter: A Monk subclass built for melee brawlers, with heavy boosts to Constitution, Strength, Pugilism, and Unarmed Combat, offset by penalties to Intelligence and Charisma.[2|2]
* Compensated Anarchist: A Monk/Rogue hybrid centered on traps, bombs, and Hide in Shadows, with a penalty to bladed-weapon damage.[2|2]
* Former Child Actor: An offshoot of the Character Actor class granting immunity to poisons and diseases, accelerated Charisma growth, and the Manager Benefit.[2|1]
* NecroBard: A hybrid class pairing an entertainment skill — bongos, kazoo, or singing — with the necromantic art of raising the dead.[2|1]

== Story ==
When the Borant Corporation claimed Earth, roughly 13 million humans volunteered to enter the World Dungeon rather than remain on the resource-stripped surface. By the time the dungeon sealed, that number had already fallen to just under 10 million.[1|4]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Human crawlers are generally viewed by the galaxy's audience with a mix of amusement and disdain, yet they keep surprising the showrunners with their sheer tenacity. By the fourth floor the humans had banded together into massive, heavily coordinated parties and guilds, drastically lowering the attrition rate the showrunners had expected.[3|3][3|29]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Humans survived in such great numbers that the AI was forced to continually ramp up the dungeon's difficulty and draw emergency NPCs from deep reserves just to compensate, straining mobs and locations that were never meant to be reused so heavily.[8|38]}}

== Dungeon Lore ==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Humans are bound up in the deepest mechanics of the World Dungeon. Every living thing born on a pre-seeded world carries a miniature Primal system inside its brain — an organic implant roughly the size of a grain of sand — and it is this implant that lets a Crawler interface with the dungeon at all.[7|Prologue]
Over a lifetime that implant accumulates rare elements, and incubating those elements is the real reason the Syndicate seeds human worlds: they are needed to sustain the galaxy's enhancement zones. When a human dies in the dungeon, the Syndicate harvests the element from the corpse and feeds it to the bloated, comatose center system, which in turn lets Syndicate citizens live unnaturally long lives. The harvested remains of an entire crawl, in the end, fit on a single transport.[7|Prologue]
The whole spectacle of humans fighting for survival is broadcast to quintillions of citizens, functioning at once as an entertainment product, an economic engine, and a systematic culling of an entire species.[7|Prologue][1|4]}}

== Trivia ==
* A human crawler who declines to remain human on the third floor can transform into a huge range of alien and fantasy species — Coal Engines, Lyrx Elves, Hobgoblins, and far stranger — a change that rewrites them down to the cellular level and can never be undone.[2|2][1|4]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Borant Corporation routinely recycles human "biological excess" — the leftover body parts of fallen crawlers — to build the dungeon's monsters. One vampire pterodactyl was assembled in part from the body of the President of the United States (plus a couple of horses from France and a panda).[6|46]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Former human crawlers who tap out to take NPC or indentureship deals — especially those who changed their race — often suffer lasting physical consequences outside the dungeon, from collapsing under heavy gravity to burning under ordinary UV light, leaving them dependent on constant care.[7|Volteeg]}}

== Speculation ==
Syndicate Cross-Contamination: As early as the first floor, crawlers learn that the Syndicate doesn't merely seed a world and leave — long-lived Syndicate agents may spend thousands or even millions of years embedded on a planet, steering its culture and technology and inventing stories and songs that already exist elsewhere in the galaxy.[1|27] It is widely speculated that this is no accident, and that the aim is to give newly harvested populations common ground with the rest of the galaxy.[1|27]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=By the ninth floor, Carl reaches the uneasy realization that the Syndicate's citizens as a whole behave remarkably like humans — same mannerisms, same bureaucratic frustrations, same social structures — despite wildly different anatomies and homeworlds, which lends real weight to the steered-culture theory.[7|15]}}
