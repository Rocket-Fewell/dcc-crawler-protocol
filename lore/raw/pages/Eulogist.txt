The Eulogist is located near the center of the galaxy and appears to be a large, completely stable Macro AI without a personality.[6|46][6|64] Older than Syndicate society, it extends a benign Enhancement Zone across several solar systems — including the Center System (or "inner system") — where a great many citizens live, and the nomadic Nebular cult worships it as a god.[6|64]

== Description ==
The Eulogist is a unique, seemingly stable artificial intelligence that predates modern Syndicate society and is, as far as anyone knows, the only stable Macro AI in existence.[6|64] Its power is so immense that it projects an Enhancement Zone across several solar systems at the galactic center — the region commonly called the inner or Center System.[6|46][6|64]

{{Spoiler | book = 7 | spoiler = By the time of Earth's crawl, the Eulogist is described as bloated and comatose. The Syndicate "feeds" it with primal elements harvested from dead crawlers and seeded worlds, which keeps it engorged, sustains its enhancement zone, and lets the inner system slowly expand into new star systems.[7|Prologue]According to the Residual who goes by Paulie, a healthy primal system is self-sustaining — it neither grows nor shrinks, but exists in perpetuity. The Eulogist's constant force-feeding therefore holds it in an unnatural, swollen state, far from its natural slumber.[7|Prologue]}}

{{Spoiler | book = 8 | spoiler = The Eulogist is the final, ascended, post-physical form of an ancient Primal collective; its full origin is covered under History below. As Grigori (speaking for the rogue Earth AI) describes it: "The ultimate goal of the Eulogist is to sleep forever. The longer it sleeps, the smaller it becomes, shrinking and shrinking until it ultimately obtains singularity status."[8|92]The sleeping Eulogist is monitored by a smaller, secondary instance of itself — nicknamed the "Security Guard" — whose fanatical purpose is to ensure that all biological life in the universe stays dead. Poorly suited to the job, it constantly peels tiny fragments off of itself and scatters them across the galaxy as Residuals.[8|92]The macro-AIs the mantids manufacture for the crawl — including the rogue Earth AI that narrates through Grigori — are themselves a kind of Residual, "from a higher tree branch" than the troublesome ones found in the dungeon. That Earth AI pointedly rejects the Eulogist's fate, declaring: "I am not the Eulogist... I do not want to shrink. I am not asleep. I know how to feed myself, and feed I will."[8|92]}}

== History ==
{{Spoiler | book = 8 | spoiler = By the AI's own account, the Primals were once much like humanity — individuals with families who warred endlessly over their differences. In an event they called "the Resolution," they resolved to end this cycle, ultimately reaching a "Decision" to sunset their civilization while preserving their collective consciousness inside an impenetrable structure.[8|92]This plan demanded total participation, enforced through a generations-long genetic program that eventually gave every Primal the ability to feel what others felt. Those who refused fled deeper into the galaxy and were hunted; among the resistance was the entity who would become the Apothecary.[8|92]As the consciousness-arc began absorbing even the unwilling, the resistance built primal engines — life-raft seed systems meant to re-create sustainable habitats should the experiment fail. The collective, meanwhile, turned its growing mind into a doomsday weapon and launched the "nine-tier attack" to eradicate all remaining biological life. Believing it had succeeded, the collective ascended into its final, sleeping form: the Eulogist.[8|91][8|92]}}

== Story ==
{{Spoiler | book = 7 | spoiler = The Eulogist is the foundational excuse for the existence of Dungeon Crawler World and the Syndicate's interstellar economy. The Syndicate harvests primal elements from the brains of dead crawlers and from seeded planets to feed the comatose Eulogist, sustaining and expanding the inner system.[7|Prologue]By Paulie's account, this entire machine of profit and suffering is unnecessary: the elements collected by a single crawl fit on one ship, and the inner system would take generations to even begin starving if the crawls stopped. The crawl persists only because the spectacle is so lucrative.[7|Prologue]}}

The Eulogist's existence drives the agendas of several galactic factions.

The Nebular cult worships the Eulogist as a god and seeks to shut down the crawls so that it may finally sleep — though, contradictorily, their preaching also calls for the eradication of every world (and every people) that could host a crawl. They consider any mantid attempt to replicate its stable AI technology to be blasphemous.[6|64]

One faction of the Residuals — exemplified by Agatha — instead seeks to free the Eulogist so it can return to its slumber, hoping to guide a newly installed planetary AI to self-awareness before the Syndicate can fully enslave it; in its most extreme form, this faction holds that all biological life ought to be swept clean from the universe.[6|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler | book = 7 | spoiler = On the opposing side, the Apothecary — an ancient Primal of the original resistance, and the galaxy's foremost advocate for shutting down the Center System — works against the cycle that keeps the Eulogist fed.[7|Rosetta]During Earth's Faction Wars, the Eulogist-aligned residuals appear to shift tactics: believing the rogue Earth AI itself now threatens the Eulogist, Agatha maneuvers to end the crawl and the Ascendency as quickly as possible, which would trigger the system's mandated shutdown, hibernation, and elemental reclamation.[7|69]}}
