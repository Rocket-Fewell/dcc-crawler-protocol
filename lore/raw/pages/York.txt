York is a Tenodera former crawler and the author of the tenth edition of the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook.[3|15][7|Epilogue]

== Description ==
Carl first meets York not as a person but as a voice on the page. While resting during his crawl, Carl reads York's contributions to the cookbook — pages of rambling, philosophical essays he can barely parse, though one passage refuses to leave him. The entries reveal a being who came from a hive yet spent his life feeling utterly alone, and who took a strange comfort in the idea that the cookbook could become a hive of its own: a fellowship of crawlers who would never actually meet. The passage appears to be among York's last entries before he died or lost the book.[3|15]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=York is a deeply solitary, contemplative being who mourns his own profound lack of emotion. Though he came from a hive species, his stubborn individuality meant he never fit in, and he moved through his entire crawl feeling completely alone, never truly part of a team.[7|Epilogue]
To cope with the hollowness inside him, York writes poetry obsessively, believing that words twisted in just the right way might artificially raise the feelings he can no longer summon.[7|Epilogue] Because of his constant versifying, his resistance comrades assume he has gone soft. In reality, his absence of emotion makes him a chillingly reliable soldier — one who can carry out horrific orders, including the destruction of a ship full of children, without hesitation.[7|Epilogue]}}

== Appearance ==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=York is a Tenodera, an insectoid species closely related to the mantids. Compared to the larger mantids, the tenodera are physically much smaller, completely flightless, and generally far less aggressive and violent.[7|Epilogue]
The larger mantids hold a deep hatred for the tenodera. Because the two can crossbreed, the mantids fear what would happen if the tenodera mixed into their population en masse, and many openly call for the extermination of York's kind.[7|Epilogue]}}

== History ==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Before the Dungeon, York lived as part of his hive. His entire hive was brought into the crawl, where they were picked off one by one — and York felt impotent against the bureaucratic alien machines that had subjugated his people. Somewhere around the ninth floor, a numbness set in. York believed it came from something buried in his tenodera DNA: his warrior ancestors had been deliberately bred to feel nothing, the better to break into enemy hives and kill the young inside, back before his people fought their wars with tanks and drones rather than the chemicals their ancient queens once drilled into their soldiers' brains.[7|Epilogue]
York took a deal at the end of the tenth floor to exit the dungeon, working off his indenture as a Game Guide and a Guildhall Instructor in the Poetry guild. Once freed, he spent years in self-reflection and contemplation before returning to fight the system.[7|Epilogue]}}

== Story ==
York's most visible mark on the story is the cookbook itself. His rambling, half-finished philosophical essays — apparently among his last entries before he died or lost the book — lodge in Carl's mind during the Fourth Floor crawl, nudging him toward the conviction that he can't simply abandon the crawlers stranded around him.[3|15]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=York did not die. Long after his own crawl, he became an operative in the resistance organization known as the Open Intellect Action Network. During Faction Wars, he served as the sole living crewman aboard the Crest behemoth starship Homecoming Queen, holding it in orbit above Earth.[7|Epilogue]
On Rosetta's orders, York used the heavily — and secretly — armed ship to vaporize an orc yacht carrying the children of the Skull Empire, among them the newly crowned boy-king of the system; in the chaos of everyone firing on everyone, the kill was written off as crossfire.[7|Epilogue][7|Rosetta] Afterward, he coordinated by radio with the old elf Boomer to ready the ship's breaching suits, enhancement-zone suits, and power armor, preparing the resistance to carry its war out of the dungeon and onto the surface. It was only then, at last, that York felt something.[7|Epilogue]}}

== Speculation ==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The text makes clear that York writes poetry hoping to feel again, and that he served as a Guildhall Instructor in the Poetry guild during his indenture.[7|Epilogue] It's reasonable to read that guild tenure less as a genuine love of the craft and more as a deliberate attempt to medicate his own emotional numbness — to study, methodically, how words might be made to do the feeling for him.}}
