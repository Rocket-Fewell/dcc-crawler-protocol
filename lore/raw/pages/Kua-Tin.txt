The Kua-Tin are an alien race of small, water-breathing humanoid fish. They are the dominant species of the Borant System and principal owners of the Borant Corporation,[1|2] which produces Dungeon Crawler World: Earth[1|28] and staffs it with Kua-Tin admins.[1|36]

==AI Description==
This is a rendition of a Kua-Tin, the dominant species of the Borant System and principal owners of the Borant Corporation. Make sure you recognize these guys. There'll be a test later.[1|2]

==Description==
Kua-Tin cannot breathe air unaided. Outside of water, most wear a small rebreather around the neck,[1|36] though Zev wears a full water suit in the Dungeon, as Borant staff receive no protections on the first three floors.[1|36][2|15] Kua-Tin admins appear with an audible pop, standing in a puddle,[1|35] and depart with another pop and a splash of water.[1|36]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Kua-Tin breathe through gills.[4|18]}}

Kua-Tin measure time in unfamiliar units, such as a "crutch," and schedule relatively, as in "minus six plus one full."[1|36] According to Zev, the Kua-Tin are not known for scripted drama, and no serials may run in the Borant System while they run the game.[2|12]

Kua-Tin are derisively called "mudskippers." The slur was rarely used until the Kua-Tin tried to have the Syndicate classify it as hate speech, which popularized it.[1|29]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The AI names Cascadia "Mudskipper Cascadia" in an achievement.[6|40]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The AI forces Zev to call herself "mudskipper Zev" over the loudspeaker.[8|23]}}

==Appearance==
Kua-Tin are small humanoid fish with fish-like heads and arms; Zev stands about two feet tall, barely reaching Carl's knee.[1|36] The fish-demon carvings on the stairwells and on the thirty-foot doors of the Dungeon Entrance and each floor depict the Kua-Tin as massive, menacing figures,[1|2][2|25] far larger than their true size.[1|36]

Zev's water suit resembles a 1950s science-fiction astronaut outfit, with a round glass helmet of bubbling water, tubes running to a bulky backpack, a white mesh body suit, and a small speaker that amplifies her voice.[1|36]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Cascadia has long, droopy, catfish-like whiskers and scales with a dull sheen. Carl suspects the whiskers may mark her as a different kind of Kua-Tin than Zev.[6|21]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Zev refers to Cascadia's limbs as flippers.[8|23]}}

==Government==
The Kua-Tin system government is widely disliked across the Syndicate, though Odette distinguishes it from the Borant Corporation, some of whose employees she considers decent.[1|29] The government is controlled by the ultra-nationalist Bloom, usually called "the Party," which held less than 15% of the vote when Mordecai signed his indentureship contract and has since driven the system into bankruptcy. All Kua-Tin must wear a badge indicating whether they are Party members, and non-members live in fear of members.[1|36]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=According to Loita, Bloom ideology centers on system strength, "True Unity," and the "Great Consensus." The Party regards Earth's "dry," diversified culture as a contagion corrupting young Kua-Tin ("fry") and intends to close the Borant System's borders after the crawl so that a "reawakening" can occur.[4|18]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=A civil war erupts in the Kua-Tin home system.[5|21] The system soon goes dark, and Kua-Tin employees who stayed with the company after the change in control do not know whether they will be welcomed home or executed as traitors.[5|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=According to Princess Vinata, the rebels hired the Valtay to help defeat the Bloom, but the war is going poorly; a derelict warship struck the liquid habitation ring around Borant Three, killing millions.[7|43] Rishi describes the Bloom as believing in racial purity and resenting the cultural influence of "dry" races.[7|58]}}

==Story==
===Book 1===
Carl first learns of the Kua-Tin from the carvings leading to the First Floor.[1|2] On the Second Floor, Zev visits the Royal Court in Mordecai's guild[1|35] and becomes their PR agent under the Crawler Assisted Outreach Program.[1|36]

===Book 3===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Mukta, Zev's former substitute, and Loita are both Bloom members.[3|25] After the Fourth Floor, Loita takes over as the Royal Court's primary PR agent, while Zev, kept on as social media manager, is sent to a "temporary reeducation retreat."[3|Epilogue]}}

===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=After Loita is killed by an exploding Veriluxx toy,[4|18] Syndicate liaison Orren tells Carl that the Kua-Tin intervened to keep him in the game, and Zev returns as the team's temporary PR agent.[4|19]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=A Syndicate court ruling grants the Valtay 51% ownership of the Borant Corporation, which remains in charge of the crawl but no longer answers to the Borant System government.[5|19] Donut reveals that she had smuggled Valtay agents to Zev and the Borant Emancipation Front.[5|20]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The voice of the daily announcements, heard since the first episode,[1|14] is revealed to be Cascadia,[6|21] the Kua-Tin lead engineer and executive producer of the season.[6|40]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=As Cascadia's health declines, the AI has Zev read a scripted announcement in her place.[8|23] With most of the Borant staff barely functioning, Zev ends up running nearly the entire production on her own.[8|27]}}

==Dungeon Lore==
The Kua-Tin-owned Borant Corporation holds regency over Earth's solar system and runs the World Dungeon.[1|1] The Valtay, who are pursuing a collection action against the Borant System, are parasites that prefer aquatic hosts such as the Kua-Tin.[1|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The level 22 Pollyslog mobs are drawn from prehistoric Kua-Tin mythology; they resemble full-sized Kua-Tin and secrete acid from their fingers.[3|5]
Mordecai recalls that in his season, the doors to each new floor bore the Syndicate logo rather than a Kua-Tin carving.[3|30]}}
