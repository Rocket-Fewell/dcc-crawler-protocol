{{Quote|Thanks a lot Carl and Donut, you fucking assholes.|Quan Ch[2|24]}}
Quan Ch is an Earth Crawler and a Half-Elf Imperial Security Trooper, unaffiliated with the Royal Court. He is best known for surviving "The Fools Who Broke the Glass" on the Third Floor to claim one of Dungeon Crawler World's rare Celestial prizes: an enchanted robe that lets him fly on ethereal wings, his left hand glowing blue with its power.
==Description==
Quan Ch makes a poor first impression. Caught within the blast radius of a catastrophic failed spell, he publicly cursed out Carl and Donut in the group Quest chat rather than focusing on his own survival.[2|24] His outburst earned him a swift block from Donut.[2|Epilogue]
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=A colder, more self-serving side emerges on the Fourth Floor. Gifted enormous power he had done nothing to earn, Quan Ch pursued his own advancement heedless of the many fellow crawlers left in his wake.[3|15]}}
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=By the Fifth Floor the pattern was clear. For all Donut's cries of "cheater," Quan Ch never earned a single player-kill skull; he was a scavenger rather than a murderer, a vulture who preyed on the weak and the nearly dead to steal their kills, and who would cheerfully doom a bubble full of crawlers if a rich enough prize dangled in front of him.[4|31]}}
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=By the Eighth Floor any restraint was gone. The scavenger who had never taken a crawler's life had reinvented himself as a prolific killer of his own kind, one of the dungeon's deadliest hunters. Yet in his final moments a different man surfaced, one who begged for a keepsake from home and confessed he had become something that would shame the person he loved most.[6|56][6|58]}}
==Appearance==
Quan Ch is an Asian Half-Elf crawler. His signature look comes from the glowing garment he won on the Third Floor: when equipped, it lifts him off the ground, sprouts ethereal, wisp-like wings from his back, and sets his left hand glowing blue.[2|Epilogue]
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=After his clash with the Royal Court on the Fifth Floor, Quan Ch is missing his entire left arm, torn away at the shoulder.[4|31][4|32]}}
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=By the Sixth Floor the loss had left him pale and visibly sickly, and the arm had still not regrown.[5|8][5|52]}}
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Stripped bare in his last fight, Quan Ch was revealed to carry half of a tattoo on the stump of his lost arm: the upper half of a young girl's face, the rest presumably lost with the severed limb.[6|58]}}

==History==
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Before the Dungeon, Quan Ch was a champion at a competitive card game, a background that fed directly into his mastery of the dungeon's card battles.[6|18] He carried a plain, sentimental "For Daddy" ring from Earth as what appeared to be his only pre-collapse keepsake.[4|32]}}
==Story==
===Book 2===
On the Third Floor, Quan Ch was among the crawlers trapped inside the 45-square-kilometer blast radius of "The Fools Who Broke the Glass," the group quest Carl and Donut set off. In the newly created quest chat he fired off a single message blaming the pair for the coming explosion, and Donut blocked him from the chat.[2|24][2|Epilogue]
The blast turned out to be survivable. Quan Ch lived through it, though gravely injured, apparently when his own equipment turned against him, and dragged himself into a saferoom.[2|Epilogue] Because he had cleared the quest without truly earning it, he drew the "Bandit" achievement, whose rare double upgrade turned his promised Platinum Quest Box into a Celestial one. Of the 83 crawlers awarded a box, Quan Ch was the only one to open his before the Borant Corporation could appeal, and inside was a glowing robe that lifted him off the ground the instant he donned it.[2|Epilogue]
===Book 3===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=On the train-themed Fourth Floor, Quan Ch turned his jacket into a weapon, flying down the rail tunnels and blasting locomotives with blue lightning to farm their mantaur engineers for experience.[3|13] Each wrecked train knocked a line out of service, and before long his rampage had stranded crawlers across the railway with no way forward.[3|15]
His rise came at others' expense in more ways than one. He also cost Prepotente and Miriam Dom a hard-won province boss: with the beast disabled and ready to fall, Quan Ch zipped in, jolted it awake with a blue bolt, chipped it to half health, then fled the instant it swung at him.[3|Epilogue][4|4]}}
===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The Fifth Floor scattered the crawlers into isolated bubbles, and Quan Ch cleared his own early before drifting into idle mob-farming.[4|17] His path collided with the Royal Court during the world quest to save Orthrus, a two-headed dog whose death would have doomed a whole bubble of crawlers. Lured by the bounty on the dog, Quan Ch repeatedly dove in to finish it off, careless of who else died, and told Tran that bagging "that fucking cat" Donut would be a welcome bonus.[4|30][4|31]
What followed was the most direct clash of his crawl. When Carl and Donut ran him down, Quan Ch shot Carl in the back and triggered a rocket-like escape, but Carl caught his left arm as he launched and it tore off at the shoulder, the same limb that fired his signature blue attack. Quan Ch still slipped down a stairwell, now an open enemy of the Royal Court.[4|31][4|32]}}
===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Quan Ch's crawl quietly unraveled on the Sixth Floor. Stripped of his lightning arm and stuck at his old level, he dropped off the top ten and all but disappeared from the broadcast; across the entire floor he surfaced only once, killing a single Odious Creeper during a doomed world quest.[5|8][5|52]
He never appeared at the floor-ending Butcher's Masquerade; Carl guessed he had slipped down the stairs early, if he was still alive at all.[5|63][5|65]}}
===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Quan Ch came roaring back on the Eighth Floor, reversing his earlier slump and cutting a bloody path through his fellow crawlers.[6|56]
During the floor's brutal key hunt he cornered Imani, then forced the Royal Court and Sister Ines into a three-way T'Ghee Card battle. Drawing on his old card-game past, he swapped hands with Donut to stick her with Flee Cards, then cast Force Play to seize both stairwell keys, outplaying either opponent.[6|55][6|56]
The Royal Court ran him down at the Capitol exit and, in a final card battle, stripped Quan Ch of his celestial cloak, blinded him, and ground him down. He died before Carl could land the finishing blow, but not before asking for his "For Daddy" ring back and murmuring that "she'd be ashamed of what I've become." Carl looted his vast inventory and the cloak, kept the severed arm, and left him the ring.[6|58][6|60]}}
===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=In the aftermath, it emerged that Donut, and not Carl, had been credited with Quan Ch's kill.[7|2]}}

==Progression==
As of the Third Floor recap, Quan Ch was a level 15 Imperial Security Trooper.[2|Epilogue]
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Powered by his flying robe, Quan Ch shot up the rankings over the Fourth Floor. He re-entered the top ten at level 31, and by the floor's final recap he had reached level 38, holding the number-five slot with a 200,000 gold bounty, the highest level of any crawler on that list.[3|13][3|25]}}
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Quan Ch stayed the dungeon's highest-level crawler across the Fifth Floor, reaching level 43, then 45, then 48, yet he slid from fifth place down to tenth as he stopped advancing and Carl's rise carried him to the number-one spot.[4|4][4|17][4|34]
His edge came almost entirely from his Celestial-tier robe, which granted flight, a protective Shield, and the short-range blue energy attack (possibly the rare Disrupter spell) that became his trademark; Mordecai suspected it carried hidden stat boosts and further abilities, and by this floor Quan Ch had begun using a second, red-glowing attack as well. He wore three rings until Carl stripped them from the severed arm.[4|4][4|31][4|32]}}
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=On the Sixth Floor his class specialized from Imperial Security Trooper to Sergeant at Arms, but with his level frozen at 48 he was already slipping. He clung to the number-ten slot at the floor's start, then dropped off the top ten entirely.[5|8][5|52]}}
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Whatever slump had gripped him passed. Over the Eighth Floor Quan Ch surged to level 70, one above Carl, and clawed back into the top ten, now bearing over sixty player-kill skulls. His Sergeant at Arms class let him hoard the equipment of everyone he killed, leaving him a bloated inventory of magical weapons, including a bowler hat that could decapitate a target when thrown, and roughly five million gold by the time he fell.[6|56][6|58][6|60]}}
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Only after Quan Ch's death was the true nature of his celestial garment revealed. Named the Cloak of the Benevolent Champion, it was a Third-Floor Celestial item whose powers were custom-tailored to each wearer, weighted toward magic and toward raising the wearer's lowest base stat; in Quan Ch's hands it had granted flight, a short-range bolt attack, multiple shields, and a cloaking ability.[7|2]
The cloak also carried a level-five Find Weakened skill, meant to flag the ally in a group nearest to death so a protector would know whom to guard first. Quan Ch had turned it inside out, using it to hunt and pick off the weakest crawlers in a pack rather than shield them.[7|2]}}
==Trivia==
* The night Quan Ch opened his box, Borant patched out the upgrade achievement behind it, so his prize can no longer be earned the same way.[2|Epilogue]
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Quan Ch's prize garment is named inconsistently in the text: the newscast that introduced it calls it a glowing robe, while Carl and Donut refer to it as a jacket throughout the Fourth Floor.[2|Epilogue][3|13]}}
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=One of the rings Carl looted from Quan Ch's arm was Rockard's Ring of Sniping, whose Ripe and Loaded benefits mark low-health and gear-wearing targets on the map, mechanically explaining his reputation as a kill-stealer.[4|32]Another was a plain, unenchanted gold band engraved inside with "For Daddy."[4|32]}}
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=By the Sixth Floor, Quan Ch's mutilation had become his public image: a fan at CrawlCon cosplayed him as a one-armed crawler complete with a prosthetic, blood-squirting stump.[5|41]}}
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The celestial cloak Quan Ch had worn since the Third Floor passed to the Royal Court on his death.[6|58][6|63]}}
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Quan Ch's cloak held the distinction of being the very first Celestial item awarded in Earth's crawl.[7|2]Among the gear Carl stripped from Quan Ch's corpse was Jefferson, a sapient weapon shaped like a nickel-stuffed sock.[7|2]Donut recalled Quan Ch as having hailed from Vietnam, though the detail was never firmly pinned down.[7|2]}}
