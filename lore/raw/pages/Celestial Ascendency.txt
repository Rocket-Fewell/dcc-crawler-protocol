Celestial Ascendency is the divine contest staged in the Halls of the Ascendency, the first floor of the Dungeon where the otherwise invulnerable gods can be killed. The war god Grull is one of the few trueborn heirs to the competition.[3|32][3|33]

For the floor itself, including its geography and layout, see Twelfth Floor.

==Overview==
Away from the Twelfth Floor, gods sit at level 250 and are effectively immortal. They lose that invulnerability the moment they arrive, which is what makes the Ascendency possible at all.[3|32][3|33]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Ascendency is the defining feature of the Twelfth Floor: a contest in which the galaxy's most powerful elites compete through their sponsored deities for the Celestial Throne. It is less open warfare than a game of intrigue and chess, though violence is common, especially toward the end. Where the Ninth Floor is the playground of governments and the merely rich, the Twelfth is where the true elites play, and the sums involved are said to be beyond belief.[6|19]
The prize is a ten-season seat at the Syndicate crawl council table, plus a pension for sitting thirty cycles on any subcommittee. Most competitors have no need of the money and play for glory. The pacifist Nebulars are the exception: they enter season after season hoping to win a seat and shut the crawl down entirely.[6|19][6|64]
Of the thousands of gods in existence, only about a hundred are equipped to compete in a given season.[6|19] The Earth season is an outlier. Roughly 300 sign-ups end up trapped in-system alongside the Faction Wars leaders and the financiers of the Scolopendra Club, concentrating so much of the galaxy's wealth and power in one place that the Syndicate council debates triggering a failsafe to destroy the entire star system before the AI's influence can spread.[6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The Earth season's contest is not the traditional game. Once the crawlers reach the Twelfth Floor, the AI announces a one-hour countdown and declares that the winner will "truly" be a god, that the rules stand as written, and that winner takes all: the victor may attempt to kill the AI, rule in its name, or produce some other outcome entirely.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Prime Minister Victory]}}

==Rules and Mechanics==
On the lower floors, deities are invulnerable and can manifest only through a host vessel, summoned by celestial boon, indentured summoning, or involuntary trigger; for the full summoning rules, see Gods. God-summoning equipment is scattered through the dungeon partly so that wealthy sponsors and celebrities can buy guest appearances in the game without belonging to one of the dynastic families that control the Ninth Floor armies.[3|32]

Because a piloted god is worn rather than inhabited, spells designed to remove biological armor, such as Take That Shit Off or Laundry Day, can in theory strip the pilot away.[3|32]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The rivalry among Taranis, Emberus, and Emberus's twin Hellik over the Celestial Throne is itself an Ascendency storyline, and it reaches crawlers through Emberus's church quests, which gate progression toward the Twelfth Floor.[4|31][4|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Armor-removal spells do not first require defeating a deity's invulnerability, as crawlers had assumed. They target the pilot directly: stripping the Soul Armor instantly forces the deity back to the Halls of the Ascendency and leaves the exposed sponsor vulnerable.[5|74]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Competitors take part by sponsoring and piloting deities rather than appearing as themselves, though a sponsor may be visibly present and driving the god during a summoning, and may choose to manifest in their own true form instead of the deity's.[6|Prologue][6|17]
A rule unique to the Earth season lets each participating deity recruit a second, unsponsored god as a retainer once the games begin. Mordecai believes it is meant to even out the wildly varying power levels among the gods and to draw more players into the contest.[6|19]
Under normal circumstances the battles do not begin until the crawlers reach the Twelfth Floor or are zeroed out. Until then, sponsors maneuver through summoned deities, attendants, quests, and the re-ascension of dormant gods, and the remaining timing of the crawl becomes part of the strategic game.[6|Prologue][6|25][6|61]
Some of that maneuvering is invisible to oversight. AI subroutines do not regularly monitor NPC-to-NPC communications and are legally forbidden from monitoring Ascendency sponsor communications, which lets sponsored deities message crawlers privately, though a crawler's own replies can still be seen.[6|Prologue][6|17]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Sponsors may normally let a deity appear in the sponsor's regular form during certain appearances, with NPCs still perceiving the deity. Causing a deity to act outside its nature, however, breaks Faction Wars rules and counts as cheating.[7|55]
An emergency action item can rewrite the rules mid-season. Once invoked, players whose deities are summoned off the Twelfth Floor lose the option to abstain from piloting them, can no longer appear as themselves, and must let every sponsored deity be driven by its designated sponsor; attendance becomes mandatory, and active summons are corrected if the pilot had stayed behind.[7|61]
Some gods can claim the throne only if no mortals are present, which is why Eris's Ceremony of Chaos, a once-ever spell that locks mortal witnesses into an invulnerable Witness state, is normally reserved for the Twelfth Floor.[7|66][7|69]
If the Ascendency is considered finished and no crawlers remain, the system automatically triggers shutdown and hibernation, allowing elemental reclamation before it is awakened again.[7|69]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=A stripped pilot is not removed from the game. When Donut ejects Prince Maestro from Grull's soul armor on the Tenth Floor, the Maestro regenerates in his quarters on the Twelfth Floor and returns to the god minutes later, after which Grull withdraws to the Twelfth Floor.[8|66]
The AI has less control over Twelfth Floor "tourists" than it did over the lower floors. It claims that those driving gods stay protected even if ejected from their bodies and killed, but only while they remain within the four corners of the original dungeon enhancement zone.[8|92]
The battles begin once the crawlers reach the Twelfth Floor or the Scolopendra encounter on the Eleventh Floor is resolved. Level-collapse timers past the Eleventh Floor are then suspended until the Ascendency has a winner, and the dungeon collapses twelve hours after that winner is crowned. For the timer mechanics, see Twelfth Floor.[8|94][8|98]}}

==Terms==
Halls of the Ascendency is the divine staging area on the Twelfth Floor. Deities forced out of their manifested forms return there.[3|32]

Soul Armor is the physical or biological shell that off-world sponsors and other entities wear to pilot deities inside the dungeon; the aliens effectively wear the gods like clothing.[3|32]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Celestial Throne is the seat at the heart of the Ascendency, contested by the competing deities. Taranis, God of Thunder, serves as its regent.[4|31][4|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Butcher's Masquerade is a celestial peace-bonding sacrament created by Apito in the Halls of the Ascendency to force her vying children to assemble without violence. Breaching the seal opens the ground and plunges the violator into the Nothing.[5|64][5|66]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Celestial Attendant is a mortal NPC role on the Twelfth Floor. The boon freezes the recipient's progress: sponsorships and inventory lock, any party is left immediately, and the attendant becomes a temporary non-combatant employed until the games begin, at which point they may exit the dungeon or stay on. Attendants are usually treated as valuable protected NPCs, though that protection is not absolute and grows especially thin once the games start.[6|19]
Retainer is a second, unsponsored god recruited by a sponsored deity, a rule in force only for the Earth season.[6|19]
Re-ascension is the process by which forgotten or diminished former gods return to the Halls. Several deities re-ascend during the Eighth Floor as crawlers repair shrines and complete ancient divine storylines, most notably in a Celestial Thorn Room, a rare chamber in which only one of several ruined shrines may be restored.[6|15][6|17][6|25][6|36]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Outside Intelligence, or OI, is an abandoned AI system or similar intelligence able to compete in the altered Earth-season Ascendency if it can reach the playing field. The AI warns that some are bent on revenge.[8|89][8|Epilogue Interlude: Prime Minister Victory]
Subjugated deity describes a former god reduced to a mortal, subjugated state. Lamashtu's level is cut in half under Ascendency rules once she loses her worshippers, and would be halved again if her subjugator were subjugated rather than killed.[8|92]}}

==Notable Competitors==
Deities and sponsors are listed here only by their direct relevance to the Ascendency; see the individual pages for full histories.

* Grull, God of War and child of Taranis and Apito, is one of the few trueborn heirs to the competition.[3|33]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Taranis, God of Thunder, is regent of the Celestial Throne.[4|32]
Emberus is a major fire deity, a candidate for the throne, and the enemy of his twin Hellik.[4|28][4|31]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Diwata is the deity whose sponsor, Circe Took, is stripped from her Soul Armor on the Sixth Floor, the first demonstration that a pilot can be exposed long before the Ascendency proper.[5|74]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Huanxin Jinx, a Grixist heiress and CEO of Icon Industries, sponsors Eileithyia. She has played since childhood without winning, once served a multi-season suspension for illegal bribery, and intends to use Eileithyia, with help from Carl, Katia, and Donut, to claim the Celestial Throne.[6|17][6|19]
Eileithyia, the level 250 goddess of Childbirth and Female Pain, is piloted by Huanxin Jinx and takes the re-ascended Yemaya as her retainer.[6|15][6|17][6|19]
Nekhebit, an ancient vulture goddess, is sponsored by Odette. She is an independent sponsored goddess and will have a retainer of her own.[6|36][6|38]
Ysalte is sponsored by Pontifex Shine of the Nebular Balance and attempts to take Amayon, a prince of Sheol, as her retainer.[6|58][6|64][6|66]
Dodola, a storm goddess formerly sponsored by Huanxin Jinx, was killed in an earlier season, eliminating Huanxin from that Ascendency and seeding her conflict with Odette.[6|Prologue][6|37]
Prince Maestro is the sponsor piloting Grull.[6|72]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Eris, Goddess of Chaos and half-sister of Taranis, is a prime contender. After her sponsor Nami is stripped from her Soul Armor and killed, she is left unsponsored and cannot be sponsored again during the season.[7|66][7|76]
Theia, a goddess of light, is normally a poor bet for the throne but is sponsored in the Earth season by Rectrix, a skyfowl in the employ of the Open Intellect Pacifist network.[7|49][7|55]
Khepri, God of Rebirth and the Sunrise, is a locked, unsponsored light god who becomes a retainer of Adrasteia after Khnum's death. Adrasteia is described as having an outside shot at the throne.[7|49][7|53]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Chalchiuhtlicue, nicknamed Chachi, is a little-known goddess who is not usually sponsored and whose broad powers make her a comparatively weak contender.[8|51][8|53]
Lamashtu appears on the Eleventh Floor as a mortal, subjugated former deity at level 125.[8|92]
Apito remains absent, though Eris refers to multiple "pieces" of her being present and warns that she will be angry when she fully returns from the dead.[8|97]}}

==History==
===Book 3===
During the battle of Trainyard E on the Fourth Floor, Carl initiates the involuntary summoning sequence of Grull, demonstrating that even an invulnerable deity on a lower floor can be neutralized by manipulating the summoning rules.[3|32][3|33]

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=On the Sixth Floor, Princess Donut uses a heavily boosted level 15 Laundry Day to strip Diwata's Soul Armor from Circe Took. Diwata is forced back to the Halls of the Ascendency and Took is left exposed and killed, revealing a major vulnerability in deity piloting well before the Ascendency itself begins.[5|74]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Huanxin Jinx, piloting Eileithyia, brokers a deal to turn Katia into a Celestial Attendant on the Twelfth Floor in exchange for help with her Ascendency game, and asks Carl's group to repair Yemaya's shrine in a Celestial Thorn Room so that Yemaya can re-ascend and serve as Eileithyia's retainer.[6|15][6|17][6|19]
Odette puts her show on hiatus to sponsor Nekhebit and compete herself. Her entry is not only about glory: she means to deny Huanxin the upper hand, and privately frames her deeper aim as bringing the crawl to a controlled end so future seasons need not repeat its cruelties. The rivalry between the two dates to an earlier season, when Odette manipulated a summoning that killed Dodola and knocked Huanxin out of the game.[6|Prologue][6|36][6|37]
During the Eighth Floor's demon crisis, Ysalte tries to enslave Amayon and bring him along as her retainer. Carl's group frees him, costing Ysalte's sponsor his intended advantage. Katia keeps the reusable Bolt of Ophiotaurus, a benefactor-box gift originally meant to kill Prince Maestro while he pilots Grull, spending it on Ysalte instead; because the bolt survives intact, the option to use it on Maestro stays open.[6|64][6|66][6|72]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=During a Ninth Floor adjudication, Team Retribution establishes that rival players were relaying real-time information to a third party competing in the Ascendency, and passes an emergency action item cutting off outside live feeds for everyone inside the dungeon, including the Ascendency players on the Twelfth Floor. When off-world teams evade the ban through renamed studio programs, Carl closes the loophole with a second action item. At the warlord council, Odette appears without her usual gear and confirms that she has onboarded into the Ascendency, where she is playing Nekhebit.[7|19][7|43]
A later action item goes further, stripping players of the choice to abstain from piloting, the choice to appear as themselves, and the ability to hand a sponsored deity to anyone but its designated sponsor.[7|61]
The Ninth Floor god crisis then drags Ascendency business onto a floor never meant to host it. Khepri manifests inside his own temple as a locked, unsponsored deity; Harpocrates and Meatus resume a feud normally reserved for the Ascendency battles; Eris burns her Ceremony of Chaos early; and the War Mage Rebellion strips her sponsor Nami from her Soul Armor, killing him and leaving Eris unsponsored for the rest of the season.[7|53][7|60][7|66][7|76]
Near the end of the floor, the Sledge casts Zerzura and transfers Larracos and Shanty Town to the Twelfth Floor's Court of the Ascendency. Shortly afterward, Justice Light's trap breaks the in-game Nothing, which drains into the Halls of the Ascendency, Sheol, and Scolopendra's Lair, and Scolopendra awakens.[7|85][7|87]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=On the Tenth Floor, Eris appears to Carl as a level 251 unsponsored deity summoned by error. She says she wants the throne and offers to send Carl and his friends to the Pineapple Cabaret, to the surface, or even to a rebuilt Earth if she wins.[8|35]
Lucia, speaking under the influence of Chalchiuhtlicue, warns that the veins are open and that the real, the imagined, and the enslaved are all gathering for the prize, and that the Ascendency is no longer the game Carl was told it was.[8|51]
At the climax of the Eleventh Floor, the crawlers summon Scolopendra during the Parade of Horribles. Carl transforms her rather than killing her, and the system treats the encounter as won: collapse timers past the Eleventh Floor are suspended until the Ascendency produces a winner. Taranis intervenes during the fight, drives off Khepri, and leaves the realm with Samantha and Krakaren.[8|94][8|97]
Carl notes that the gods' invulnerability runs out after the Eleventh Floor, and resolves to remain on the Twelfth Floor and stop anyone from winning until every surviving crawler has escaped the dungeon.[8|98]
After the crawlers enter the Twelfth Floor, the AI announces a one-hour countdown, declares that the winner will become truly godlike under rules that stand as written, and adds that abandoned AI systems may enter as Outside Intelligences. The broadcast of possible contenders reaches well beyond sponsored deities, listing Taranis, Eris, Odette as Nekhebit, Donut, Li Na, Carl, Prepotente, Elle, Lucia Mar, Agatha, Juice Box, Akuma, Samantha, and demons among others.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Prime Minister Victory]}}
