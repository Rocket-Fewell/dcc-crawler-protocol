The Inevitable Ruin is a curse Juice Box describes as being inflicted on the mind of a Hunter Wraith, which shows the wraith a painful, unwanted truth about anyone it touches or possesses. It is not to be confused with the broader concept of the same name, with This Inevitable Ruin, the title of Book 7, or with This Inevitable Ruin (Nine-Tier Attack)," the name of the third of Scolopendra's prophesied nine-tier attacks.

== Description ==

Per Juice Box, the curse is native to Hunter Wraiths, monsters found on the tenth and eleventh floors, and is the reason the species is so bitter.[7|82] When a Hunter Wraith possesses or touches someone, the curse activates: it "ruins something pure," not by changing anything about the person, but by forcing the wraith to see the unvarnished truth about them. Juice Box describes it as difficult to explain and something that manifests differently depending on the circumstances.[7|82]

Carl rejects the curse's fatalistic framing, arguing that what it reveals is really just another form of hopelessness rather than an unavoidable truth.[7|82]

== Story ==

=== Book 7 ===

To rescue Louis after he crash-lands and becomes trapped inside a coffin-sized cell in the Reaver castle, Juice Box, a changeling, transforms herself into a Hunter Wraith so she can slip through the walls undetected. Tipid warns that turning into that particular wraith won't be a clean getaway, since something happens when it touches people, though he notes it's more of a problem for Juice Box than for Louis.[7|79][7|80]

Afterward, while tending to the recovering Louis, Juice Box tells Carl she wishes she hadn't taken the Hunter Wraith's form, even though it was worth it to save him. She explains that the curse, the Inevitable Ruin, showed her the truth while she carried him: that Louis is terrified of her and doesn't actually love her, and that he doesn't trust her feelings and never will.[7|82] Carl argues against the curse's fatalism, telling her nothing is truly hopeless until someone is dead.[7|82]
