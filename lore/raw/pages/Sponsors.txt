{{Quote|But ultimately, it's all about the patrons.|Mordecai[1|4]}}

Sponsors, also called patrons or benefactors, are the Syndicate organizations and individuals who bankroll a crawler by buying loot boxes on that crawler's behalf. The arrangement is advertising rather than charity: the Borant Corporation takes a commission on every credit a patron spends.[1|4]

==Description==
Patrons are usually organizations rather than individuals, and they watch for crawlers accumulating favorites, since association with a popular crawler is advertising. They are the fourth figure tracked on the Ratings screen of the crawler interface.[1|4] A separate market, deity sponsorship, backs one of the gods rather than a crawler.[1|44]

==Acquiring a Patron==
Patronage rules are set per season, and Borant announced the current terms at the opening of the second floor.[1|31] Every crawler is limited to three patrons; all three spots are auctioned for one day upon the induction of the fourth, fifth, and sixth floors; benefactors may transfer their patronage at their own discretion from the seventh floor onward; and bidding opens at one credit per spot, so virtually every surviving crawler ends the auction with patrons. Bids above the standard patronage fee return to the patron as loot box discounts, and bids below it are charged back as a surcharge on boxes.[1|31]

Sponsors historically wait for the fourth floor, since race and class selection on the third floor can gut a crawler's popularity overnight.[1|29] Early bids may be placed from the start of that floor[2|7], and a mid-floor patch introduced an unexplained mechanism called collective bidding.[2|13]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The first auction opens on arrival at the fourth floor, with a 45-hour window announced against the crawler's crawler ID.[3|1] A Sponsorship tab is added to the crawler interface and populates when bidding closes, listing a crawler's sponsors, the organizations they represent, and any other crawlers they sponsor.[3|8] Communication runs one way: a crawler may message a sponsor from a notes section inside the tab[3|13], but a sponsor cannot reply.[3|8] The award arrives as an admin notice naming the sponsor.[3|11]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The second round opens the same way on arrival at the fifth floor.[4|1] Because sponsors watch the feed, a crawler who wants something specific can lobby for it aloud.[4|2] Client counts vary enormously, from roughly 500 crawlers at once down to a single one.[4|7] A group opposed to the crawl may buy a slot purely for the advertising and never send a box at all.[4|8]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The third and final round opens on arrival at the sixth floor[5|1] and sets a bidding record.[5|21] A patron may not speak to their crawler directly, which would breach the sponsorship contract.[5|33]
A sponsorship can also end outside the auction cycle. When the Valtay Corporation acquired a controlling stake in the company running the crawl, the resulting conflict of interest voided its sponsorship immediately and triggered a special 60-hour replacement auction, with all previously awarded prizes retained.[5|19] That auction then had to be rerun, because the winning bidder could not pay.[5|40]
From the seventh floor onward, sponsors may sell their stakes outright, and Borant warns crawlers that an inactive or stingy sponsor is probably holding the slot in order to sell it.[5|21] A showrunner may also contest a gift, though one such appeal was denied.[5|63]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The transfer right is exercised without consulting the crawler: Donut learned that her patron had sold her sponsorship on to another party from a notification that arrived in the middle of a card battle.[6|29] Valtay rules require a liaison to attend any occasion on which a crawler might be alone with a sponsor[6|12], including media appearances on programs a sponsor owns.[6|7]
A slot can be frozen rather than reauctioned. When one of Carl's patrons was declared a fugitive organization and its assets seized, the slot was frozen with them.[6|51]}}

==Benefactor Boxes==
Patrons sponsor a crawler chiefly by buying boxes for them. Each box type comes in six quality tiers: bronze, silver, gold, platinum, legendary, and celestial, and most patrons can afford only silver or gold. Only a patron can send a Benefactor Box, which holds the rarest items in the game, including technology from the patron's home world that the Dungeon itself will never generate.[1|4]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Boxes arrive branded with the sponsor's mark and slogan, and delivery is not instant; when the entire dungeon acquired sponsors at once, fulfillment backed up, and Borant gave priority to boxes carrying an emergency delivery fee.[3|13] Viewers watching a sponsored crawler's feed periodically see commercials produced by that sponsor.[3|11]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=A sufficiently wealthy patron may commission a unique item built around a specific crawler's fighting style and inscribed with the sponsor's name.[4|34] Borant also brokers product placement, under which a sponsor sends a product sample dressed up as an ordinary benefactor box, free of charge to the sponsor. The crawler is expected to interact with the product repeatedly, refrain from disparaging it, and appear on a program discussing it[4|7], and the sponsor must build a self-destruct into the sample so the crawler cannot reach its internal components.[4|15]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Sponsors routinely attach a restriction binding an item to its recipient, which Mordecai describes as standard practice to stop other crawlers from murdering the recipient for the gear.[5|60] Written notes may accompany a box; notes are billed by the character, and anything insufficiently vague is translated into code by the system before it arrives.[5|55]
A patron may also route a gift to a crawler it does not back. Five Apothecary boxes reached five different clients, each carrying a note instructing the recipient to hand the contents to Donut.[5|63]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=A liaison can seize a Benefactor Box in transit, though one such seizure arrived overridden by Syndicate rule.[6|48] Loot recovered by following a sponsor's directions belongs to the crawler, on the settled caselaw of Grixist Swarm v. Syndicate Council, though communication devices and neural enhancers acquired as found loot are carved out.[6|51]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Sponsors buy access to gods as well as gear. Prepotente worships the Midnight Epicure because his sponsors sent him the ability to do so in a benefactor box, the same sort of gift the Skull Empire once used to point Maggie My at a deity of their choosing.[8|70]}}

==Patron Economy==
Patronage is the dungeon's principal revenue stream. Borant's income breaks down into mining proceeds, advertising, a stipend from the Syndicate government, and a commission on every credit a patron spends, and Mordecai states that the mining operation is a pretext.[1|4] None of that money arrives until the season closes.[1|36]

The box tax lands on the host rather than the patron. A showrunner owes the Syndicate on every non-sponsored box it hands out, with only a small seasonal allowance of free celestial boxes before the bill starts climbing, while a sponsored box is the patron's expense.[2|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Sponsorship revenue acts as a brake on the dungeon's lethality. When crawlers found an escape that risked summoning extinction-level deities, Borant permitted the exploit but blocked the worst of the summonings, citing the floor of sponsorship bidding still to come.[4|33] The System AI's formal duty is to monitor prohibited speech against the sponsors.[4|28]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=A patron's interest is not always the crawler's survival. The last entry Everly left in the cookbook concludes that her own sponsor had been steering her toward a death that would be watched and remembered.[5|55]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Syndicate pays enormous sums in advance for use of the tunnel system that carries the broadcast, none of which was refunded when an entire floor was skipped. Mordecai reads that skipped floor as a maneuver engineered to profit the Apothecary and the Plenty at the expense of the Valtay Corporation and the Syndicate as a whole.[6|2]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The tunnel network itself was built by the Plenty, who alone can shut it down, so the entire broadcast runs on infrastructure owned by one of the sponsors.[8|28]}}

==Deity Sponsorship==
For the deities themselves, see Gods.

A second market sells sponsorship of a deity rather than support of a crawler, and it can be aimed at crawlers rather than at helping them. Zev notes that a foreign government angered by a crawler can do very little to them before the sixth floor, unless that government springs for a deity sponsorship.[1|44] King Rust of the Skull Empire spends heavily on one before the fourth floor opens, and Odette suspects it is aimed squarely at Carl.[2|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The purchase buys control of the god and a seat in the Celestial Ascendency rather than mere association, and a sponsor is named on the broadcast whenever their god appears.[3|32][3|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=A deity sponsorship is bought per season rather than held permanently.[4|31] Worshipping a sponsored god means answering to that god's sponsor: the cookbook records a crawler who took a goddess as his patron only to find that the alien who had paid for her treated the worship as directed at himself.[4|30]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=A sponsored god's aura displays the sponsor's name, with a green dot beside it when the sponsor is present and actively driving the god, and the System AI is legally forbidden from monitoring Ascendency sponsor communications.[6|Prologue] Cheating on the sponsor's side is punished separately: one sponsor who took a bribe drew a multi-season suspension and a permanent ban from driving Dodola again.[6|37] Some deities act as patrons of performers rather than of crawlers, and Ysalte is one of the few who will sponsor a bard, asking nothing in return.[6|66]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=A sponsorship constrains the deity's personality, and only an unsponsored god shows its true nature. A deity whose sponsor is removed cannot be sponsored again for the remainder of the season.[7|76] Making a god act against that nature is explicitly cheating under Faction Wars rules[7|55], and a conscription potion strong enough to press a god into a mortal army will not work on a sponsored one.[7|69]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Catchall deities with broad but weak portfolios are rarely sponsored, since they fare poorly in the Ascendency[8|53], though a god passed over at the start of a season can still be bought later.[8|41]}}

==Trivia==
*Mordecai accumulated nearly 30 million followers and four patrons during his own crawl, and credits that support as the only reason he survived.[1|4]
*Odette's five sponsors were a record at the time of her crawl.[1|29]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Donut holds the record for the highest sponsorship bid in the history of the series, beating out both Carl and Prepotente.[4|7]}}

==Speculation==
*Collective bidding may be pooled patronage. Patrons are capped at three per crawler[1|31], and a mid-floor patch introduced an unexplained collective bidding mechanism.[2|13] A system letting several parties combine funds behind one spot would let small backers reach popular crawlers without raising the cap.

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The Apothecary may have bought the leaderboard to lock rivals out. A single backer took the third slot on nine of the strongest crawlers in one round[5|16] and coordinated its gifts with a second sponsor[5|43], cornering the highest-value feeds among the likely survivors.}}
