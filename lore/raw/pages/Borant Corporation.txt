A Kua-Tin megacorporation operating within the Syndicate, the Borant Corporation (aka Borant) is the current planetary regent of Earth and the producer of Dungeon Crawler World: Earth.[1|1][1|2]

:See Also: Bloom • Borant Headquarters • Borant System

== Description ==
The Borant Corporation is based in the Borant System and principally owned by the Kua-Tin, though its workforce also includes Syndicate Humans and other alien stock.[1|4][6|Epilogue] The company is closely tied to the Borant System's governing party, the Bloom.[1|36]

Although Borant is technically a mining concern dealing in rare elements (such as primal elements), its real income comes from the Syndicate stipend together with the advertising, licensing, broadcasting, and patron-commission proceeds of Dungeon Crawler World, which the company is chosen to host roughly once every 7–15 seasons.[1|4][5|33]

=== Dungeon Crawler World ===
Known for "making the most elaborate, most entertaining dungeons," Borant invests heavily in preparing each dungeon and tailoring it to the world it takes place on. At any given time, five forward teams are deployed to candidate planets, where they study the locals and — when needed — quietly influence local pop culture so the inhabitants will understand the game and its notifications when the time comes.[1|4][1|3] The first prep team arrived on Earth in 1937.[1|4] As showrunner, Borant controls the dungeon's mobs and the distribution of experience and loot, though the System AI remains the ultimate arbiter.[2|21] The corporation also manufactures and owns the dungeon's genetically engineered biological NPCs, rewriting their memories to fit a different storyline each season.[2|3]

Where other companies prefer to use holos for Tutorials and Dungeon Admins, Borant takes a more "fins-on" approach, teleporting live staff and NPCs directly into the Dungeon.[1|36]

== Departments ==
=== Communications Department ===
The Communications Department, which includes Zev, serves as a PR agency and liaison between privately owned programs and crawlers. While certain production companies hold rights to interview crawlers between Floors, requests to pull a crawler mid-Floor are processed by Communications. When a crawler receives too many requests, their party is entered into the Crawler Assisted Outreach Program and assigned an agent with full authority to sign legal documents on their behalf.[1|36][2|11]

=== Engineering ===
Engineers design the Dungeon's Floors and occasionally appear on talk shows to discuss the different levels.[2|Epilogue][5|Epilogue]
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Cascadia, the lead engineer of Dungeon Crawler World: Earth, is also its executive producer and appears to oversee most of the other departments.[6|21]}}

=== Dungeon Administration ===
Though never officially named in the books, this area likely encompasses the various personnel working on and inside the Dungeon. Employees here may work in Site Prep or serve as Location Managers.[1|35][1|36]

== Story ==
=== Book 1 ===
At the Bloom's insistence, Borant opens Earth's Dungeon two years (or possibly two cycles) early in an attempt to stave off bankruptcy and a seizure action by the Valtay.[1|2] Borant receives no income until after the crawl ends, so the Bloom also pushes to wrap the crawl up as soon as enough money is raised to clear the company's debts.[1|36]

As a result, Borant cuts corners and introduces "bugs" that accelerate crawler deaths, such as the exploding-and-suctioning toilet issues and overpowered mobs on the First and Second Floors.[1|20] The Syndicate Council subcommittee that oversees the games puts Borant on notice during the Second Floor, and employees within the company begin pushing back against the Bloom's efforts to end the game early.[1|36]

When Carl appears on Death Watch Extreme Dungeon Mayhem and humiliates the Maestro, Borant actually commends Carl for showing "proper respect to the organization."[1|44] Because the show is owned by the prince of the Skull Empire — allied with a faction at odds with Borant — disrespecting it amounted to respecting Borant.[1|44]

=== Book 2 ===
Mordecai recalls that the last season Borant hosted set its Fourth Floor inside a giant living creature; the floor ended when a bacterial infection spread, the walls collapsed, and the level unintentionally filled with gore. It was not popular with viewers.[2|14]

On the Third Floor, the Fools Who Broke the Glass Quest climaxes with two waves of magic — the first rendering magical items inert, the second activating magical weapons. Carl had rigged a controlled explosion triggered by a magical Hobgoblin Detonator, and the System AI ruled the detonator exempt from both pulses. Carl's plan worked, saving thousands of NPCs and dozens of crawlers, and the AI distributed Celestial Loot Boxes to every crawler in the blast zone. Borant appealed the exemption ruling and lost, then appealed the prize decision and was ultimately forced to burn its once-per-season veto to cancel the boxes — had they gone out, the entire Borant System would never have financially recovered.[2|Epilogue] In their place, the AI handed out the Cuck Aquaman Achievement to everyone in the blast radius, which proved almost as costly.[3|2]

=== Book 3 ===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=There is no denying that Borant has taken the cheapest possible approach to the crawl. The robot Mexx-6000 bluntly describes the whole production as slapped together "with spit and duct tape" from interstellar swap-meet parts, built with little regard for system security and surviving on sheer dumb luck.[3|20]
Employee Protections were meant to begin at the end of the Third Floor, when a "pocket system" would replace the dual-layer enhancement zone. As of the Fourth Floor the dual-layer system is still in place; most assume Borant is simply being cheap, but the company claims Hinter refuses to rent it the necessary cores. Meanwhile, unrest stirs in the Borant System and non-Party employees face worse persecution than ever.[3|25]
When Carl is pulled into the Death Challenge, the System AI — acting on Borant's behalf — set his odds of survival at an abysmal 50:1. Viewers bet heavily in Carl's favor, Carl survived, and Borant was left on the hook. Borant appealed the AI's odds-setting, accusing it of reacting "sluggishly"; the outcome is unclear, but the company likely lost a great deal of money on the bet.[3|Epilogue]}}

=== Book 4 ===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Control of the corporation passes from its board of directors to a politburo.[4|34]
By now Carl and Princess Donut have risen to become the dungeon's top crawlers, repeatedly breaking the game and costing Borant staggering sums — enough that the new politburo sends a pointed warning against game-breaking antics that harm the show's sponsors.[4|34][5|1]}}

=== Book 5 ===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=It is rumored that Borant, strapped for cash, ran Earth's dungeon on a damaged, "used" macro AI bought under the table from the Mantises — a corner-cutting choice widely blamed for the system going "primal" unusually early.[5|53]
Midway through the season, a Syndicate court ruling grants the Valtay Corporation a 51% ownership stake in the Borant Corporation, legally severing it from the failing Borant System government.[5|19][5|21] In the aftermath, the Borant System itself erupts into a bloody civil war.[5|21][7|43]
With management tightening, Zev and the rest of Borant's administration team are reduced to running the crawl from a submerged production vessel parked on the floor of Earth's ocean.[5|30]}}

=== Book 6 ===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The dungeon's System AI formally "goes primal," seizing control from Borant and circumventing the company's rules; a liaison must negotiate a settlement just to keep the broadcast running on the corporation's terms.[6|48]
With the planet sealed under a Syndicate quarantine, Borant finds itself trapped in-system alongside everyone else, its continued "control" of the crawl reduced to little more than theater while the primal AI effectively runs the show.[6|21][6|Epilogue]}}

== Pop Culture ==
Across the broader galaxy, Borant's Kua-Tin are derogatorily called "mudskippers."[1|29] The corporation once tried to have the term classified as hate speech by the Syndicate, but the effort only drew attention to the slur and popularized it further.[1|29]

== Trivia ==
* Borant has a notorious reputation for being cheap and "nickel and diming everything."[1|42]
* The corporation forbids crawlers from disparaging Borant or the Syndicate on camera; violations can result in an AI-"accelerated" death.[1|4]
