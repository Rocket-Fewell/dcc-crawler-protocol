{{Quote|Larracos is the capital city of the disputed lands.|Igor[3|9]}}

Larracos is the capital city of the disputed lands on the Ninth Floor, and the prize over which the factions of Faction Wars fight.[3|9]

==Description==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Larracos is a living city rather than a set of blocks built to be knocked over. Its rings hold theaters, museums, galleries, temples, stores, and colleges full of bright-eyed NPC students, and the NPCs who fill them have lives and purpose rather than parts to play.[4|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The system greets arriving crawlers with a title the city has more than earned, the City of Dreams[7|1]; once the siege begins, that greeting becomes the City of Broken Dreams.[7|70] The architecture is heavy gothic stone, all columns, arches, gargoyles, and vertical lines climbing into vine-covered gables, ancient and weathered but plainly lived in, and it coarsens as the rings descend.[7|8] The Larracos College of Magecraft campus rises as a cluster of alabaster medieval towers holding an observatory and a lecture hall roofed in stained glass.[7|7]}}

==Layout==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The city is shaped like an inverted cone, dug deep into the ground rather than spread across it, descending layer after layer to an inverted castle at the bottom of the well.[4|24] Its diameter is modest, smaller than several cities on the Hunting Grounds.[4|33] The upper levels hold NPC residences[4|34]; deeper down are the pleasure districts where visiting aliens congregate, home to the Desperado Club, the brothels, and the trade in weapons and hired mercenaries[4|33], with the mercenary market and the faction markets sitting deep in the city near the castle.[4|24] A shanty town huddles outside the walls but is not considered part of the city.[4|24]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The rings
The pit is sunk into flat ground rather than carved into the volcano wall, a hole the Semeru dug themselves, its lip rising only three or four hundred feet above the topmost ring. Each level is a wide ring around a yawning central gap that narrows as it descends until it vanishes into a wall of smoke. The Semeru count sixty-six levels, numbered from the bottom up, though not every ring completes a full circle. A single waterfall pours in from one side and steps down from ring to ring, and ramps curl between levels like freeway off-ramps, every other one mined with explosives.[7|8] That waterfall is fed by the region's only river, which doubles as a territorial border[7|56]: it runs out of the Skull Empire's sector, curves through the territory beside it, and crosses Shanty Town before plunging into the funnel.[7|14]
Level 66, the topmost ring: barracks, soldiers, and the aqueduct infrastructure.[7|71]Level 50: the Larracos College of Magecraft campus[7|7], which takes up a third of the ring and shares it with the uppermost of three cultural districts.[7|8]Level 9: the Piccadilly, the city's largest theater, sitting directly beneath the waterfall.[7|8]Level 7: the weapon shops, magic shops, and mercenary markets, all ruined.[7|8]Level 4: the Desperado Club, a squat modernist concrete block on pillars with a neon sign, wildly out of place among the surrounding stonework.[7|8]
The flooding reached level 39 before draining to 29, rose again to 15 while Juice Box had the dwarves keep the drains blocked, and stood at level 3 by the time the crawlers arrived, still deep enough to submerge the castle's highest tower.[7|8]
The castle
The castle at the bottom of the well was raised as a capstone against any further digging toward the gods, built of living wood and later encased in carved stone inlaid with gemstones. By the time crawlers reach it the wood has long since rotted out, the roof is gone, and the walls carry scarring where tree roots once climbed them. A statue stood at the entrance[7|78]: the city's goddess, pickaxe over her shoulder.[7|85]
Defenses
More than a hundred automatic towers are seeded through the rings, each keyed to a colored gem.[7|71] Red towers fire lances of flame, and a tower can be upgraded up to five times, splitting the upgrades between range and rate of fire.[7|70] Green towers throw poison splashes, black towers deliver gale strikes that punch clean through stone, yellow towers lob globs of hobbling goo, and once a tower locks onto a target with Inexorable it will not stop firing until one of the two is paste.[7|71] Separate deflector towers turn gods aside rather than shooting at them.[7|70] The whole network answers to whoever holds the throne room, with a half-hour grace period whenever it changes hands.[7|1]
The Larracos Defense Force is understood to hold the subterranean passages beneath the city.[7|56] One of the floor's two Club Vanquisher entrances sits in Larracos and is almost always destroyed in the fighting, while the other, outside the city, is hidden inside whichever temple the season has assigned it, this season a plain temple of the Dagda tucked among the shacks of Shanty Town.[7|50] Shanty Town itself rings the top entrance, a settlement of shacks and markets that sprang up on its own after the flooding and grew large enough to count as a settlement in its own right[7|9], far larger than those of previous seasons.[7|14] It has no throne room and therefore no way to take it by occupation, though anyone entering it exposes themselves to the city's towers.[7|29]}}

==History==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=By Priestly's account, the Semeru dug their city downward in an attempt to reach the Celestials who dwell below rather than above, and it was that digging that woke Scolopendra at the base of the volcano.[4|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The city's faith belongs to a single deity, Ysalte, who keeps her one and only temple in the entire Dungeon here; to the dwarves she is the Downward Spiral, and everywhere else she is known as the Vinegar Bitch.[6|64] Its earliest citizens worshipped her, and she takes no worshippers beyond the city.[6|66] She did not live to see the season's end[6|72], which left her city with a dead god and, since she had never shared her worship beyond its walls, no substitute.[6|66]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=By the popular story, the land was once flat and verdant, and the Semeru who farmed and mined it began digging toward the gods beneath their feet because they loved their goddess and wished to know her. They burrowed through solid rock and stopped only when they struck the roots of the All Tree. A city grew in the pit as scholars arrived from everywhere, some to help and some to beg them to stop, and Larracos became a wealthy cultural capital. At the bottom the dwarves built their castle, less a fortress than a monument and an apology, raised once they understood that some things are better left asleep.[7|8]
The Semeru had numbered in the millions before the attack that drowned the city; fewer than ten thousand remain. The structures inside the pit were untouched, and the defeated survivors appointed themselves stewards of the empty city, which in time filled again as new scholars moved into the college and found the old research intact.[7|8]}}

==Story==
===Book 3===
Visiting the casino of the Desperado Club for the first time, Carl notices a guarded spiral stairwell labeled Larracos and asks what it is. A crocodilian guard named Igor explains that it is the capital of the disputed lands and the prize the factions fight over, and that the fighting is mostly good-natured for now: the armies are forbidden from attacking one another until the crawlers reach the ninth floor, so until then the serious commanders drill their units, everybody else parties, and the Desperado Club is where they come to unwind.[3|9]

===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Faction leaders have no access to the crawlers' marketplace interface, so they must physically travel into Larracos to buy or bid on gear from NPC proprietors[4|3] and fill out their armies at the mercenary market deep in the city near the castle.[4|24] That window closes the moment the crawlers arrive: everyone is expelled from the city and the fighting begins[4|3], and by the time a season ends no building is left standing and no NPC is left alive.[4|33]
At the end of the fifth floor, Katia opened the Gate of the Feral Gods directly into the faction market and fed timed charges, a chum bomb, concierge sharks, and pain amplifier jellies through it along with a draining ocean. The bombs leveled the market, and the water poured down the inverted cone faster than the drainage could carry it off, submerging more than half the city and sparing only the upper residences. Tens of thousands died, and the loss could not be undone: mobs can be added to a finished level but worker NPCs cannot, so the drowned shopkeepers, merchants, and mercenaries were gone for good.[4|34]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The city still had not drained; something kept clogging the drains, and the sharks were not helping. Outworlders were left stranded on the Larracos level of the Desperado Club, comfortable enough with a hotel and casino to hand, while the weapons brokers' trade with the hunters stopped entirely. Crawlers are barred outright: the secret stairwell hidden in the club's restrooms continues down toward the Larracos level, but any attempt to take it is refused. The flooding also cost Faction Wars a team, the Dreadnought Clan quitting after their war chief's wife was stung by a jellyfish.[5|58]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The flooding remained a live problem well into the eighth floor. The boardroom where the faction warlords normally meet is inside Larracos and was still unusable, forcing preproduction meetings to be held remotely[6|21], and Empress D'Nadia later spent an emergency action item on a proposal to delete the city's remaining NPCs outright, which the system rejected.[6|35]
Juice Box had meanwhile turned the surviving NPCs into a problem of their own, assassinating warlords and their representatives during those meetings to win the unaffiliated NPCs recognition as a tenth team.[6|22] The Semeru had stopped being caretakers and started being wardens, letting no one near the flooded part of the city, keeping the castle submerged, and feeding mercenaries and faction players alike into the pit. When a demon destroyed the Desperado Club, the outworlders who had been sitting out the season on its bottom floor were ejected into the drowned city, where thirty-five were devoured and the rest pulled their failsafes.[6|35]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The crawlers arrived to find the lower rings freshly drained, dead concierge sharks still lying in the alleyways where the Semeru warden had finally opened the drain. Ten teams remained: eight attackers who immediately banded together as the Bloc, the Princess Posse, and the NPCs of Team Retribution, headquartered in the city under co-warlords Juice Box and Sir Ferdinand.[7|1] The season's rules were rewritten so that the city stayed sealed until four teams remained, at which point the last of the four combat phases, the Peeling of Larracos, would begin[7|14]: the defenders win by holding out for a full week, and any single attacking team wins by holding the throne room, and with it the defense system, for six hours.[7|1]
The city Carl finally walked through was nothing like the ruin he expected. Arief, a Semeru war leader commanding the Larracos Defense Force infantry, met the crawlers at the college, where dwarves were teaching their children to fight and fairies were mounting defensive jewels atop the towers, and where his own Despondent debuff was plain: his goddess is dead, and he cannot worship another.[7|7] He guided them down ring by ring[7|8], and the Desperado Club reopened under new management the same day[7|8], its new manager promptly banning every crawler from the premises.[7|10] Ferdinand held the mayorship of Shanty Town[7|9], and Carl traded him a magical bowler hat for it[7|10], handing the Princess Posse the ring that encircles the city's entrance.[7|14]
Trouble reached the city from outside before any army did. The crawlers had the dwarves agitate the lava tubes beneath the Skull Empire's castle to blow the mountain apart[7|60], the eruption ran far larger than planned[7|61], and a slow river of lava crept down toward the funnel[7|69] before spilling over the topmost rings and stopping the waterfall entirely.[7|70] The Peeling then arrived early: the moment the Madness fell, the city was declared open, and the War Mage Rebellion, a faction the system had never recognized before, seized Team Retribution's throne room and took the defense system with it.[7|68] The rebellion filled the rings with zarogoths spawned by a soul-crystal generator[7|69] hidden inside the Piccadilly.[7|70] The dwarves held the college gate while defenders blew the connecting ramps to drop the swarms into the pit[7|70], until Carl rose out of the top of the funnel on a levitation potion and dumped an entire inventory folder of explosives onto the theater, destroying the generator and the zarogoths at a stroke, wrecking the lower rings, and turning the city's own towers against him.[7|71]
Defeat now killed every soldier and warlord of the losing team where they stood, so rather than fight the NPCs for a victory[7|68], the crawlers used Zerzura on the city itself. The Sledge cast the spell, and Larracos and Shanty Town, combined into a single settlement, were lifted whole off the ninth floor and set down in the cotton fields outside the Halls of the Ascendency on the Twelfth Floor. The Semeru, the changelings, and thousands of citizens went with it; hired mercenaries did not, and the outworlder soldiers were ejected from the dungeon entirely. With both of its warlords gone, Team Retribution was defeated and the Princess Posse won Faction Wars. Before the city went, the dwarves gathered up the pieces of the shattered statue at the castle gate and rebuilt it: Ysalte, with her pickaxe.[7|85] What remained on the ninth floor was a bare pit, with a bed of dead roots as thick as elephant trunks where the castle had been, crumbling to mulch at a touch and covering a hole that went further down still[7|86]; Emberus burned the floor to nothing minutes later.[7|87]}}

==Speculation==
The chain of command described for the pre-cataclysm Skyfowl ends with an unnamed sprawling capital city on the ninth floor.[2|18] Larracos is the only capital established on that floor, which makes it the near-certain destination of that reporting line, though the text never says so.[3|9]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Priestly believed Larracos was too detailed and too real to be an invention, and suspected it was a copy of a place that genuinely existed somewhere. He also read the city as a retelling of a legend from his own culture, about a people who built a tower to reach the gods and were struck down for it, with the Semeru building the same tower downward rather than upward.[4|33]}}
