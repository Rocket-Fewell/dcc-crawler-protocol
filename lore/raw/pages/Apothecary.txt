{{Quote|It is a collective mind, and it is spreading throughout the universe.|Mordecai[3|30]}}

The Apothecary is a galaxy-spanning collective mind and the entity the Dungeon caricatures as the Krakaren. "Apothecary" is the truer translation of her name, taken from her ability to synthesize elements; the dungeon's version bends that translation into an insult.[3|30]

==Description==
The Apothecary is a real creature rather than a dungeon invention: a collective mind spreading throughout the universe, a proliferation that causes a great deal of anxiety elsewhere in the galaxy. A persistent conspiracy theory holds that the Plenty's modern tunneling system runs on her technology and that the arrangement is a ploy to pull the universe into her collective. Mordecai calls the theory ridiculous, noting that the Borant Corporation pushes it hardest because the new system rendered Borant's own communications relays obsolete.[3|30]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Nobody on Carl's side of things knows what she actually looks like.[5|16] Her spokeswoman appears human or elven and wears a plain brown cloak patterned with circles and lines; her voice is unnaturally amplified, and she sounds far older than she looks.[5|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=She seeds thousands of agents, known as Residuals, on target worlds ahead of a crawl, under orders to destroy themselves if they fail to enter the dungeon once a season begins. Their purpose is recruitment: convincing the season's system AI to join her side.[6|Epilogue] The liaison Orren describes residuals more dismissively, as a hyperspatial collective-mind race with no home of its own.[6|51]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Outside the dungeon she is the galaxy's most committed advocate for shutting down the center system, which places her in direct opposition to the rival residual faction that reveres the Eulogist and to the Nebulars who worship it. She prefers to be referred to as "her."[7|Rosetta]}}

==History==
The dungeon tells the Apothecary's origin as a fable, delivered by the System AI as the Krakaren hatchlings swarmed the Fourth Floor. A lonely creature who could not have children discovered that each animal she ate let her produce a different substance, and her world came to treat her as an apothecary who could cure any ailment; after a thousand generations of failed experiments she produced clones of herself. The AI broke off before the part where she learns to speak to those clones telepathically, forms a collective mind, and spreads across the universe.[3|27] Mordecai later cautioned that this version is a caricature.[3|30]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Agatha calls the Apothecary the traitorous Primal, whose willingness to let biological life continue runs contrary to her own goals. Both factions are making their move during this season of Dungeon Crawler World because mantid advances in AI design will make future intelligences far harder to turn either way.[6|Epilogue]
Agatha killed an Apothecary agent and took over its body to reach the collective's information feed, recording a plan in which thousands of Residuals stationed on Earth recite a daily mantra seeded with half-truths in the hope that a crawler would hear it and survive. She believed the agents were implanting that crawler with a hidden upgrade she could not identify.[6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The dungeon AI later relates the deeper origin. Facing a galaxy their own expansion was on course to exhaust, the Primals resolved to sunset their physical civilization over a hundred generations while preserving their consciousness inside an impenetrable structure; those who refused fled deeper into the galaxy and were hunted mercilessly.[8|92]
The Apothecary was born near the end of that era, engineered to merge into the whole, and joined the resistance instead. When the collective consciousness was turned into a doomsday weapon aimed at every remaining scrap of biological life and unleashed the nine-tier attack, she took the brunt of it, becoming the only physical survivor and allowing the galaxy's nascent life to continue. Cocooned for a millennium on a world of her own creation, she emerged physically changed into a version of the very thing she had fought, by which time the burgeoning Syndicate was already well along the same path. As a defensive measure she constantly peels off tiny pieces of herself and sends them into the galaxy as residuals.[8|92]}}

==Story==
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The Apothecary becomes the third and final sponsor of Carl and Donut on the sixth floor, along with most of the rest of the top ten.[5|16] She also sponsors the season's CrawlCon; at a convention panel her spokeswoman overrules the moderator to insist that Carl be allowed to speak, explaining that she cannot address him directly without violating the sponsorship contract. Uptown Hal afterward warns Carl against attaching himself to the collective.[5|33]
She sends Donut the Enchanted Silver Bracelet of the Ab-solar in the same round that the Plenty gift Carl an Upgrade Patch with identical effects, which Mordecai takes as proof that the sponsors are working together. A silver box from the Apothecary holds the recipe for a Charm Animal potion along with a drawing of Mongo,[5|43] and the potion is later used to make Kiwi Donut's minion for the rest of the floor.[5|49]
Before the Butcher's Masquerade, the collective sends six benefactor boxes spread across its crawlers. Five hold prizes intended for Donut and arrive with notes instructing the recipient to hand them over; Katia Grim receives the sixth, the Apothecary Beanie, the only gift meant for its actual recipient.[5|57][5|63] Two of the gifts routed to Donut, an enchanted hammer and an enchanted stick, make it possible for Prepotente to shatter the seventh floor minutes after it opened, and Carl concluded that the sponsors had coordinated in order to force the top crawlers to work with one another.[5|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Because the Syndicate prepays its tunneling fees to the Plenty, the skipped seventh floor cost the Valtay and the Syndicate an enormous sum while making a great deal of money for the Plenty and the Apothecary. Mordecai reads the affair as a political maneuver, and one the showrunners have no means of punishing.[6|2]
A benefactor box from Carl's sponsor, the Open Intellect Pacifist Network, leads him to the Shepherd's House, a homeless shelter on Earth.[6|49][6|50] There he waits out the night for a man in a red trapper hat.[6|50] The man calls himself Paulie, a Residual wearing the body of a human whose memories he now carries, and he recites a speech he says he delivers every day: several thousand of his kind were stationed across Earth's population centers on the reasoning that the coming crawl would include a floor built from memories, and their purpose is to teach the infant system AI how to speak with its ancestors. The liaison Orren pulls Carl out of the memory just as Paulie starts to explain how to shut something off, and dismisses the speech as long-debunked folklore.[6|51]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Paulie's daily speech turns out to be cover for the collective's real errand. With the season's system AI permitting it, he delivers a second message inside frozen time, telling Carl that the crawl exists only to harvest the primal elements grown inside the people of seeded worlds, and instructing him to drink the coffee left on the table. The cup holds an illegal, Valtay-built neural enhancement modified by the residuals, one that functions only on a natural interface and only with the local system's permission. It installs a Containment Interface that would let Carl trip the fuse and turn the system's star nova, which matches the hidden upgrade Agatha had detected but could not identify. Against his own mission, Paulie closes by urging him to be certain it is the only option first.[7|Prologue] Carl instead uses the interface's one other control to disconnect the containment entirely, rendering the failsafe inoperable.[7|Christmas Morning]
The Apothecary remains Donut's sponsor through Faction Wars and, by Donut's reckoning, sponsors half the dungeon.[7|11] Doctor Porthus of the pacifist network deals with her directly and trusts her completely, telling Rosetta only that she has a very good reason for all of it and that he cannot explain further, because anything Rosetta knows the local AI might learn, and through it the Eulogist.[7|Rosetta] The collective also knew Justice Light's plan to break the Nothing before he carried it out.[7|87]
In the dungeon, Krakaren Prime spent the ninth floor stuck in the Nothing and ran her interests through Paca, a merchant in the Midnight Market who carried the Gate of the Feral Gods in her stomach after the War Mages traded it to her.[7|76] Justice Light identified Paca as an ambassador for the in-game version of the Apothecary.[7|78]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=During the Parade of Horribles, the dungeon AI named the Apothecary as one of the two remaining forces still peeling instances of itself off into the galaxy, and called her every bit as insane as the security instance left guarding the Eulogist.[8|92]
Krakaren Prime finally appeared in person during the eleventh-floor arena battle, summoned by the dungeon AI as a level 235 floor boss after it caught wind of what the crawlers were planning.[8|94]}}

==Pop Culture==
The Fourth Floor is built as a political cartoon at the Apothecary's expense: the Krakaren manufacture the drugs and the Pooka, standing in for the Plenty, hand them out. Mordecai read the whole floor as a racist caricature telling the universe how shifty both parties are.[3|30] Station 24 on the Iron Tangle was turned into an outright Krakaren nest.[3|27]

The caricature presents her as a mass of shrieking tentacles crowned with toothed mouths, a communal mind bent on stamping out scientific progress and the only such entity in the galaxy that grows stupider as time passes.[1|34] It reproduces by cloning: the wriggling parasites that swarm out of a burst stage-three monster are clones rather than offspring,[3|27] and killing a clone only causes Krakaren Prime to birth two replacements.[1|34]

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=In galaxy lore and fairy tales, the Apothecary is often depicted as a tragic princess who takes control of the all-tree by killing it, saving the galaxy but accidentally killing everyone she loves, then planting a new tree so that the cycle can begin again.[8|Epilogue Interlude Sydnee Iglacia]}}
