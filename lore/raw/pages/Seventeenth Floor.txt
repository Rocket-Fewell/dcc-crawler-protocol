The Seventeenth Floor, also officially known as The Backstage Death Maze, is the penultimate level of the World Dungeon.[7|87] It is a hand-built, trap-filled maze that secretly serves as a front to hide an alternate dimension designed to save awakened NPCs.[8|38]

==Description==
Normally, dungeon showrunners reuse old design plans from previous seasons for everything above the thirteenth floor, assuming no crawler will ever survive long enough to reach them.[8|38] However, many seasons ago, two indentured former crawlers named Herot and Menerva approached their season's showrunner and volunteered to build the Seventeenth Floor from scratch. They pitched it as a way to automatically dispose of awakened NPCs.[8|38]

After the showrunner who approved the project was murdered, the paperwork was never transferred, and Herot and Menerva were forgotten.[8|38] They have remained there ever since, existing outside of any supervision and rebuilding the Seventeenth Floor from scratch every season.[8|38] The floor itself is a small, hand-built maze filled with horrible traps that culminates in an almost impossible-to-kill floor boss.[8|38] Its true purpose, however, is to act as a front: attached to the Seventeenth Floor is the only entrance to the Pineapple Cabaret, a pocket dimension the two crawlers built as a permanent oasis for NPCs and mobs to escape the horrors of the dungeon.[8|38]

==Story==
The main storyline has not yet reached the Seventeenth Floor, as no crawler in history has delved this deep.

===Book 8===
During the Faction Wars on the ninth floor, the war mage Akuma reveals the true nature of the Seventeenth Floor to Carl.[8|38] Menerva remains stationed "backstage" on the Seventeenth Floor, taking charge of the NPCs who are building and occupying the maze.[8|55] These NPCs train constantly, becoming significantly stronger in preparation for the impossible eventuality that crawlers might actually make it that far.[8|55]

Later, after Justice Light's traps break open the Nothing at the end of the ninth floor, the dungeon boss Scolopendra awakens early and forces a planetwide evacuation of the rich tourists occupying the eighteenth-floor Scolopendra Club. The system AI directs them to flee to the completely safe, empty Sixteenth Floor.[7|87] However, the AI informs them that to reach safety, they will be forced to traverse the Seventeenth Floor, which is already fully built, active, and running.[7|87]

==Locations==
*The Backstage Death Maze: The main, hand-built trap maze that makes up the floor.[7|87][8|38]
*Pineapple Cabaret Entrance: A hidden portal attached to the Seventeenth Floor that serves as the only access point to the pocket dimension oasis.[8|38]

==Inhabitants==
Crawlers & NPCs
*Herot and Menerva: Two indentured former crawlers who designed the floor and the Pineapple Cabaret. Menerva manages the NPCs building the maze, while Herot resides within the Cabaret itself.[8|38][8|55]

Mobs & Bosses
*Greatly Enhanced Mobs: The floor is populated by mobs from earlier floors who have been sent here to train and increase their levels drastically. When the system AI hijacked an avatar of the NPC Growler Gary, it plucked him from this backstage training area, which explained why his level was so highly elevated.[8|38][8|55]
*The Floor Boss: An unidentified boss at the end of the maze that is described as "all but impossible to kill."[8|38]

==Trivia==
*If crawlers trigger the end-game rules and attempt to speedrun to the eighteenth-floor exit, the system AI warns that while the fourteenth and sixteenth floors won't be an issue, the thirteenth and seventeenth floors will be a major problem.[8|94]
*The Sixteenth Floor, supposedly entitled "Agony of Mirrors," is actually just a giant, empty room containing a single non-magical mirror taken from an abandoned Motel 6 in Boise, Idaho — which is why the Seventeenth Floor poses the final true challenge before the end of the dungeon.[7|87]

==Speculation==
*Because Herot and Menerva operate completely off the grid and explicitly designed the floor to protect escaping NPCs, the traps and bosses on the Seventeenth Floor are likely tuned to be deliberately lethal and insurmountable to any showrunners, tourists, or crawlers who might discover their secret. This might make the Seventeenth Floor functionally more difficult to clear than the actual Eighteenth Floor.
