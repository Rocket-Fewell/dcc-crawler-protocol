Emberus is a Deity, specifically the God of Sun and Ash, known for his unmitigated cruelty and his intense grief over the murder of his son, Geyrun.[4|31][4|32] He becomes Carl's patron deity during the events of the Fifth Floor, setting off a chaotic relationship defined by powerful boons, demanding tithes, and constant threats of fiery retribution.[4|31][4|32]

:This article is about the character. For information about Emberus's church and adherents, see Emberus High Temple of the Ending Sun.

==Description==
Emberus is the personification of a star's destructive power, and he embraces a core philosophy that power, once held, must never sit idle but must be used and never squandered.[4|32] He is known to be stoic and indifferent to the suffering of all but those he deems worthy, and while he can be a just god when the fancy strikes him, the fancy has not struck in a very long time.[4|31] More often he is an "unmitigated asshole" who will arbitrarily burn people just for looking at him funny, and the system counts him among the most unhinged members of the Pantheon.[4|31] Since the murder of his favorite son, Geyrun, Emberus has grown even more unhinged, going so far as to pluck out his own eyes in his grief.[4|28][4|31] Yet for all his cruelty, he shows profound love and relief when reunited with Geyrun's two-headed puppy, Orthrus.[4|31]

==Appearance==
Emberus manifests in several terrifying forms. When he first appears on the Fifth Floor, enraged and grieving, his natural body is a vaguely muscular, human-shaped mass of curling orange and red fire; seen from behind he is asymmetrical, with a huge curved horn smoking like an incense stick erupting from the left side of his head while his right side appears caved in.[4|31] By others' accounts his skin is normally a gooey, blue-hued rock, and he only sets himself fully alight once his fury takes hold.[4|31]

When summoned by Carl's celestial grenade, Emberus takes a different shape: a city-sized floating head and shoulders, his skin a shimmering, smoldering rocky gray dotted with multicolor hotspots like acne.[4|31] A second mouth, glowing red, screams on his right cheek, above which the skin is sunken and flattened like an old injury that healed poorly, and his empty eye sockets, blind since he tore out his own eyes, constantly trail smoke and rain blood.[4|31]

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=When taking a more humanoid, smaller form, he appears as a tall, fifty-year-old bald man with blazing sockets for eyes and a black beard streaked with red, smoldering cracks. In this form he wears a white toga with a gold sash covered in scorch marks, bears an angry scar down the side of his head, and scorches the ground wherever he steps.[8|95]
When pushed to extreme anger, he can transform into a massive, half-faced monstrosity capable of melting worlds, with fire flowing down the sides of his head.[8|96]}}

==History==
Emberus is the youngest brother of Taranis, the God of Thunder and regent to the Celestial Throne, and the twin brother of Hellik, the God of Sun and Life; together the twins form the "sun duology."[4|31][4|32] Emberus himself strongly believes he is the candidate best suited to ascend to the Celestial Throne.[4|31] His bond with Hellik is venomous: Hellik is obsessed with killing both Emberus and Taranis, and Emberus in turn regards his twin as a fool and a danger with no right to exist.[4|31][4|32]

According to lore, Emberus once destroyed an entire country, waking a volcano and burying the world in lava, all because a drunk man had snuffed out his torch by urinating on it; even Eris was appalled.[4|28] Following the unsolved murder of Geyrun, the boy's beloved two-headed puppy Orthrus was accidentally sucked into the Nothing on the very day his master died.[4|28][4|29]

==Story==
===Book 4===
During the Fifth Floor, Carl accidentally unleashes Orthrus from the Nothing, and an enraged, grief-blind Emberus rampages through the Lacuna outside the bubbles in search of the puppy.[4|28][4|29] Wrongly convinced Orthrus is trapped inside a neighboring world, bubble 18, the blind god pounds on and melts that bubble, too busy shrieking to notice the puppy running loose or the crawlers steering him back just miles behind.[4|30][4|31] Trapped in a death-spiraling biplane with the hunter Quan closing in to kill both him and Orthrus, Carl resorts to what he calls the nuclear option: he pledges himself to Emberus and then hurls a celestial grenade, which, because he now worships a deity, involuntarily summons Emberus instead of a random god.[4|31] The god manifests as a massive floating head and shoulders, his weeping blood turning to rain as he tearfully reunites with Orthrus before both blink away to the Celestial Halls, leaving Carl unharmed.[4|30][4|31]

In the aftermath, Carl becomes a formal adherent of Emberus, bound by the god's rule set, requiring a daily drop of blood at one of his temples, a five percent tithe on looted gold, an oath never to harm a fellow worshipper or to wield gear blessed by Hellik, and completion of every church quest; in return the god confers immunity to the Burn effect, a chance to inflict Burn in melee, bonus experience against Hellik's followers, and free access to Club Vanquisher.[4|32] Emberus then messages Carl to grant a boon raising his constitution by 25% for thirty hours and to issue two standing quests: find Geyrun's murderer, and kill Hellik before the twelfth floor, the latter promising a Celestial God Box.[4|32]

==Progression==
As a locked deity, Emberus is a Level 250 immortal who cannot be sponsored by external entities during the current season.[4|31] Like all gods, he is invulnerable on the dungeon floors, a protection that is not meant to lapse until the twelfth floor.[4|28][4|31]

==Speculation==
Although Emberus is desperate to find Geyrun's killer, he does not firmly blame his twin Hellik for the murder: his own quest notes concede that the obvious suspect was in council with the other gods when it happened, giving Hellik an alibi.[4|32]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Emberus's "Find Out Who Killed My Son" quest updates during the Butcher's Masquerade, directing Carl to obtain a Memorial Crystal for the goddess Apito and to determine whether it bears on Geyrun's death. {{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=During the Amayon event on the Eighth Floor, the demon prince claims to know exactly what became of Geyrun and to have witnessed "the deal being signed," implying the murder was bound up in some bargain. {{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Hellik denies killing Geyrun and claims their mother-sister Apito has been corrupted by a malevolent presence that Geyrun may have uncovered, suggesting the newly returned primordial goddess Nekhebit is actually behind both the murder and the rotting of the All Tree.[7|54]
