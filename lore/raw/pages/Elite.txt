Elites are powerful, one-of-a-kind NPCs that make up the non-boss, non-divine hero class of Dungeon Crawler World.[2|6] They are created (designed, printed, and programmed), licensed to, and owned by private production companies for the sole purpose of appearing in tunneled shows set inside the Dungeon, and unlike most mobs, they can travel freely between the dungeon's urban floors.[2|7]

==Description==
Elites are not natural dungeon mobs. They are artificial entities that are designed, printed, and then imprinted with the memories they need to act out their drama, and as far as any Elite is concerned, that scripted world and those manufactured memories are absolute reality.[2|7] They are highly powerful, often egomaniacal entities that regard crawlers as little more than tools to be used, killed, or otherwise exploited in service of their own goals.[2|6]

On each season of Dungeon Crawler World, multiple new shows and storylines launch when the Third Floor opens, and thousands of Elites are spread across the third, sixth, and ninth floors, traveling freely between them; some shows, all centered on the ninth floor, have run across multiple seasons.[2|7] Although Elites are not directly controlled by the System AI or by their showrunners, they are not autonomous either. A full team of writers and producers steers each Elite through a prepared story and will, if a storyline veers off course, hot patch new instructions right into the elite's mind.[2|7]

Because Elites are typically powerful and protected by thick "plot armor," and because the rules meant to keep the dungeon "fair" for crawlers are applied loosely whenever an Elite is involved, guides universally advise crawlers to keep their distance. A crawler who blunders into an Elite's storyline is often treated as an expendable extra, or "red shirt," in the Elite's personal drama.[2|7] As Mordecai explains:
If it comes down to saving the life of a single crawler or blowing an entire drama that's been building for weeks, what do you think is going to happen? I've seen it a dozen times. A hot shit crawler comes across an elite, and instead of trying to solve the quest, he decides to go all murder hobo and kill the NPC. Something always happens. Something bad. Most of these elites have very thick plot armor, and in those stories, you're the extra. The red shirt... And if they can write in the death of a popular crawler, all the better. It will guarantee their show gets more viewers.[2|7]

Elites initially appear as ordinary white or red dots on the minimap, depending on whether they are friendly. The first time a crawler meets an Elite, the crawler receives the Meet an Elite Achievement, and from then on every Elite is marked on the minimap with a black cross.[2|6] Elites are typically quest givers.

==History==
The history of any given Elite is entirely fabricated by the dungeon's writers.[2|7] Elites are generated for the more scripted aspect of the dungeon, and their storylines usually activate when the third floor opens, much like the debut of a new television season in the fall.[2|7]

The writers give them elaborate, dramatic backstories to hook viewers. For example, Tsarina Signet believes she is the bastard daughter of a High-Elf King and a Naiad, living as an outcast.[2|6][5|14] Another Elite, Ringmaster Grimaldi, was given a history in which he ran a beloved traveling circus in the Over City before a cataclysmic poison cloud froze him and his family center stage.[2|10]

==Story==
Carl and Donut interact significantly with Elites, most notably Tsarina Signet, who sits at the center of a revenge drama titled "Vengeance of the Daughter."[2|11][5|15] Although The Royal Court is warned by Mordecai to steer clear of Elites entirely, they repeatedly find themselves tangled up in Signet's storyline.[2|11]

Carl manages to turn the behind-the-scenes machinery surrounding Elites to his advantage. When he poisons the Elite Ringmaster Grimaldi—a move that would normally trigger immediate retaliation—he instead forces the showrunners to cut a deal, leveraging his own popularity by promising to appear as a recurring guest star in the "Vengeance of the Daughter" show on the sixth floor if they let him survive the encounter.[2|11]

==Dungeon Lore==
Elites are the stars of their own programs and serials within the wider Dungeon Crawler World broadcast network.[2|7] Viewers across the galaxy tune in to watch these elaborate dramas unfold, and take particular delight when crawlers blunder into a scene and ruin the producers' hard work.[2|7]

Because the production companies invest so heavily in these storylines, the system permits showrunners to bend the rules of reality around Elites to protect their narratives. A crawler who tries to go "murder hobo" and kill an Elite outside the script will often run into sudden, inexplicable, and deadly consequences engineered to protect the show's ratings.[2|7]

==Trivia==
* Because they have special travel privileges, important Elites who survive their third-floor storylines automatically travel down to the sixth floor (the Hunting Grounds) to continue their dramas while crawlers work through the floors in between.[2|7]
