The Scavenger Myth is the dungeon's own origin story for its pantheon, explaining how the current generation of gods overthrew an older order, and the source of the "Scavenger's Daughter" title tied to Carl's own divine back patch.

== Description ==

Like the Scolopendra myth, the Scavenger Myth exists in multiple, contradictory versions, and Rosetta suspects the dungeon simply uses whichever version suits it best at any given moment. Before the current pantheon, older, wilder gods ruled. When the new gods rose up and expelled them, some of the old gods were banished to Sheol, some to the Nothing, and some were killed outright. If an old god put up too much resistance, the new gods would instead target its worshippers directly; once all of a god's worshippers were dead or converted, that god simply faded away.

=== The All Tree and Nekhebit ===

The All Tree is both a literal tree within the dungeon and a metaphor for existence itself. It bore fruit for the old gods, including Nekhebit, later known as the Scavenger Mother of Mothers. In the old times, Nekhebit was a life and protector deity, depicted as a vulture, a scavenger who cleaned up after the others. She supposedly wasn't permitted to have children, but had three anyway: a son, a daughter, and a centipede. Nekhebit was the last of the old gods to fall, ultimately defeated by her own daughter, Apito, who absorbed the last of Nekhebit's worshippers.

=== Nekhebit's three children ===

Nekhebit's daughter, Apito, went on to have children of her own who overthrew the old order and founded the current pantheon. Nekhebit's centipede child is Scolopendra, who eventually became a hotel for the wealthy and now occasionally unleashes world-ending magic from the bottom of the dungeon. Nekhebit's third child, an unnamed son, exists in the empty plane, cleaning up the souls that escape there.

Critically, Nekhebit herself is not "the Scavenger," despite being a scavenger goddess. Which of her three children actually holds that title is exactly where the myth's competing versions diverge.

=== Who is the Scavenger? ===

The myths disagree on which of Nekhebit's children is "the Scavenger," and by extension, whose child is "the Scavenger's Daughter":
* In some versions, Scolopendra is the Scavenger, making the Scavenger's Daughter the result of a union between Scolopendra and someone else.
* In other versions, the unnamed son is the Scavenger, having impregnated someone from within the empty plane; sometimes he's even described as "the first mortal," though the myth's own internal logic makes this contradictory, since he comes later in the story.
* In still other versions, Apito, Scolopendra, and the unnamed son are treated as a single divine trinity, split into three but ultimately one being.

Whichever version is true, what all of them agree on is that the Scavenger's Daughter holds the same reality-erasing power Apito once used to overthrow Nekhebit: the ability to end the current pantheon and start over, but magnified further, since she can also seize the powers of other gods and use them as her own.

== Story ==

Carl first starts piecing the myth together after Akuma mentions being able to smuggle allies to "the Pineapple Cabaret," in the same breath as Samantha, her sand ooze daughter Mrs. Ghazi, and the still-unsolved murder of Emberus's son Geyrun. Carl eventually asks Rosetta to explain the full myth directly, hoping to figure out how it connects to the war mages' claim that Samantha's daughter is somehow this same entity. Rosetta admits neither she nor Carl actually know how the war mages arrived at that conclusion, or whether it's even true; as she puts it, much of this is simply left up to faith.

Carl's own Scavenger's Daughter Upgrade Patch is tied to this same mythology: it "opens her eyes" and reacts whenever Carl is near a deity, and treats him as though he might himself be connected to the Scavenger's Daughter storyline, though the patch's behavior around the possessed Lucia Mar and at the close of the Parade of Horribles suggests even the patch itself doesn't fully understand what it's reacting to.

== Trivia ==

* Rosetta compares the myth's origin to her own culture's stories of an ocean goddess with sapient menstrual blood, which she says was really just a folk explanation for a red algae bloom that evolved the ability to move onto land, suggesting the dungeon's mythology was built the same way real-world religions are: as origin stories reverse-engineered to explain observed phenomena.
