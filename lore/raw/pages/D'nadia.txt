D'nadia is a Saccathian and Princess of the Prism Kingdom, a small Sac government in Syndicate space that carries outsized weight in certain trade circles.[2|19] An outspoken fixture of the intergalactic talk show circuit and an enthusiastic fan of Carl and Donut, she became one of the Skull Empire's loudest critics after a botched assassination attempt on the crawlers killed her closest friend instead.[2|19]

==Description==
D'nadia belongs to the Saccathian people, a race common across the Syndicate whose members are generally called Sacs, and she is known for saying exactly what she thinks.[2|19] She is a regular guest on Danger Zone with Ripper Wonton and holds strong opinions on intergalactic politics; in the Valtay Corporation's dispute with Borant over an unpaid debt, she found both parties disagreeable but sided with the Valtay.[2|19]

Her manner is warm and physically demonstrative. She trumpets through her facial tentacles when amused, and she repeatedly tried to take Carl's hand during his first broadcast even though her webbed claws passed straight through his fingers.[2|19] That warmth has an edge to it. When the comedian Tucker treated the murder of the singer Manasa as a piece of political arithmetic, D'nadia told him to shut up and reminded him that their friend had just been killed.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=She sponsors crawlers in her own name rather than through a corporate arm, and spends most of her time traveling from talk show to talk show.[3|12]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=She remains fond of a loophole. Judging a contest technically made her part of the production, satisfying a requirement she needed to meet, and she cheerfully described the arrangement as a technicality.[5|66]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Cornered at the end, she framed her actions as service and said she had done it for her people. Carl rejected the framing and told her she had done it for herself, and that her people would suffer for it.[7|26] He later grouped her with Rishi of the Blood Sultanate as simply another power-hungry leader dressing ambition up as policy.[7|58]}}

==Appearance==
D'nadia has a cephalopod head with a tangle of squid-like tentacles hanging from her face; Carl compared the effect to a human-sized Cthulhu. Her skin is bumpy and gray, her hands are webbed claws, and when she cries black oily tears cascade down the front of her face.[2|19] On the Danger Zone panel she wore a crown and a long, flowing dress that hid the shape of her body entirely.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=She stands taller than Carl, and her facial tentacles work something like elephant trunks: she drinks by dipping one into a glass and drawing the liquid up.[5|67] At the Butcher's Masquerade she appeared by projection in a glittering purple gown covered in sparkles and curved with unnatural lumps, alluding to a body carrying additional tentacles; the projection could be touched gently, passed through under a firmer push, and smelled faintly of sweet calamari.[5|66]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Seen in person rather than as a projection, the body beneath the gown is green-tinged and covered in tentacles, which sprang loose in every direction when the fabric was torn away. Her blood is blue.[7|26]}}

==History==
The Prism is a comparatively small kingdom, but D'nadia's standing in Syndicate trade circles is considerable.[2|19] She had been close friends with the singer Manasa for countless years.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Her father's retirement elevated her from Princess to Empress of the Prism.[5|66] She corrects anyone who uses the old form.[5|67]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=She was the last living heir of her dynasty, a small but powerful trading house, and her death left the Syndicate probate courts bracing for a long run of inheritance litigation.[7|27]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The Prism controlled a holding group that owned residential habitat complexes in the Makoka Cloud, a densely settled asteroid field outside the center system. Her death broke that control, and the holdings were absorbed by whatever administration was left running the Skull Empire.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Sydnee Iglacia]}}

==Story==
===Book 2===
Carl and Donut first meet Princess D'nadia on the Third Floor, when they appear alongside Hekla on an episode of Danger Zone with Ripper Wonton; she had asked specifically to sit beside them, and she laughed openly at Donut's demolition of the comedian Tucker.[2|19] When a pulse fired from Prince Stalwart's personal yacht destroyed the production trailer holding the singer Manasa, a shot meant for the crawlers, D'nadia checked on the two of them first, then wept openly and announced that the Prism Kingdom officially condemned the Skull Empire's action and would file an immediate grievance with the Syndicate Court.[2|19]

===Book 3===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=D'nadia becomes Donut's sponsor during the Fourth Floor Iron Tangle.[3|12] Her first Benefactor Box held a unique item, the Prism Industries Capacitating and Focusing Goggles, a special high-fashion edition subtitled "The Princess Donut" and personally signed.[3|15] Donut later credited the gift with saving Katia's life.[3|30]}}

===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Her next box for Donut was a bronze one that appeared to be empty.[4|8] The gift turned out to be an upgrade to the goggles rather than a new item, sharpening their heat vision.[4|9] By the time the crawlers received their second sponsors, D'nadia had taken on five additional crawlers, which Donut took as a personal slight.[4|7]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=D'nadia attended the Butcher's Masquerade on the Sixth Floor as one of three judges for the talent show and pet contest, appearing by projection from orbit and warning Donut not to expect favoritism. She told Carl she was cross with him because his attorney and his new corporation had almost ruined her buy-in bid.[5|66]
She spent much of the evening with Signet, pushing the half-naiad toward understanding that her entire reality was constructed: the whole universe was watching, the goddess present at the party was under the control of Circe Took, and D'nadia was looking forward to meeting Carl on the battlefield outside Larracos.[5|73]
Afterward the Prism claimed one of the two Ninth Floor Faction Wars positions left open by the withdrawal of the Dreadnoughts and the Burrowers, setting D'nadia against Donut's own team, the Princess Posse.[5|77]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=She attended the fifth Faction Wars pre-production meeting as warlord of the Prism Kingdom Clan, announcing that she had just onboarded onto the playing field and greeting Donut warmly across the table.[6|21] During the Eighth Floor she sold Donut's sponsorship to Long Haul Biological Waste Management Solutions; Donut treated it as a betrayal, while Carl had been expecting it for a while.[6|29]
Researchers for Odette's program later surfaced three emergency action items D'nadia had filed, all rejected by the System AI: deleting every NPC on the Larracos level, barring crawlers from Faction Wars in any capacity, and removing the Princess Posse from the game or deleting Carl and Donut. Each team gets only three such filings, so all of hers were spent before the games began. Seeing the notifications was the moment Donut understood that D'nadia was not the friend she had pretended to be.[6|35]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The Prism held position five on the ninth floor, directly south of the Blood Sultanate and bordering Princess Posse territory, and D'nadia committed most of her force to that border.[7|22] After the southern front broke, Carl and Donut flew to the Prism headquarters, a small and lightly defended building. Rosetta reported that D'nadia had spent every penny getting to the floor and that the officers she expected never arrived once the quarantine took effect, leaving her with only a few friends.[7|25]
She fought from her throne room with a magical crossbow, a concealed pulse pistol, and three separate shield modules built into her gown, and she cut herself with a hidden knife to keep her health from topping off so that she could not be marked with the Ring of Divine Suffering. Clockwork duplicates of Mongo stripped away the gown and severed the tentacle holding the knife; Carl then healed her to full and marked her.[7|26]
She pleaded with them both, calling Carl her friend and reminding Donut that she had given her the goggles. Carl instead confronted her over what she had said to Signet and over the years she had spent buying her way into the dungeon to kill people like him, and Donut delivered the killing line on Mongo's behalf.[7|26]
The system credited the kill to Mongo, declared the Prism defeated, and awarded all of its assets to the Princess Posse.[7|27]}}

==Speculation==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Mordecai explained that benefactors commonly send an item on one floor and upgrades to that same item on later floors, building toward something of legendary or celestial quality for a fraction of the cost. D'nadia's decision to upgrade the goggles rather than send a second item suggests she was deliberately following that strategy with Donut.[4|9]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Her three emergency action items all targeted crawler participation rather than any particular tactical advantage, which reads less like an opening move than like an attempt to avoid facing the crawlers at all. Having bought her seat before the Princess Posse existed, she may have understood by the time she onboarded that the field she had paid for was no longer the one she was entering.[6|35]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=A financial reading of her arc holds together across three books. Carl assumed she had overextended herself buying the Faction Wars seat and that selling Donut's sponsorship was a way to recover cash.[6|29] Rosetta reported that she had spent every penny getting to the floor and was poorer than the Blood Sultanate, which would explain the small castle, the thin garrison, and the borrowed mercenaries.[7|26] The achievement for her death describes her as bankrupted as well as killed.[7|27]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=D'nadia said only that her father had chosen to retire.[5|66] Yet after her death the Prism's holdings passed to the Skull Empire rather than reverting to him.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Sydnee Iglacia] Had he still been alive and able to resume rule, that transfer is hard to explain, which raises the possibility that the retirement was not voluntary and that he did not outlive it.}}
