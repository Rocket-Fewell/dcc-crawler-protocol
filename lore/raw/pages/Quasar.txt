{{Quote|The universe's four lubricants. Blood, tears, taxes, and lawyers.|Quasar[5|3]}}

Quasar is a Nullian advocate and Carl's legal representative under Syndicate law. Carl gains him on the Sixth Floor by demanding a lawyer, far earlier in the crawl than counsel is normally available, and Quasar tells him at their first meeting that he represents Carl alone.[5|3]

==Description==
Quasar is an advocate licensed to appear before Syndicate courts and in liaison proceedings. He is blunt, profane, and impatient with any question he considers a waste of billable time, but candid about his limits: he tells Carl he cannot stop the Syndicate from exploiting him once he becomes a citizen, only reduce the damage.[5|3]

The work pays little, and few outfits will hire a Nullian, so he makes his living representing condemned clients. He cites twenty cycles of study and practice and says the day he accepts a bribe from the Skull Empire is the day he withers. Sabotaging a client in Carl's position would take no effort at all, he argues, so the work he visibly puts in is the only assurance Carl has that nobody has bought him.[5|3] His standing advice is simple: in front of a liaison, say nothing except a request for counsel, and touch nothing until he arrives.[5|3]

==Appearance==
Quasar is a gray-skinned alien roughly four and a half feet tall, with oversized eyes and a bulbous, hairless head. Carl identifies the species from the cookbook as a Nullian, a rarely seen race similar to the much taller Forsoothed. Where the soothers speak haltingly, Quasar talks in a gritty, rapid-fire accent Carl compares to a New Yorker's.[5|3]

He appears exclusively as a holo and is never physically present in a room with his client; the smoke from the vape pen he uses constantly pixelates as it spreads, which is how Carl first confirms he is a projection. He dresses in a tan suit and tie, and the ties are animated, the first one featuring a gyrating Nullian hula girl that winks.[5|3]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Later ties feature pickles wearing sunglasses[6|12] and an upside-down pineapple that blinks.[6|51] Despite being bald, he is vain enough to ask Carl how his hair looks before meeting a woman he admires.[6|12]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=For the exit-deal meeting at the end of the Ninth Floor he wears a tie printed with Mongo, having hoped to be assigned Donut as a second client.[7|Epilogue]}}

==History==
Quasar spent twenty cycles studying and working before he could, as he puts it, scrabble for scraps representing condemned clients.[5|3] Among the cases he studied in school was the exit deal made by the former crawler Remex the Grand,[5|3] whose use of the Zerzura spell he still retells with open admiration.[5|56]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=He has a brother and a niece named Tempest, a young Nullian who interviews him for a school report on the crawl. He was married to a woman named Nova, who has since left him. His family lives far out on the galactic frontier, which he comes to regard as a stroke of luck.[7|Tempest's Floor 8 Recap School Report]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Nullians were nominally guaranteed equal access to education but shut out in practice, and Quasar had to sue his law school simply to be admitted. The case drew attention across the net, and Judge Victory ruled in his favor. He met his closest friend, a dromedarian attorney named Kiki, at orientation. His home system sits at the far end of the Franciscan Arm, days of travel from the nearest tunnel gate.[8|Interlude: Quasar]}}

==Story==
Carl acquires Quasar by demanding a lawyer during a meeting with the liaison Orren early on the Sixth Floor, since counsel is not normally available until the Tenth Floor, when a crawler becomes an official citizen of the Syndicate.[5|3] Orren turns off his auditory receptors, and Quasar uses his five minutes to stop Carl from picking up the Gate of the Feral Gods while it sits under administrative hold, an act that would have deleted the artifact outright.[5|3]

Carl refuses to sell the gate and suggests treating it as collateral instead, and Quasar builds the proposal around the idea,[5|3] planning to open at six mercenaries and settle at four.[5|56] The final agreement gives the Royal Court four cretin bodyguards and two installed spells, including Zerzura, which is his idea, sold to Carl on the precedent of Remex the Grand and on the argument that every faction, fan, and showrunner will want to watch the carnage repeat rather than interfere with it.[5|3] He is candid about the spell's drawbacks.[5|56] At the table he produces an obscure rule, subsection 674 of the secondary program amendment, and uses it to lock a ninth-floor extension for Sensation Entertainment, giving the producers a financial incentive to keep Carl alive; his claim that they have already agreed is a bluff.[5|13]

He also works the hunter problem, telling Carl to complain loudly and constantly about hunters receiving information from outside the dungeon while he pushes from his end, though he is far less confident about this front than the others. It pays off: a court injunction later bars the hunters from communicating with anyone outside the dungeon, and Carl credits him for it.[5|8][5|21] On a separate matter, Carl asks him to sue over the subscription service that lets viewers listen in on crawler messages; Quasar reports that a case is already working its way through the courts and gives it even odds, and the service is discontinued.[5|8]

Access to him is fragile. He goes silent for stretches behind an automated reply,[5|13] and rule changes force him to obtain a court order before sending Carl a message at all.[5|15] With litigation frozen, Orren circumvents the restriction once to allow a supervised conversation.[5|59]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=When the caprid liaison Harbinger turns up at one of Carl's interviews, Quasar zaps in furious that nobody warned him, noting that his contract permits him to appear in an emergency. During the adjutant selection on Odette's show he coaches Carl silently from the side of the room, pushing hard for the orc judge Victory.[6|36] When Orren later moves to seize a bin Carl looted from a Residual, Quasar cites Grixist Swarm v. Syndicate Council to argue the seizure has no standing, then turns a straight confiscation into a trade.[6|51]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=He warns Carl at the start of the Ninth Floor that Sensation Entertainment has been bought by a subsidiary of the Dream and is demanding the third season he negotiated, an obligation that can now be turned against Carl. He dismisses the offers as garbage, allowing only that a joint deal offered to Carl and Donut is worth a second look, and records Carl's refusal. He will also describe the coming floor, but warns that hearing it adds a full season to any deal Carl later takes, and he delivers the news that Donut has been assigned separate counsel, a naga named Princess Chandra who is now Carl's wife.[7|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Chandra identifies Quasar as the single biggest threat to her scheme and hires two Taurin contractors to eliminate him.[8|Interlude: Chandra] The contractors go to his brother's house instead. His communications with Carl grow unreliable, and at least one attempted warning is cut off mid-message.[8|28] By their final meeting at the end of the Tenth Floor he is visibly shaking and distracted. He presents the single offered exit deal, a one-season indenture ending in freedom, then tells Carl he would not trust it, and that he would not trust the offer promised at the start of the Twelfth Floor either. He flees the meeting without explaining what has him so frightened.[8|88]}}
