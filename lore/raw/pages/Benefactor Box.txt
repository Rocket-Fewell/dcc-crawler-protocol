{{Quote|Patrons are the only ones who can send what are called Benefactor Boxes.|Mordecai[1|4]}}
Benefactor Boxes, also called sponsor boxes, are a class of loot box purchased for a crawler by their sponsors.[1|4] They use the same six quality tiers as every other box, but rank for rank they outclass ordinary loot: a bronze benefactor box beats a gold adventurer box.[1|4]

==Description==
Only a sponsor can send one, and the sponsor chooses the item personally.[1|4] Because the contents come from outworld commerce rather than the dungeon's own loot tables, a box can deliver gear that is otherwise impossible to find inside, such as a pulse rifle or automated power armor.[1|4] The item's strength and value set the tier and therefore the price, so most sponsors can only afford silver or gold; the tiers run Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Legendary, and Celestial.[1|4] The more a sponsor pays for the slot and the higher a crawler's social numbers climb, the more and better boxes that crawler can expect.[1|31]

==Operation==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Delivery is not always immediate. When the Borant Corporation fell behind on fulfillment, sponsors could pay an emergency fee to have a box arrive at once.[3|13]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The tier an item requires is calculated from its origin, value, rarity, and a hundred other factors; the sender picks the gift, not the grade.[4|8] Because exceptional items cost a fortune, sponsors commonly send a modest item on one floor and upgrades for that same item later, the upgrade arriving in an apparently empty box. Four or five cheap boxes cost a fraction of a single legendary, which lets a patient benefactor build a crawler up to legendary or celestial gear across several floors.[4|9] Sending more than one box in a single floor is possible, but the cost is astronomical.[4|9] A sponsor may also send a product sample, which looks like an ordinary box but is written into the sponsorship contract as a freebie, sent so the crawler will handle and discuss the product on air.[4|7]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Delivered items are commonly bound to the named recipient, a measure meant to stop other crawlers from murdering them for the gear; a rare scroll called Cracker Jack can lift the restriction.[5|60] A sponsor may attach a written note, but notes are billed by the character and anything insufficiently vague gets rewritten by the system AI instead, so benefactors are forced to speak in code.[5|55]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=A liaison can seize a box in transit, though the system AI is capable of overriding the seizure and delivering it anyway.[6|48]}}

==Story==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Benefactor boxes became routine on the Fourth Floor, when the Borant Corporation announced that every surviving crawler in the dungeon had been assigned a sponsor for the first time in the show's history.[3|13]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Late in the Sixth Floor, the Apothecary and the Plenty sent a coordinated run of boxes to half a dozen different crawlers, every one of them holding an item and a note directing that it be handed to Donut, a pattern Mordecai had never seen before.[5|60] Borant appealed to block one of them, the Hammer of Fast Forward routed through Imani, and was denied immediately.[5|60]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Carl's last delivery from the Pacifist Network arrived already seized by liaison action, only for the system AI to override the seizure.[6|48] Orren later told him the whole delivery had been a trap, engineered to force the showrunners into a choice that would end the crawl either way.[6|51]}}

==Trivia==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=A benefactor box and a fan box are separate awards, and a crawler can have both waiting at once.[3|29]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Mordecai called Carl's emergency platinum box the most expensive box in the game's history, worth more than the gross domestic product of some star systems.[4|34]}}
