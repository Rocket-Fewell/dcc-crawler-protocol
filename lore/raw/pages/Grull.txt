Grull is a war god worshipped by equine-class mobs throughout the Dungeon.[1|25]

==AI Description==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=When summoned before the Twelfth Floor: 
War God Grull. Level 250. Sponsored by Prince Maestro of the Skull Empire.

Warning: This is a deity. He is invulnerable on this floor.

This god has been involuntarily summoned to this location. Summoning rules apply.

==Description==
Mordecai names horses, centaurs, and tikbalangs as likely adherents of Grull, and notes that llama-type creatures pray to him as well, though not until after the Third Floor. His worshippers smear themselves in blue makeup and talk incessantly about wenches and dying gloriously in battle.[1|25]

Mordecai is blunt about what that worship is actually directed at. There are no real gods in the Dungeon, he tells Carl, only those who pay for the privilege.[1|25]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Mantaurs are among his most visible followers.[3|11] He is a sanctum-tier deity and a sponsored god on this season of Dungeon Crawler World; the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook records that gods are invulnerable except on the Twelfth Floor and above.[3|32]
A summoned Grull does not control his own size; he swells until he fills whatever chamber holds him, and an involuntary summoning arrives untethered, free to smash everything in reach.[3|32] In the field he is gleefully cruel, taunting Carl as a piglet and telling him that his planet was nothing and that all of human history will be forgotten.[3|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Pawna, the goddess of peace, is Grull's sister and his arch enemy within the pantheon.[4|2]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=His congregation is narrower than it first appears. Female mantaurs do not worship Grull at all; they follow Enyo, an unhinged goddess of war.[8|13][8|58]
Corcunda, severed from his devotion when he was split in half, described Grull afterward as a very unpleasant deity and wondered how he had ever fallen into the trap of worshipping him.[8|58] When his two halves were rejoined, Grull sent word that he was to be punished for failing in his faith rather than simply released.[8|65]}}

==Appearance==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Grull is a black-skinned, overly muscular minotaur and centaur hybrid, with the torso and head of a bull on the body of a horse and a gold ring hanging through his snout. He stands approximately 42 ft (12.8 m) tall at the front and carries a smoking, double-headed axe roughly the size of a passenger jet; the handle appears to be a live oak tree, and the head appears to be still-molten metal.[3|33]
His hooves are shod in flaming horseshoes, one of which flattened Carl when the god stepped on him without noticing. Even at four stories, Carl judged the summoned form to be a miniature of the god's true self.[3|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The form is consistent across appearances. Several floors later, he arrived through a factory wall as the same bull-headed centaur with the same enormous axe, wading ankle-deep through a river of molten steel without any apparent discomfort.[8|66]}}

==History==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Grull is the child of Taranis and Apito, the Blessed Oak Mother, and one of the few trueborn heirs to the Celestial Ascendency. His temper is severe enough that his father doubts he should ever rule the heavens.[3|33] The Ascendency runs as its own game with its own storyline on the Twelfth Floor, separate from Faction Wars, and deity spots there are frequently purchased by celebrities.[3|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=At the first Butcher's Masquerade, held by his parents as a night of respite for their perpetually scheming family, it was Grull who asked why the gathering was called a masquerade when no one wore masks. His mother's answer, that the masks are the smiles and goodwill the guests present to one another, is treated as sacred gospel by the high elves. Grull did not understand it, and attended anyway.[5|64]}}

==Story==
===Book 1===
Carl receives the Enchanted War Gauntlet of the Exalted Grull on the First Floor and asks Mordecai how he can tell if someone worships Grull, prompting a warning not to strike adherents with the gauntlet.[1|25]

===Book 2===
King Rust of the Skull Empire purchases a deity sponsorship shortly before the fourth floor opens. The empire almost never buys them, and Odette reads the expense as an attempt to reach Carl, naming Grull as her best guess for the god involved.[2|Epilogue]

===Book 3===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Carl nearly summons Grull while fighting the Mantaur Gore-Gore on the Fourth Floor, because the war gauntlet carries a compounding summoning chance per strike against an adherent, but he kills the vessel before the ten second timer expires.[3|11]
On the last day of the floor, blocked from the employee line by mantaur guards, Carl deliberately punches one until the summoning begins, intending to throw the god through an interdiction cart's portal at the Mimic Rex occupying Trainyard E. The guard drags him along on the way through.[3|32]
The system frames the result as a Death Challenge, with Grull and the city boss competing to kill Carl. Grull splits the boss in half with a single swing, then hunts Carl across the yard, buckling the ground with his axe, melting trains to slag with a blast of heat from his mouth, and at one point stepping directly on him. The sponsor is revealed to be Prince Maestro, presumed dead aboard his family's ship but in fact smuggled into the Dungeon beforehand, using the debut as his own public return. A second mantaur is punched into summoning range and thrown into the portal to the abyss with one second left, pulling Grull away mid-transfer and leaving a smoke outline where he had been standing.[3|33]}}

===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Surviving the encounter earns Carl achievements for summoning a god, seeing one, being attacked by one, and being stepped on by one. The last of these adds a deity tab to his interface along with the option to worship Grull, which he refuses in the crudest terms available, and his Legendary Deity's Box holds Pawna's Tears, a gift from the god's own sister.[4|2]
Grull is one of the first two gods Carl is eligible to worship. When Carl finally pledges himself in desperation on the fifth floor, the tab offers Grull or Emberus; he chooses Emberus, closing Grull off for the rest of the crawl.[4|31][4|32]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Princess Formidable had given Katia the Bolt of Ophiotaurus specifically so it could be used on her brother while he wore the god, and Carl had been training Donut on the armor-removing Laundry Day Spell with Grull in mind. Circumstances force him to spend that opportunity on another deity at the Butcher's Masquerade instead, which at least proves the technique works.[5|74]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Blue-painted mantaur guards patrol the halls of Club Vanquisher, where the satyr jeweler at the club's Emberus shop dismisses them as idiots too frightened to set foot inside a rival god's storefront.[6|56]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=As part of the Varg Vikernes Achievement, Carl learns that Grull has marked him as a friend of the church, an inclusion he finds completely inexplicable.[7|57] Driving a god also costs the player: because Prince Maestro is occupied inside Grull, he is barred from competing in Faction Wars, which is why his father takes the field as the orc team's war chief instead.[7|32]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Grull is summoned to the factory during Heat 5, moments after Corky and Porky are recombined into Corcunda and a marked-for-death symbol appears over the reunited mantaur.[8|65] Maestro, still driving him, ignores Corcunda to go after Carl, announcing that he had been waiting for Carl to summon him by accident and revealing that friend-of-the-church status doubles as a tracking measure.[8|66]
Donut uses the Laundry Day Spell to forcibly eject Maestro from the god, dropping him into the molten metal below. Freed of his driver, Grull ignores Carl and resumes walking after Corcunda, who teams up with Dong Quixote to attack him so the rest can finish the course. The pair strike the god of war together and die doing it. Grull calls it a good death and vanishes back to the Twelfth Floor.[8|66]}}

==Trivia==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=An involuntary summoning lasts a number of seconds equal to the god's level plus the vessel's level. Grull at 250 inside a level 40 mantaur therefore runs 290 seconds, slightly longer than the roughly five minutes the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook estimates.[3|32]The cookbook records an observation from Crawler Tin that gods and goddesses are Soul Armor, worn by their off-world sponsors. Later events bore the theory out.[3|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Asked outright whether he worshipped Grull, the dromedarian gate guard Clay answered by asking whether he looked like a face-painting, bull-worshipping bitch.[4|1]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Because the Scavenger's Daughter Patch is a divine item, Grull's arrival granted Carl a temporary effect: every four-legged creature present, ally or enemy, had its strongest attack doubled.[8|66]}}

==Speculation==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The Skull Empire's willingness to buy a sponsorship purely to reach a single crawler suggests deity slots function as a way for wealthy outsiders to settle personal grudges inside a game they are otherwise barred from touching directly.[3|32]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Carl's white-listing at Grull's temples is never fully explained. One reading is that the god's underlying programming, which runs independently of whichever sponsor is driving him, respects a crawler who fights the way Carl does.[7|57]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=A colder explanation surfaces later, when Grull reveals the status doubles as a tracking measure. Whether that was its only purpose, or whether both readings hold at once, is left open. His ejection from his vessel also leaves his status ambiguous: Prince Maestro was expected to return to the god within minutes under the rules requiring soul armor at all times, but whether that reunion held is not stated.[8|66]}}
