{{Quote|How is that machine greased? The universe's four lubricants. Blood, tears, taxes, and lawyers.|Quasar[5|3]}}

The Syndicate Council is the governing body of the Syndicate, the interstellar government whose member states set and enforce law across more than three billion independent star systems.[1|4] Races apply to the council for membership.[2|19]

== Description ==
Syndicate rules on independent system sovereignty give member governments wide latitude inside their own borders, and advocating sedition against one is itself a crime.[2|19] Grievances against a member government are filed with the Syndicate Court rather than with the council.[2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The government maintains a bureaucrat or a committee for every one of its moving parts, each beholden to rules that can date back tens of thousands of cycles.[5|3] Oversight of a crawl season falls to a crawl subcommittee rather than to the council, which is a considerably weightier body.[5|60] Proceedings involving a crawler may be attended by subcommittee members and corporate representatives.[5|1]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=A Prime Minister chairs the council, which seats representatives of its member governments. Sessions can be convened on an emergency basis, are conducted remotely, and run on formal procedure; a decorum sanction carried by supermajority mutes a sitting representative for the remainder of the session.[6|Epilogue] The crawl council reserves a ten-season seat for whoever wins the Twelfth Floor Ascendency battle, and thirty cycles on any subcommittee carries a pension.[6|19]
The council's ultimate instrument is the failsafe, which kills a system's star and everything inside it, and which was written for exactly the circumstances a rogue AI creates.[6|51][6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=An old rule bars anyone from holding political office while an underage member of their family holds a leadership title. Vacancies are filled by acting appointment, with a real vote held months later.[7|86]}}

== History ==
The Syndicate created the crawl.[1|43]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The Syndicate formed a few hundred cycles after the ship Escape Velocity discovered the wormhole to the first system.[3|30]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Enforcement of the Syndicate's own rules has been uneven. Kidnapping surviving natives from a crawl world through the Walk-On List has never been legal, and rights organizations have written bills in subcommittees demanding the practice be officially condemned, but nothing has come of them. The use of collected children was banned, and pregnant women along with them, only after Crawler Hoon found his own children repurposed as sentinel hunter killers guarding a floor exit.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=By the account given at CrawlCon, the original council nations tripped the primal engines by accident and set off the chain reaction that overpopulated the galaxy, then answered it by collecting the primal elements left behind on the pre-seeded worlds. The Hive later approached the council demanding the right to field test macro-AI-controlled enhancement zones; the request went to a subcommittee and was put under advisement, and the crawl followed from there.[5|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=A later ruling barred the use of reconstituted loved ones under the age of maturity, and directed that all reconstituted loved ones be used sparingly.[6|46]}}

== Story ==
===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The council first considered Carl's case after the death of Administrator Loita. The liaison assigned to the investigation recommended he be removed from the game regardless of legal cause; the council set that recommendation aside and imposed a punitive measure on Borant instead, barring third-party teleportation of crawlers for the remainder of the floor.[4|19]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The council had been indifferent to Carl until his seizure of the Gate of the Feral Gods brought him before a liaison proceeding attended by members of the crawl subcommittee. Afterward, nobody was indifferent.[5|1] As the season's AI began refusing court orders, the council ruled that no further lawsuits could name the AI as a defendant, an attempt to slow its deterioration that immediately generated a fresh wave of suits challenging the council's authority to make it.[5|60]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The broadcast of a Residual's speech from a Florida homeless shelter threw the council into disarray, with several members stranded on the planet and locked out of the internal debates entirely.[6|50] The gambit had been engineered by the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network, which wagered that the council would either provoke the AI past its limits by cutting the speech off or pull the failsafe over what it revealed.[6|51]
An emergency session was in progress by the end of the floor. Princess Formidable argued that the system had to be destroyed before the AI reached the center system, and was answered with the observation that over half the galaxy's wealth was already trapped in-system at Club Scolopendra, the Prime Minister's own children among it. The council instead backed the Valtay solution, which was to engineer an extinction event by flooding Faction Wars with mercenaries. Formidable was muted by a decorum sanction, and the seat held by the Plenty went unoccupied as always.[6|Epilogue]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Unknown to the council, the failsafe had already been quietly disconnected by the time Formidable took a Skull Empire cruiser and announced that she intended to trigger it by hand from a monitor station staged outside the AI's reach.[7|34] The council tried to cancel the warlord roundtable outright and was overruled by the AI, which insisted it be broadcast live.[7|40] Prime Minister Glory then attempted to have Carl killed using a little-known but still active control panel that let the Syndicate override showrunner-controlled aspects of the game. The attempt failed, and the AI answered with a public warning about Glory's adult children. With warships firing on one another in orbit, the council ordered the Borant headquarters to self-destruct, an order the Valtay showrunners countermanded and the staff ignored.[7|81]
A no confidence vote against Glory followed, with members openly accusing one another of being agents of the Reavers.[7|83] Former Prime Minister Fopsy was passed over because of his relationship to the Dream, and Victory, an orc judge newly eligible after the deaths of her underage relatives in orbit, was installed as acting Prime Minister pending a real vote.[7|86] Within days the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network sent boarding crews against every major ship in orbit and demanded the Syndicate end the crawl.[7|Epilogue]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The insurgents achieved total system domination in a day, leaving the surviving Syndicate staff on site working at the sufferance of an organization the council had no way to dislodge.[8|27] Victory ran the Syndicate's response from a pop-up emergency deployment bunker on the surface of Earth while local survivors shelled it daily.[8|Epilogue Interlude Prime Minister Victory]
The mantids declared their intention to leave the Syndicate hours before Hive Home went dark, and the checkpoints into the center system were overwhelmed by traffic.[8|Epilogue Interlude Prime Minister Victory] The Plenty announced that it had lost control of the tunnel network outside the center system.[8|Epilogue Interlude Sydnee Iglacia]}}

== Pop Culture ==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Among the races the council does not seat, it is regarded as an institution that protects corporations, the rich, the royal families, and the gilded homeworlds of free citizens, and nobody else. Entire systems refuse entry to the null in open defiance of Syndicate law, and the Syndicate has never cared enough to stop it.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Working aliens inside the dungeon take it as given that the council will never move against a profitable AI: the rules and procedures exist, the showrunners step back, the elites wait it out at Club Scolopendra, and the only people who get hurt are crawlers and the people who have to work for a living.[5|53]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The council's own acting Prime Minister describes the body as total gridlock, and reckons the office carries less real power than her old judgeship did.[7|86]}}

== Trivia ==
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Valtay representative to the council occupies the body of a Gleener who was once Prime Minister Glory's mother, an arrangement a fellow councilor found both inappropriate and confusing.[6|Epilogue] The decorum sanction that silenced Princess Formidable passed with 86% of the vote.[6|Epilogue]
The council has been sued often enough to generate settled caselaw bearing its name: Grixist Swarm v. Syndicate Council governs whether found loot can be taken back off a crawler once it is in inventory.[6|51]}}
