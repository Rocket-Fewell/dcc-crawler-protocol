The Nothing is an alternate dimension that serves as a prison for feral gods, demons, and other powerful beings banished from the world. Any creature that lingers within it is driven permanently insane.

==Description==
In the mythology of the setting, the Nothing predates the world itself. The pantheon banished the primordial feral gods, monstrous immortals from before creation, into the dimension at the moment they made the world, and it has served ever since as a prison for banished gods, demons, and lesser deities.[4|15][4|24][4|34] The Nothing also acts as the "inbetween" that travelers pass through when a magical gate carries them from one place to another.[4|16]

Escape is difficult: the passages into the Nothing tend to be one-way, and a trapped being can generally leave only when something tears open a way out from the other side.[4|21][4|23] Prolonged exposure drives almost any creature permanently and irreparably insane; even lingering too long while passing through, or standing too close to an open portal, is enough to taint a weak mind with feral madness.[4|16][4|20][4|23] Because of this, the escape of its inhabitants is regarded as an apocalyptic danger: a freed feral god can devastate whatever world it emerges into, and its arrival may provoke the ruling pantheon to intervene.[4|15][4|23][4|27]

The Nothing can be reached from within the Dungeon in several ways. The Desperado Club casino houses a route into it: its games of chance carry a "Nothing!" result, a losing space on the vertical Wheel of Fortune and, on the roulette table, a trapdoor that drops the player into the dimension amid ethereal screams.[3|9][4|24] The artifact called the Gate of the Feral Gods opens a doorway by tearing a hole into the Nothing, with the side effect that a feral god escapes into the world once the gate closes, and its Winding Box component can also open a passage directly into the dimension when its lid is opened, the box is wound, and a stream of charged energy is fed into it.[4|15][4|23]
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Surviving the Nothing leaves a permanent mark: any crawler or NPC who has entered the dimension and lived carries the "Damned" status. Because temples are bound by tradition to shelter the damned, that status lets its bearer open the door to any temple, a loophole crawlers have used to break into otherwise sealed sanctums.[7|52]When a prisoner does escape, the deity who originally banished them is usually the one dispatched to hunt them down.[7|64]}}
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=More specifically, the Nothing is where the Dungeon discards problematic entities, a "toss-and-forget" system that doubles as a pocket dimension woven so deeply into the setting's lore that countless storylines and artifacts depend on its existence.[8|83]}}

==Story==
===Lore===
Several of the Nothing's prisoners drive many events in the series. A demonic harem, including Slit and Gash, was captured and cast into the dimension during the original Ascendency, and every member of it bears a grudge against the minor deity Psamathe.[4|34] Psamathe herself, better known as Samantha, was banished to the Nothing by her father after she took up with an ancient king; her sand-ooze familiar, the cursed child of that union, was imprisoned alongside her.[4|24][4|34] She eventually tricked the Mad Dune Mage Ghazi into opening a portal to the Nothing so that she could escape.[4|21][4|24] The banished god of Lust, Yarilo, is another of its prisoners; Ghazi originally sought controlled access to the dimension in the hope of bargaining with him.[4|21]
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Yarilo earned his place among the prisoners at the very first Butcher's Masquerade, hosted in the Halls of the Ascendency by Apito and her husband Taranis. Drunk, he shattered the party's sacred peace by assaulting another god's familiar, and Apito banished him to the Nothing for it.[5|64][8|70]}}
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The sisters of the harem are demons of Sheol, banished to the Nothing by one of that realm's four princes;[6|61] their shared grudge against Psamathe traces back to an insult she once paid their king.[6|32] One of them, Slit, briefly escaped the dimension at kaiju size, only for Psamathe's own mother to tear her in half; another, Gash, reputed to be the cruelest of them, remains behind.[6|32][6|33]}}
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Krakaren Prime, an entity banished to the Nothing for her hunger to dominate, turns her imprisonment toward a war she believes is inevitable, brewing hell-kissed potions in its depths and smuggling her wares into the world through agents such as the merchant Paca.[7|71][7|76]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Queen Imogen's Butcher's Masquerade is protected by a celestial peace seal: the instant a guest commits a violent act, the floor opens beneath the offenders and feeds them to the Nothing, which the queen calls a lightless realm of endless torment. Every true masquerade is said to end this way, with the Nothing fed and the host's promised boon withheld.[5|64][5|66]The half-elf Signet, the queen's estranged half-sister, is seated in the same ballroom as Imogen and deliberately breaks the seal by cutting her own throat; the floor tears open and pulls her down into the dimension. As she vanishes, a hollow voice rises from the dark, promising that the harem is coming for Psamathe.[5|72]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Desperado Club casinos hold one-way entrances to the Nothing, and a gambler who loses the wrong game is dropped straight in.[6|31][6|32] When Samantha rolls into the Hunting Grounds Casino beneath the club, one such portal tears wide as it opens rather than sealing shut, and the level-225 feral demon Minge, Slit's younger sister, hauls herself out into the club.[6|32][6|33]Minge rampages across the floor hunting Psamathe and wrecks most of the club before she is finally cut down by the club's own vorpal guards.[6|33]}}

===Book 7 & 8===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=At the end of the Ninth Floor (Faction Wars), the former crawler Justice Light springs a legendary trap built around the broken Winding Box of the Gate of the Feral Gods, which by then had become a permanent portal into the Nothing. The trap folds four planes together and shatters the dimension for good.[7|87][8|28]The broken Nothing drains all at once into the Halls of the Ascendency (Twelfth Floor), Sheol (Fifteenth Floor), and Scolopendra's Lair (Eighteenth Floor), spilling every feral god, demon, and horror it held into the world and stirring the sleeping Scolopendra awake.[7|87][8|28]}}

==Trivia==
*Orthrus, the universe's "goodest boy" puppy, is one of the few creatures whose mind the Nothing could not break, his pure innocence sparing him the feral madness that claims almost everyone else.[4|29]
*The Feral Goose Mother Denise, a fifth-floor Borough Boss, lost her mind after lingering too long in the Nothing while a magical gate carried her between worlds.[4|16]
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Among the Nothing's stranger inmates is a perpetually weeping beaver god, who is kept and worn as a hat by another deity.[5|24]}}
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Orthrus did not escape wholly untouched, though: his time in the Nothing left his memory unreliable, muddying Emberus's later efforts to search the puppy's mind for clues.[6|18]The Orisha healing god Inle, one of only two of his pantheon ever imprisoned in the Nothing, clawed his own way out but emerged permanently unhinged.[6|15]Samantha describes a crocodile-headed goddess in the Nothing who runs a stall selling sushi rolled from her own endlessly regrowing innards.[6|31]}}
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The Unwashed (also called The Hag, The Stalker, or The Beautiful Place, among a thousand names) is rumored to be the personification of the Nothing and is said to be what some beings glimpse at the moment of death.[7|44][8|92]}}
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Tem, a resident of the Nothing until the dimension broke, was slain by Odette the instant he emerged; the Dungeon then harvested his striking corpse for parts, using pieces of him as the basis for several monster races.[8|32]}}
