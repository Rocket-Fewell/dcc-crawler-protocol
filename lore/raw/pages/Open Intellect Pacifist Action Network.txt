The Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network, Intergalactic NFC is a small, tax-exempt organization operating in Syndicate space and the second sponsor of Carl on the Fifth Floor. Behind its charitable registration, the Network is an activist group of former crawlers working to bring the crawl to a permanent end, and it is later re-formed as an openly militant resistance movement.

==Description==
The Network is registered as an Intergalactic NFC, or Not for Conquest, a designation meaning the organization answers to no system government and pays no taxes: the Syndicate equivalent of a non-profit.[4|7] Its boxes carry a spiral galaxy emblem with alien lettering that Mordecai did not recognize.[4|8] Leadership is credited to the CEO and president of Outreach Operations, Dr. Porthus Hu.[4|34]

Anti-crawl groups have sponsored crawlers before, but by Mordecai's account they do it only to get people talking about their cause, taking no real interest in the crawler himself. A Silver benefactor box arriving on day one, to the group's only sponsored crawler, told him this was something else entirely.[4|8]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The liaison Orren characterized the Network as an organization of former crawlers who want to see the crawl fail, and one willing to spend Carl, and the planet along with him, to achieve it.[6|51]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Its membership is drawn heavily from crawlers who survived the dungeon and then spent lifetimes working off indentureship contracts.[7|11]}}

==History==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Dr. Porthus Hu served out a long indentureship before his release.[7|Porthus] He founded the Crawler Project, a not-for-conquest dedicated to helping destitute former crawlers and dungeon employees find their footing after transition,[7|Tipid] and its campaigning eventually produced laws granting newly released crawlers automatic citizenship, free healthcare, and guaranteed housing.[7|11]
Hu then formed the Open Intellect Pacifist Network as a separate and far more radical effort aimed squarely at stopping the crawl, and it never attracted anything close to the Crawler Project's support. The Syndicate eventually accused the organization of being a terrorist group and its people scattered,[7|11] with Hu himself formally listed as missing and wanted by Syndicate Security.[7|Porthus]
It was Hu who suggested refurbishing one of the garbage freighters at Tipid's employer into a passenger liner. He put out a call for former crawlers willing to re-enter the dungeon and fight, and was overwhelmed by the response.[7|11]}}

==Story==
The Network secured Carl's second sponsorship slot after a bidding war that ran to the final possible microsecond, at one of the highest fees ever paid.[4|7] Its Silver Benefactor Box contained a single Toraline root, an alchemy material flagged Very Rare but valued no higher than unenchanted clothing, which Mordecai had never seen before.[4|8] Roughly half of it was ultimately spent on a Phase Lava Rock potion.[4|34]

Near the end of that floor the Network sent an Emergency Platinum Benefactor Box, which Mordecai estimated cost more than the GDP of some star systems and called likely the most expensive box in the game's history. It held a unique xistera extension commissioned to fit a decapitated love doll head, with Hu's name and design approval inscribed on it.[4|34]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=On the Sixth Floor the Network sent a Pawna's Cries potion and a written note telling Carl only to save the toraline. His Escape Plan skill surfaced a second, hidden message signed P. Hu: a warning that Circe Took had sponsored the deity Diwata, an instruction to pass the potion to Donut, and a method for stacking temporary boosts from three allies at once to push a skill to fifteen without anyone noticing.[5|55]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Network owned the weekly program Shadow Boxer,[6|7] and host Rosetta used Carl and Donut's taping to pass a coded message, working in a doctored photograph that pointed him back at the root the Network had sent two floors earlier. The liaison Harbinger cut the taping short, accused the two of speaking in code, and banned Rosetta from all future crawler appearances. Carl concluded from the exchange that his sponsor employed at least one, and possibly as many as three, former owners of the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook.[6|13]
On the Eighth Floor came the Network's last gift, an Emergency Gold Benefactor Box[6|48] holding a single paper flyer advertising Christmas breakfast at the Shepherd's House, a homeless shelter in Homestead, Florida, with the hour circled in red. Zev reported that the organization vanished immediately after sending it: assets frozen, the entire charity gone, everyone involved missing.[6|49]
The flyer was a trap. Orren told Carl that standing him within earshot of the Residual was meant either to provoke the liaisons into pulling him out and enraging the System AI badly enough to kill everyone, or to surface information dangerous enough that the Syndicate Council would trigger the failsafe and send the system's star nova. Either outcome would have ended the crawl for a long time. Orren believed the Network had gambled without knowing what the Residual would actually say, and he declared its people fugitives with their assets frozen.[6|51]
The organization was in fact hiding in plain sight. A converted biological waste freighter, renamed the Homecoming Queen and by then the largest ship in Earth orbit, held 50,000 former crawlers waiting for clearance. Tipid and Rosetta ran the cockpit, recruited personally by Hu on the promise of two things: "Eventual death. And eventual justice." When the AI approved their request, all 50,000 registered as troopers for the Princess Posse. The Network also footed the bill for a Celestial box sent to Donut.[6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The Network's people arrived on the Ninth Floor openly, on the standard mercenary package at level 60 and without gear.[7|11] An agent in its employ, Rectrix, held the sponsorship of the goddess Theia, an arrangement made to protect Carl at the end of the fifth floor.[7|49][7|57] Another operative, buried deep in Syndicate bureaucracy, altered Volteeg's paperwork so the system would process him as a former indentured rather than a former crawler, letting him enter Faction Wars on an enemy team without being purged.[7|Volteeg]
Rosetta's assignment was financial. Working through a private arrangement with Victory, she confirmed a transfer of stolen funds used to purchase two starships, which were to carry Hu and the families of the returning crawlers toward the Plenty system and out through the gate at the Impetus Point.[7|Rosetta]
With that plan on track, a pre-recorded broadcast from Hu announced the formation of a militant successor organization, the Open Intellect Action Network, which would move against Syndicate government properties and systems everywhere unless the crawl was stopped by the end of the season.[7|Epilogue] Of the 50,000 who came, roughly 28,000 survived to the end of the floor, and with the embargo still in place they were shipped back to the Homecoming Queen rather than home.[7|85]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The successor organization sent boarding crews to nearly every major ship in orbit and achieved total system domination within a day, the Homecoming Queen by then carrying functioning enhancement-zone weaponry. Its forces controlled who was permitted down to the surface, and the region of South Asia was considered their territory.[8|27]}}

==Pop Culture==
Viewers watching a sponsored crawler's feed see advertisements produced by that crawler's sponsors, so the Network's purchase bought it airtime in front of Carl's audience.[4|7] Hu also tracked Carl's talk show appearances as recruitment: by his account, every time Carl went on Odette's program more people signed up for the cause, though his associate countered that they were signing up for Carl's cause rather than the Network's.[4|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Network funded Shadow Boxer, a weekly program from documentarian Rosetta that told the story of Earth through interviews staged to look like ordinary conversation. It was compared to the earlier program Earth Beautiful, but was considerably rawer.[6|12]}}

==Trivia==
Mordecai's immediate reaction to the name was that there is no such thing as a non-profit religion, at least not in the legal sense.[4|7]

On opening the Toraline root, Carl thanked the mystery aliens for the yam and remarked that he was more of a mashed potatoes guy.[4|8]

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Notes are billed by the character, and the AI will rewrite anything insufficiently vague into code on its own. Sponsors are effectively forced to be cryptic, which explains the Network's habit of burying instructions inside puzzles.[5|55]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Carl's offhand remark about mashed potatoes turns out to be almost exactly the code the Network later uses to reach him.[6|13]
Later references shorten the name, dropping "Action" to leave simply the Open Intellect Pacifist Network.[6|7]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=When the group reorganized as a resistance movement, the word "Pacifist" was dropped as well.[7|Epilogue]
An unusual number of the people connected to the Network are authors of the Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook:
Porthus, second edition[7|Porthus]Tipid, fourth edition[7|Tipid]Justice Light, eighth edition[7|Justice Light]Rosetta, ninth edition[7|Rosetta]York, tenth edition[7|Epilogue]
Volteeg, author of the seventh edition, was not a member, but it was Hu who found him and pushed him to act.[7|Volteeg]
Hu never entered the dungeon at all, leaving field command to Rosetta.[7|49] He refused to tell her what the Apothecary whispering in his ear wanted, on the grounds that anything she knew could reach the local AI.[7|Rosetta]}}

==Speculation==
Hu insists Carl knows what the Toraline root is really for, but neither of them ever says what that is. How a small NFC affords deity sponsorships, a Platinum emergency box, and its own media network is raised in-universe by Hu's associate and never answered directly.[4|Epilogue]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The starships used for the evacuation were bought with stolen funds, which leaves open whether the earlier spending was financed the same way or by backers who wanted the Syndicate destabilized for reasons of their own.[7|Rosetta]
The unnamed associate who argues with Hu is never identified. Rosetta later describes the Apothecary as the one with his ear, and as someone who knows things she is not permitted to know, which fits the role that voice plays.[7|Rosetta]}}
