{{Quote|If the Orcish Supremacy is a child with a lemonade stand, the Skull Empire is the Wal-Mart corporation.|Zev[1|44]}}

The Skull Empire is one of the largest and oldest system governments in the Syndicate, ruled by an orcish royal family that claims descent from gods and royalty. The family owns the Death Watch Extreme Dungeon Mayhem production and fields the Skull Clan in Faction Wars on the Ninth Floor.

==Description==
The Skull Empire is a Syndicate system government of orcs, separate from the Orcish Supremacy, the orcish system ruled by the Tusklings. Zev ranked it among the largest and oldest of the Syndicate's governments, and warned that it is not known for measured responses to insults.[1|44] The empire spans multiple home systems, makes its money selling weapons, and was once described as a strong ally of the Valtay Corporation.[2|19] Unusually for a family so invested in the crawl, it does not typically purchase deity sponsorships.[2|Epilogue]

The empire is headed by King Rust.[1|Epilogue] His queen consort is Ugloo, and the couple have at least three children: the crown prince, Prince Stalwart, who owns the Death Watch Extreme Dungeon Mayhem production; the younger Prince Maestro, who hosted it; and a daughter who stands behind both in the line of succession. Rust funds the show with a small stipend, which Zev suspected was mostly a way to keep the two brothers occupied.[1|44][2|19][2|20]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The daughter is Princess Formidable, the youngest of the three siblings.[3|29]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The empire's holdings include the Gun-ya system and its publicly traded companies.[4|28] Whatever bound it to the Valtay Corporation had come apart by the Fifth Floor, with the two openly at odds.[4|34]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The family's money reaches into the Syndicate's justice system. Buying out one's own conviction warrant is technically forbidden, but the family franchised Maestro to a subsidiary of a subsidiary it secretly owned, a loophole open only to the ultra-rich.[5|42]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The empire holds a seat on the Syndicate Council.[6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The empire manufactures much of the Syndicate's hardware, including its security satellites, and builds a backdoor into everything it makes so its products cannot be turned against it.[7|34]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The empire's solvency is not its own. Harry Tisch, a Crest investor trillionaire, lives in the Skull Empire system because Rust used his council veto to kill an extradition request over tax fraud; the orcs routinely extend safe harbor to financial criminals in exchange for investment funds, and Tisch pays a third of everything he earns for the privilege. He quietly turned the arrangement around over the years, buying himself so deep into the empire that, by Rosetta's account, he was the only reason the orc economy had not collapsed.[8|88]}}

==History==
Prince Maestro claimed his family has a long history with the Dungeon, that they were present when the Syndicate created the crawl, and that they are "of gods and royalty." The family's Skull Clan has won six of the last ten Faction Wars and placed in the top three every time, a record Maestro contrasted against the Blood Sultanate, which he claimed is almost always the first faction eliminated.[1|43]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The empire's early expansion left marks that outlasted the conquest: during one campaign a system warlord developed a taste for the local Lepus and hunted the species nearly to extinction, and the survivors fled underground and devolved into the cornets later found as Fourth Floor mobs.[3|6]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Crawler Priestly's account in the cookbook calls the empire's brutality and sadism unparalleled and wonders how its citizens could see it and not rise up.[6|54] The orcs also have a habit of partnering: Mordecai's account of the Reavers has the two frequently allying to clear the other teams off the board before turning on each other for the trophy.[6|21]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=King Rust had not competed in Faction Wars since his own days as a prince.[7|32] Rosetta remembered one of those earlier seasons: with the fighting over, the orcs stood on the far side of the Scolopendra Club glass mocking the crawlers who had survived, and when the crawler Brooks raged at them, then-Prince Rust lassoed him with a whip and pulled him into the glass, which cut him in half.[7|Rosetta]
The run of six wins in ten ended this season, when the Princess Posse took Faction Wars.[7|Epilogue]}}

==Story==
===Book 1===
The family's Death Watch production reached Carl and Donut through an override: administrator Mukta scrapped Zev's booking of a round-table program and put the pair on the show instead because it paid a higher fee. The shoestring production leased a windowless, closet-sized rental rather than owning a trailer, and this was its first live episode, which Carl read as a sign the family had overextended itself to book them.[1|41][1|42]

The segment ended with Prince Maestro humiliated on air: Carl dubbed him "pork boy" and needled him into hurling his own guest crawlers into the show's death trap, all of whom escaped.[1|42] In the interview that followed, Maestro boasted of his family's ties to the Dungeon and warned that if the pair reached the Ninth Floor they would learn exactly what his family could do, announcing that he would meet them there as War Leader Maestro of the Skull Clan.[1|43]

Within hours, an anonymous and sexually explicit snick starring the prince went viral across the Syndicate; Zev cautioned that the empire was unlikely to find it funny, and noted that Carl's appearance fee had doubled at the empire's expense.[1|44] King Rust arrived in Earth orbit soon after and stayed conspicuously quiet, a royal spokesperson asked the public to stop calling the prince "Carl's Naughty Little Piggie" (nobody obliged), and Death Watch went into hiatus to restructure.[1|Epilogue]

===Book 2===
The humiliation metastasized. Unrest broke out in several of the empire's home systems, with citizens objecting to the royal family lavishing time and money on the crawl while neglecting their duties at home, and calling for democratic reform. Rust responded by disowning Maestro, stripping his titles, and telling his people to shut the hell up.[2|19]

Asked on Danger Zone what he made of the unrest, Carl urged the empire's citizens to rebel. Barely two minutes later, a pulse from Prince Stalwart's personal yacht struck a production trailer floating off Earth's coast, and the prince had a statement ready claiming the summary execution was justified under Syndicate rules by Carl's advocacy of sedition. It killed the wrong target: Carl and Donut had swapped trailers at the last minute with the singer Manasa, a Valtay asset, who died in the strike.[2|19]

In retaliation the Valtay destroyed Stalwart's yacht. Stalwart was not aboard, but Ugloo and the disowned Maestro were both believed to have been, and both were reported dead. The Prism Kingdom filed an immediate grievance, public opinion cast Stalwart as the villain, and Zev expected Rust would be forced to disown his eldest son as well, leaving only his daughter as heir.[2|19][2|20][2|Epilogue] Rust then spent heavily on a deity sponsorship, which Odette read as a possible move against Carl and guessed to be the war deity Grull.[2|Epilogue]

===Book 3===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=By the Fourth Floor the family had entered the Dungeon itself. Carl's party understood that Stalwart was inside, presumed to be wearing Grull's skin, and that Rust had entered as well, though nobody knew where.[3|12][3|29] Princess Formidable surfaced as the sponsor of the crawler Katia, and her first benefactor box held the Bolt of Ophiotaurus, one of only 100, which pauses a struck deity's invulnerability for fifteen seconds. Carl took the gift to mean she was no fan of her brother.[3|12][3|29]
The party had the wrong brother. When Carl finally summoned Grull, the system credited the god to Maestro, who had never boarded the doomed yacht at all: Stalwart had brought him into the Dungeon beforehand, and Maestro had been content to let the galaxy mourn him. The god manifested wearing the prince's face and his grudge, level 250 and invulnerable on that floor, taunting Carl as "piglet" before the party banished him.[3|33]}}

===Book 4===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Having failed twice in person, the brothers hired the job out. By the Fifth Floor they held all four sponsor slots between Maggie My and the crawler Chris Andrews 2, and they steered Maggie toward Algos, the god of pain, arming her with a Celestial Grenade that could summon her chosen god for sixty seconds. The plan collapsed: Formidable answered with an emergency box of ten Petrify Rock bolts for Katia, one of which dropped Maggie before she could pull the pin, and Carl carried the grenade off as salvage.[4|14][4|31]
The empire also went to court, and lost twice. Stalwart was named first plaintiff of seven factions that sued Carl and the dungeon AI to stop the feral god plan; the empire then sued alone for an injunction, undone in part because Carl's new stockholding made him a taxpayer in its own system and in part because the Valtay sent an attorney to argue against it.[4|28][4|34]
The empire's ninth-floor encampment was Carl's announced target for a feral god. Instead he flooded the city of Larracos, wiping out the faction markets the empire and its rivals had been counting on to outfit their armies.[4|33][4|34]}}

===Book 5===
{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The empire bought its way onto the hunting grounds, purchasing a license that let its hunters pursue any top-ten crawler and paying for first crack at Carl, a claim it shared with the Dark Hive.[5|15][5|22][5|25] All three hunters it sent died without accomplishing anything: two ambushed Tsarina Signet's camp beside an all-tree, which turns magic back on its caster, and electrocuted themselves with their own lightning scrolls, while the third threatened Carl with the empire's vengeance, admitted she should never have come, and was sacrificed to power Signet's summoning spell.[5|25]
When Faction Wars voting opened, the empire and the newly formed Princess Posse were the only two of nine teams to vote to strip the safety protections that keep faction sponsors from dying on the ninth floor. Carl gave the orcs a rare compliment: they are crazy and they are all going to die, but they are not chickens.[5|77]}}

===Book 6===
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Stalwart carried the empire through the run-up to the season, taking the top spot at Cascadia's fifth preproduction meeting in person and refusing even to introduce himself. Told by Donut that she and Carl had humiliated him more times than she could count, he promised to skin her alive and line his codpiece with her pelt.[6|21]
The empire also, inadvertently, supplied Carl's adjutant. Victory, an attorney and judge formerly of the Skull Empire and the half-sister of the late Ugloo, told Carl that nobody except her brother-in-law and nephews blamed him for the queen consort's death and that the relations he had killed on the hunting grounds had all been within his rights. Carl picked her.[6|36]
The Princess Posse then put the empire's own gamble to the audience, which voted 98% in favor of stripping the safety protections from faction sponsors, placing the life of every sponsor on the ninth floor, the empire's included, on the line.[6|72]
With the crawl approaching the ninth floor and the dungeon AI holding Earth's gates shut, the empire took the most extreme position on the Syndicate Council. Formidable demanded the council pull the failsafe and blow the star immediately rather than let the AI infect the center system, conceding under accusation that she would love to see her inept family deposed. Prime Minister Glory refused, citing the half of the galaxy's wealth already gathered in the system, and the council muted her by an 86% decorum vote.[6|Epilogue]}}

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The empire fielded its team as the Bone Clan, holding the map's only pre-built castle, the King's Point, atop a small lava-veined mountain. Within hours it announced a truce with the Operatic Collective that seven more teams joined; the eight named themselves the Bloc and declared war on Team Retribution and the Princess Posse. Stalwart was the clan's warlord and led the Bloc, while Maestro sat the season out, occupied driving Grull in the Celestial Ascendency.[7|1][7|14][7|32] The army favored size, with rhino guards, missile-carrying camels, and a platoon of explosives-expert basher trolls, and the clan rounded up the former crawlers scattered among the mercenary forces to feed them to Rust's Ring of Divine Suffering, vaulting the king several levels.[7|32][7|34][7|Volteeg]
Rust then rode onto the Princess Posse's border himself to request a registered parlay, and what he brought to it was not a threat. He told Carl that his daughter had seized one of his own cruisers and meant to trigger the system failsafe and kill everyone, and asked Carl to plead with her to stop for the sake of the millions of humans still on the surface. He allowed that all three of his children were idiots, warned that the daughter was the one to worry about, and said he would rather any further humiliation fall on him than on children with long lives ahead. Carl negotiated a withdrawal instead.[7|32][7|34]
Rust never reached his lines. Rosetta surfaced behind him under an invisibility cloak and took his head off with a whip, then drained his credit chit through a bank transfer that Orren put at a few billion credits and could not be clawed back.[7|34][7|35][7|36][7|Rosetta] The death cost the orcs their king but not their place in the game, since Stalwart, not Rust, was the clan's warlord. He spent his brief reign insisting the clan remained stronger than ever, until his own war mages killed him: when Operation Ruin opened, a bombing run set off the mountain beneath the King's Point, and Stalwart teleported clear only to land among four of them, who tore him apart while he knelt. His last words were that his sister had been right.[7|37][7|39][7|43][7|61][7|62]
Formidable made good on her threat, taking her father's cruiser toward the Venus monitor station and broadcasting the empire's backdoor secret to the whole Syndicate before the AI plucked her off her own ship and dropped her on Earth's surface.[7|34][7|37][7|Volteeg] The disclosure ruined the family: Epitome Tagg told the warlord council that the Dream had already deactivated every piece of Skull Empire hardware in its fleet, that Rust's personal reserves had been drained through his own lax security, and that the empire faced a civil war.[7|43]
The empire's own adjutant had been working against it the whole time: long before she was chosen, Baroness Victory had traded the codes to Rust's accounts to Porthus of the Open Intellect Pacifist Network in exchange for the downing of an orc yacht carrying Stalwart's six children, one of whom had become king the instant Stalwart died. With the throne no longer held by a minor nephew, Victory herself was named the Syndicate's acting prime minister.[7|Rosetta][7|86][7|Epilogue]}}

===Book 8===
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=As the eleventh floor opened, the royal family was gone from the running of the empire altogether, and whoever had taken over moved quickly: with Empress D'Nadia dead, the Skull Empire swallowed the real estate holdings of a Prism-controlled group, then tripled the rent on the historian Sydnee Iglacia's habitat in the Makoka Cloud and stopped including oxygen in it.[8|Epilogue Interlude: Sydnee Iglacia]}}

==Pop Culture==
Carl coined the prince's nickname himself, calling Prince Maestro "pork boy" to his face on live tunnel, and the snick that followed made "Pork Boy" and "Carl's Naughty Little Piggie" both trend across the social media tunnels within hours.[1|42][1|44] Zev suggested any official demand that the public stop would only backfire, comparing it to "the mudskipper thing all over again."[1|44]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=A second snick, "Carl's Naughty Little Piggie Goes to Market," landed minutes after the fourth-floor Death Challenge and climbed just as fast, though it was more obviously a fake.[3|Epilogue]}}

==Trivia==
* Zev summed up the empire's aesthetic in a line: "They're orcs. Everything they do is mawkish."[1|44]
* Prince Maestro used "Skull Clan" for his family's Faction Wars entrants and "Skull Empire" for the system government; Carl split the difference, calling it "the Skull Clan or Empire or whatever."[1|43][1|44][1|Epilogue]
* The empire's royals are coarse-haired, wild-boar-like orcs, darker and bristlier than the bright-pink tusklings.[1|42][2|19]

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=The clan's orcs wear their flags on their backs like feudal Japanese warriors: black vertical rectangles with a shimmering silver orc skull.[7|32]}}
