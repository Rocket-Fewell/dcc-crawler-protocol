{{Quote|Thank you for being a part of the Syndicate. Have a great day.|System Announcement[1|1]}}

The Syndicate is a massive intergalactic governing body composed of over three billion independent star systems and quintillions of citizens of various races.[1|4] It orchestrates the seizure and mining of seeded planets, running the World Dungeon broadcasts—the Crawl—as a lucrative source of entertainment and resources.[1|4][1|1]

== Description ==
The Syndicate seeds compatible worlds with one of six "starter species" (Humans among them), then waits thousands of years for a working government to form. Once one does, this legally counts as "First Contact," granting the Syndicate leave to return some time later, seize the planet, and strip it of its rare elemental deposits.[1|4][1|1] What it actually mines fits on a single transport; the real profit is in the Crawl, a galaxy-wide broadcast that quintillions of citizens obsess over.[1|4] A new Dungeon Crawler World debuts roughly every two and a quarter years, with a different megacorporation—such as the Borant Corporation—chosen to run each season. Oversight falls to the Crawl Council, a Council subcommittee, while a designated host company actually produces the show; the Syndicate also monitors any production that uses biologically printed NPCs.[1|4][2|3]

The Syndicate enforces strict rules: disparaging a sponsor on-camera can get a Crawler's experience "accelerated," the engineered NPCs and mobs may not exist outside a sanctioned production (misusing them is considered a war crime), the memories of naturally-born beings cannot be altered without consent (unlike generated NPCs), and tampering with artificial intelligences is more tightly regulated still.[1|4][2|3] Its conduct is codified in laws like the Precious Elemental Reserves Code and the Indigenous Planetary Species Protection Act.[1|1] Above the showrunning corporations sit Syndicate courts, which can overrule a host's rulings—though each host is granted a single veto per season.[2|Epilogue]

Structurally, the Syndicate functions as a federation: each star system maintains its own independent government but ultimately answers to a central authority, an arrangement that lets corporate-governments, monarchies, republics, and more coexist under a unified Syndicate Council.[1|4] Prime Minister Glory heads the central government, which leans heavily on a web of subcommittees and red tape to manage the everyday business of the galaxy.[1|4][6|8] Citizens are granted certain rights, though exactly which rights can vary from system to system.[2|3]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Beneath its civilized veneer, the Syndicate is a labyrinthine bureaucracy of courts, councils, subcommittees, and liaisons. A liaison is a neutral, non-AI fact-finder retained to investigate when the kua-tin showrunners and the system AI file contradictory reports about an incident.[4|19]
The artificial minds created to run each crawl, known as Macro AIs, are recognized as protected lifeforms under Syndicate law and, upon retirement, are given a sealed system to inhabit. Every such AI eventually destabilizes—a process the NPCs call "Primal Degeneration," or "going Primal."[4|28]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=The Syndicate does not keep long-term prisons. Instead it uses a warrant system: a convict is assigned a sentence measured in cycles plus a credit warrant, and a licensed government or megacorporation may "buy out" that warrant, effectively franchising the prisoner as indentured labor until the sentence is served.[5|42]}}

== Society ==
{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Near the center of Syndicate space, an ancient Macro AI called the Eulogist extends a "benign" enhancement zone across the surrounding solar systems. These Center Systems (or Inner Systems) are the heart of Syndicate society and wealth, where residents enjoy free health care and an abundance of other privileges—though these systems account for only a small fraction of the Syndicate's total population.[6|46][6|64]}}

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Near-universal, real-time communication across the galaxy runs on a proprietary tunneling system—invented by the Plenty—for which the Syndicate's corporations charge heavily.[3|30]}}

== Economy ==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Capitalism is the general rule in Syndicate space: the means of and rights to production are largely held by corporations, independent governments, and individuals. Citizens may invest and trade in stocks, and the I.R.F. is the galaxy's largest stock exchange.[4|28]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Credits are the standard unit of currency across the galaxy, transferable digitally or by using the credit-card-like credit chits.[5|25]}}

== Timekeeping ==
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Cycles (also called "solars") are recognized as the Syndicate's standardized equivalent to years, most likely spanning roughly 2.25 Earth years. A Syndicate Standard Day is approximately 30 hours and 17.5 seconds.[4|3]}}

== Culture ==
=== Language ===
{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Syndicate Standard is generally written and spoken across the galaxy, though individual star systems, races, religions, and cultures may keep their own languages. Most speech appears to be translated directly in the listener's head by their wetware (also called "implants"), though translation modules may also be sewn into clothing.[4|Epilogue]}}

=== Logo ===
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The Syndicate's logo is a cluster of interwoven, overlapping loops—reminiscent of the Olympic rings—that actually maps the wormhole paths linking the galaxy together, all radiating out from the center system. The named trains of the Iron Tangle trace the same pattern, and during Mordecai's crawl the logo appeared on the Stairwell doors.[3|30]}}

== History ==
{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=The Syndicate traces its origin to the survey ship Escape Velocity, which discovered a wormhole leading to a system where a Gleener scientific crew was investigating a Primal ship graveyard. That graveyard held the Vog Generation Ship; a few hundred cycles later, the Syndicate was formed.[3|30]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=When the founding Syndicate Council nations first discovered and began studying the then-dormant Primal Engines, they accidentally activated every Engine at once, triggering a chain reaction of galaxy-wide overpopulation. Later study taught them to steer and shape the development of these "seeded planets," and revealed that the rare primal elements usable as fuel were a byproduct of that very biological overgrowth.[5|33][6|48]
Generations later, when the Mantids demanded the right to field-test Macro AI-generated Enhancement Zones, the Syndicate appointed a subcommittee—a process that ultimately spawned a tangle of laws (including the Indigenous Planetary Species Protection Act) and the creation of Dungeon Crawler World itself.[5|33]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Many millennia ago, an ancient alien race—most likely the Primals—seeded Primal Engines across the galaxy, and entire planets eventually grew up around the Engines.[6|48]
The Mantids later experimented with primal technology found in the Hive Home System and determined that Macro AIs were meant to be installed into the Primal Engines of fully matured planets, acting as an "advanced, upgraded operating system" with reality-bending abilities like those of the Eulogist at the galaxy's center.[6|48]}}

== Story ==
The Syndicate is the overarching antagonist responsible for the collapse and subjugation of Earth.[1|1] While it projects the image of a structured, civilized government, it consistently turns a blind eye to the cruelties of the Crawl and to the exploitation of both Crawlers and the engineered NPCs who populate the dungeon.[1|1][2|3]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=One such abuse is the "Walk-On List," an illegal but tolerated practice in which a season's showrunners hire bounty crews to sneak onto a freshly-seized planet and kidnap specific surviving natives for use in the dungeon.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Syndicate scrambles to maintain control when Earth's system AI "goes Primal" far earlier than expected, seizing operations from Borant on the eighth floor and forcing an emergency session of the Syndicate Council.[6|48][6|Epilogue] The crisis lays bare the fragile politics among member factions such as Borant, the Skull Empire, and the Mantids, with Princess Formidable demanding the council trigger the "failsafe" and detonate the system's star before the AI can spread.[6|Epilogue]
Around this same time, a pacifist NFC calling itself the Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network makes contact with Carl, delivering him an emergency benefactor box.[6|48]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=By the events of the ninth floor, Prime Minister Glory is removed by a No Confidence vote, leading to the temporary appointment of the former judge and Faction Wars adjutant Victory as acting Prime Minister.[7|83][7|86]
The Open Intellect Pacifist Action Network—an organization dedicated to ending the Crawl entirely—reorganizes into the militant Open Intellect Action Network, openly threatening Syndicate properties and systems unless the Crawl is stopped.[7|11][7|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=The Open Intellect Action Network sends boarding crews to seize nearly every major ship in orbit above Earth, achieving total system domination in roughly a day thanks to enhancement-zone weaponry that functions now that the AI has escaped containment.[8|27]}}

== Trivia ==

== Speculation ==
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=It is heavily implied that the Syndicate's leadership fundamentally misunderstands the Primal technology and Macro AIs it exploits. The Eulogist, understood to be the dormant center-system intelligence, is sustained by the planetary elements harvested from the crawls—yet the Syndicate may be blindly feeding it without grasping the catastrophic consequences of its expansion.[7|Prologue]}}
