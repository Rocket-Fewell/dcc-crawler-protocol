Beatrice, usually called Bea, is an Earth character, the ex-girlfriend of Carl, and the owner of the Persian show cat who becomes Donut. She never enters the Dungeon, but is referenced frequently by both protagonists.

==Description==
Beatrice is described entirely secondhand, and the two accounts do not agree: Donut remembers a benevolent authority, while Carl remembers someone who lied to him.

Her main interest was the competitive cat show circuit. Her family raised and showed Persian cats, and she took Donut to an event almost every weekend, accumulating Best-in-Show and Best-in-Breed ribbons, trophies, and roughly fifty framed photographs.[1|1] She wanted to be on television and tried out for numerous reality shows,[1|4] and she was drawn to multi-level marketing schemes, once filling a closet with leggings she meant to sell.[1|32] She banned smoking indoors[1|1] but kept candles throughout the apartment, and worried the cat would set herself alight.[2|5] She insisted Donut be fed only wet food[1|5] and rationed her yogurt,[1|9] and she told people Carl had served in the Navy when he had been in the Coast Guard.[2|23]

Her own account of the relationship comes from a Thanksgiving conversation Carl overheard at her parents' house. Answering her mother's advice that a woman must appear less intelligent than her partner, Beatrice called Carl uncomplicated, honest, unwilling to fight even when she picked one, and financially steady, and said plainly that she loved him and knew she kept ruining it.[2|12]

Donut remarks that Beatrice dated three men at once, Carl among them,[1|20] and mentions that a man used to come by while Carl was at the gym.[1|10] Carl privately believed she was preparing to give Donut up and replace her with a younger show cat.[1|34]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=Carl later recalls the argument that confirmed it. Once a litter of her mother's cats was weaned, Beatrice meant to keep two kittens and return Donut to her parents to be sold as a show-quality breeder. Her answer to his objection was financial: she asked whether he knew what a former international grand champion would fetch, said the cat was past her prime, and asked why he cared, since he did not even like her.[3|18]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=She once made Carl sleep on the couch for a week without explanation, having read an article claiming that big fights strengthen relationships and sometimes prompt proposals,[4|13] and she told him she thought she was pregnant roughly ten times without ever being pregnant.[4|15]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Donut adds that Beatrice ate when she was upset and made herself sick afterward,[5|50] that she dosed the cat with castor oil before judging so Donut would empty her bowels on schedule,[5|24] and that she once wrecked the kitchen during an argument after Carl suggested she give up on selling leggings.[5|34]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Before the collapse, a coworker named Sally sent Carl a friend request and asked him out. A friend of Beatrice's closed the deal by sending the supervisor nude photographs pulled off the internet, and Sally, a single mother, was fired that day. Carl learns of it only on the eighth floor.[6|47]}}

==Appearance==
Beatrice has a faded tattoo on her lower back, a Persian cat surrounded by uneven wisps, based on Princess Chonkalot, Donut's long-dead grandmother and Beatrice's own childhood cat. Carl considers it a bad tattoo and once joked that it looked like a stoned Ewok; Donut regards it as a masterpiece.[2|6]

An Instagram photograph taken during the trip that ended the relationship shows Beatrice sitting on the lap of her ex-boyfriend Brad,[1|1] in a bikini, laughing, and taking the selfie herself.[2|17]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=A pair of movie-star sunglasses is pushed up on her head in the photograph. When Donut receives similar goggles from a sponsor, she says they make her look like Miss Beatrice.[3|15]}}

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=After the collapse she is covered in cuts and scratches across both arms and her face, inflicted nightly by a half-feral cat she refuses to stop holding. She does not appear to notice them happening.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=She is described in detail on Odette's program: thin, with long straight hair, freckles, and pouty lips, and having lost a great deal of weight. Carl thinks she looks hollowed out and aged by about ten years, her skin startlingly pale. She wears a glittering red gown scooped below the waist at the back, which leaves the Princess Chonkalot tattoo on display, a pair of very tall heels she stumbles in, and an Aries anklet Carl notes she has never been without.[5|19]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=She needed contact lenses, and Carl's 20/20 vision used to make her jealous.[8|35]}}

==History==
Beatrice shared a two-bedroom apartment in Seattle with Carl for at least a year before the collapse, one bedroom given over entirely to Donut's ribbons, trophies, and photographs.[1|1] Her parents live on the far side of the Cascades[1|1] in a house with about ten other Persians.[2|12] She was friendly with a neighbor who owned a cocker spaniel named Angel,[1|13] and on much worse terms with another neighbor, Marjory, over Marjory's unneutered yellow tomcat.[2|19]

Days before the transformation, Beatrice left for a New Year's trip to the Bahamas without telling Carl that her ex-boyfriend, Brad, was going along.[1|1][2|17] Carl called her, ended things over the lie rather than the infidelity itself, and agreed to look after Donut until she returned, since a winter storm had closed the mountain passes.[1|1] He assumed she had been asleep in her hotel room and collected instantly.[1|4]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Her parents had money; her father was a lawyer, and the two of them tithed twenty percent of their income to their church and got angry with Beatrice when she did not donate herself.[4|32]
Carl's assumption about her fate is incorrect. After the breakup call she broke down so badly that her friends pushed her and Brad out of the suite four days early, and the two were waiting for rides outside an airport parking garage when the world came down. A dungeon entrance opened nearby, but Beatrice would not move, so Brad stayed with her. They eventually joined a survivor settlement called New Queen Anne, where she did no useful work and was widely disliked. She insisted on returning to the old apartment, found a crater, and spotted a starving yellow tomcat in the tree outside the window: Gravy Boat, the stray she had twice called animal control on. She picked him up and would not put him down, telling Brad that the cat had been a lot smarter than she was, and every night she held him until he clawed his way free, then sang him a short lullaby of apology.[4|Epilogue]
A bounty crew working the season's Walk-On List raided the camp while tracking the tomcat, and the null hunter recognized her on sight. Before they could take her, Lexis intervened, shot Brad and left him to bleed out, and put Beatrice in a bounty cube on Odette's order.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Odette later explains why. Beatrice was a named bounty target, wanted so she could be sold to Borant, transformed, and installed as a country boss,[5|18] specifically as Queen Imogen of the high elves, the sixth floor's country boss.[5|20] Odette had her lifted off the planet first, both to protect her investment in Carl and Donut and to convert the rescue into leverage over Borant, intending to tell Carl nothing until he was free.[5|18] Officially, she is a full-rights Syndicate citizen who will remain in an undisclosed location until the crawl is over.[5|19]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Her parents' house is in Yakima, where she spent Christmas before driving back to pack,[6|50] and the Bahamas flight left on December 26.[6|1] Her mother is among the faces used to build a composite boss on the eighth floor,[6|46] which Carl takes as confirmation that she died in the initial collection.[6|47]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=A recreation of the old apartment building on the tenth floor prompts Carl to reframe the feud with the first-floor neighbor. Marjory was being harassed by an abusive ex in violation of a restraining order, and police were at the building three days running because of it. Beatrice's reaction was to be annoyed that they had blocked her from getting inside after a cat show, to telephone her father and tell him to do something about it, and to start complaining that the building sat in a crime-ridden slum.[8|76]}}

==Story==
Beatrice takes no direct part in the crawl, but she remains a recurring subject for both protagonists.

Donut initially refuses to accept that she is gone. She calls her "Miss Beatrice," describes her as her agent, and estimates that the Dungeon will have shrunk enough by the fifth or sixth floor for them to find her.[1|12] She invokes Beatrice as an authority in arguments, from household policy on cigarettes[1|10] to how displeased she will be about the Desperado Pass tattoo.[1|19] Carl worries instead that Donut absorbed her attitudes wholesale.[2|12] On the third floor, Donut's Gold Fan Box turns out to contain a framed print of the Instagram photo, voted in by viewers who understood exactly what it meant to Carl; he braces for the sting and finds it is no longer there.[2|17]

{{Spoiler|book=3|spoiler=On the fourth floor, Donut realizes that a cruel corporate NPC reminds her of Beatrice's mother.[3|18] Later, after risking herself to come back for Carl, she brushes off his objection by asking who he thinks she is, Miss Beatrice: the first use of the name as a byword for abandonment rather than as an appeal to authority.[3|34]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Odette breaks the news of her survival minutes before airtime, then asks permission to bring her onto Dungeon Crawler After Hours with Odette. Carl refuses; Donut overrules him.[5|18] Beatrice, visibly drugged and working from a cover story she has not absorbed, cannot follow the questions, is astonished to discover the cat can talk, and asks Carl why he is not wearing pants. Donut wishes her a good life, leaves a short list of instructions for any future cat, then accuses her of having made her feel loved while planning to give her away regardless, and finishes by informing her that Carl is her person and always was.[5|19]
Donut uses the nonsense lullaby a drunken Beatrice used to sing to her, about a good girl who is like a root beer float, to carry the charm spell All Eyes on Me.[5|74] It is the same tune, words changed, that she sang nightly to the tomcat in the survivor camp.[4|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=On the eighth floor, one head of a composite boss belongs to Asher, Carl's half-brother, whose existence Carl learns of that day.[6|46] Donut recognizes the face but not the relationship: she had seen the boy's photograph on Beatrice's phone and heard her discuss him regularly with her mother, and had taken him for a nephew. Carl concludes that his stepmother, Tami-Lynn, wrote asking for help and that Beatrice intercepted the message. It is the first time he is angry at her, rather than at himself for being fooled.[6|47]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=By the ninth floor, Donut invokes her routinely and without much heat, as a benchmark for betrayal and beauty routines,[7|5] and as a yardstick for how bad Carl looks: shaking like Miss Beatrice during pumpkin spice season,[7|14] or resembling her after she started taking diet pills.[7|41]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Facing down a naga attorney who has claimed Carl as a husband, Donut allows that Beatrice was bad but had an eye for exceptionality and cannot be accused of aiming low, at least on the cat show circuit.[8|Interlude Chandra] Her verdict on the new wife is shorter: she thought Beatrice was bad, and Carl clearly has a type.[8|1]}}

==Trivia==
*It was Beatrice who shortened the cat's registered name to "Princess Donut"; Carl trimmed it further to just "Donut."[1|1]
*She called Donut "Princess Money Pit," because the household spent more on the cat than on rent. Donut remembers it as "Princess Money Bags."[2|14]
*She called animal control twice on the neighbor's non-pedigreed yellow tomcat, Gravy Boat, and once had a screaming fight with the neighbor about him.[2|19]
*She rewatched Gossip Girl repeatedly, clutching Donut and sobbing at the season five car accident.[1|36]
*Her taste in music ran to country.[1|45]
*Donut quotes a phrase Beatrice used to use, "Time to pay the Daddy tax," and confirms it referred to Carl.[1|45]
*Beatrice's name is likely inspired by the muse-like character from Dante's Divine Comedy, a series of three epic poems that begins with Inferno, the story of Dante Alighieri's descent through nine layers of Hell.
