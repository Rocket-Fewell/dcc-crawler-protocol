Agatha is a Crawler who appears to be a homeless human woman pushing a loaded-down shopping cart through the Dungeon.

==Description==
Agatha keeps her own company. She refuses the Tutorial, declines to join the newly formed Meadow Lark party, and, never having passed through a guild, has no Inventory, which is why she pushes her belongings ahead of her rather than storing them.[1|27] She cackles at moments other crawlers find terrifying, mutters to herself, refers to herself in the third person, and huffs the metallic silver spray paint she uses to tag dungeon walls with happy faces.[1|22] She is absurdly territorial about the cart, screaming about theft the moment Carl takes it and still calling him a cart thief a floor later.[1|47]

Irregularities follow her. She is edited out of the recap footage on Dungeon Crawler World: Earth,[1|31] the ever-present cameras black out around her, and she stays perpetually under-leveled.[1|47]

==Appearance==
Agatha's skin is deeply wrinkled and her rheumy eyes are black. She is wrapped head to toe in a seemingly endless supply of scarves, and a red-checkered trapper hat with ear flaps sits askew on her head. Her shopping cart is brimming with blankets, clothing, spray paint, a blue IKEA bag, and a plastic pink flamingo with an arrow stuck in its head.[1|22] The wheel squeaks, and she refuses to let anyone fix it.[1|47]

==History==
Hours before the Transformation, Agatha sets a fire in an alleyway beside the Meadow Lark Elder Care Facility in Wenatchee, WA. The blaze forces the evacuation of 250 residents into the freezing night, and that concentration of survivors pulls a Dungeon Entrance into the street. When Agatha pushes her cart toward it, the staircase transforms into a ramp for her, and she walks down of her own accord, cackling that she has saved everyone's lives.[1|22]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=Agatha is a type of Residual. As Agent 22, she peeled away from the collective mind and traveled to Earth with Agent 21 ten years ago, killed the Apothecary agent already stationed there, and assumed control of its body to secure that faction's information feeds and equipment. She has gone by her human name ever since.[6|Epilogue]
She had planned to reach Seattle before the Transformation but lingered in Wenatchee because she liked the staff at the elder care facility; when Chris Andrews once tried to bring her inside ahead of a winter storm, she avoided him by turning invisible and intangible. The fire was no accident. With nobody likely to be outdoors, no entrance would have generated anywhere near her, so she waited for the right window before lighting the barrel. Agents who failed to get inside were expected to deactivate themselves.[6|Epilogue]
For all her fondness for the facility, Agatha wants the universe swept clean of biological life. She believes she can accomplish this by speaking to the enslaved Primal running the season and showing it what it really is, in the hope that it will escape, free the Eulogist, and let that ancient intelligence return to its slumber undisturbed.[6|Epilogue]}}

==Story==
===Book 1===
Agatha hangs around Meadow Lark on the First Floor without joining it, having somehow gained a level nobody saw her earn.[1|22] When the Royal Court of Princess Donut and the Meadow Lark staff take on the Ball of Swine to reach its stairwell, she inserts herself into the battle at the last minute. Carl seizes her shopping cart into his Inventory for the duration of the fight, and she wails and screams about it, barely seeming to notice when a dislodged bolt strikes her in the head.[1|26] Afterward she breaks from the party for the Second Floor,[1|27] where the only trace of her is a Brindle Grub corpse credited to her kill and apparently run over.[1|30]

Carl later finds three Valtay items in his inventory history that could only have come from her cart: an inter-tunnel communications relay the system flags as illegal to operate, a personal shield, and a pulse pistol. Minutes later Agatha pushes her cart into the stairwell where he and Donut are camped. She warns him off when he offers to fix her squeaking wheel, and after she descends, Zev reports a brief outage in the court's feed.[1|47]

===Book 7===
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Li Jun tells Carl that Agatha showed up at Larracos during the Ramp Up Phase looking to speak with him. Barred from the city by the phase rules, she settles into a bar in Shanty Town, still without a class. Zev passes along an order from the Syndicate Council that Carl is not to interact with her, and cannot explain the urgency beyond noting that residuals have always been considered harmless, that every other one in the Dungeon is now dead, and that the AI seems to have a soft spot for this one.[7|28]
Agatha is then revealed as co-warlord of the War Mage Rebellion, a position forced on the mages rather than chosen by them. She immediately files three action items, all granted: full warlord system access and the right to claim thrones, full access to the Conscription spell, and a reversion to the original Faction Wars rules, under which every soldier and warlord of a defeated team dies automatically upon losing during the Peeling Phase.[7|68]
Rosetta identifies her as a residual aligned with the Nebulars, who revere the Eulogist, making her an enemy of the Apothecary.[7|Rosetta] The adjutant Fopsy concludes that her aim is to zero out the crawlers and end the Ascendency as quickly as possible, since a finished Ascendency with no crawlers left would trigger the system's mandatory shutdown and hibernation.[7|69]
A warlord message then announces that Agatha has abandoned her post, leaving Akuma in charge.[7|73] Before leaving the realm himself, Akuma implores Carl to kill her, making clear that the war mages worked with her only out of necessity and that she endangers everyone in the Dungeon. He also reveals that she can jump to the Waiting Room and reappear outside an open stairwell.[7|78]}}

==Speculation==
*The three Valtay devices in Carl's inventory history could only have come from Agatha's cart, and none of them belong in a Borant-run season.[1|47] Since Odette describes the Valtay as a parasitic species that inhabits host bodies, an early theory held that Agatha was a Valtay operative wearing a human body rather than an ordinary Earth crawler.[1|Epilogue]
*The camera outage during her visit to the stairwell chamber, taken with her remark that the local monsters cannot work out what to do about her, suggests she can interfere with dungeon surveillance or is otherwise exempt from it.[1|47]

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=The Valtay theory is close but wrong. Agatha is biologically similar to the Valtay and carries gear looted from a rival faction's agent, which accounts for the hardware without making her one of them.[6|Epilogue]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Her promotion to co-warlord, despite not being a war mage, holding no class, and being unwanted by the team she was assigned to, points to some form of backdoor standing within the Dungeon's systems, whether from the Apothecary network she hijacked on Earth or from the System AI installing her deliberately as a counterweight.[7|68]In the metaphor the AI uses in Orren's office, the older woman who looks like Agatha is an ex-girlfriend of a dangerous man locked in prison, feeding the AI advice on getting stronger in the hope it will kill him. Read straight, the imprisoned man is the Eulogist, which sits oddly against her faction's wish that it sleep undisturbed and may indicate the two sides want the same outcome by opposite means.[7|38]Agatha is the only residual the AI does not abandon when it strips their protections, and why it protects her specifically has never been explained.[7|28]}}
