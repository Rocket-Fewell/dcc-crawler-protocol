{{Quote|Together, we will burn it all to the ground.|Hidden welcome message of The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook[3|7]}}

The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook is a Unique, Heirloom book item that has passed in secret from one Crawler to the next since it was first generated into the system in the fifteenth season of Dungeon Crawler World. Disguised as a humble recipe book, it is in truth a covert archive of survival knowledge, recipes, strategies, exploits, and warnings left by previous crawlers, and the founding artifact of the crawlers known as Anarchists.[3|8]

==AI Description==

Book.  The Dungeon Anarchist’s Cookbook by Anonymous.This is a unique item.Chicken and Goblin recipes galore! But it’s more than that, too. Each recipe is accompanied by a hilarious tale by the anonymous author, recounting some of the zany and madcap misadventures they experienced gathering these mouth-watering recipes. Fun for the whole family! This book is a real hoot.[3|7]

==Description==
The cookbook is a thick volume, roughly the size of a large dictionary, with a nondescript cover that bears an anarchy symbol in place of a title. It is one of two single books offered to Carl on the Prize Carousel on the Fourth Floor; as the carousel turns the book glints, though Carl cannot tell whether the shine is magic or simply light catching its gold-leafed pages.[3|7]

After he selects it, Carl receives the same public AI description everyone else sees. It is only when he cracks the book open that his Escape Plan Skill triggers and the true welcome message appears, an address to the crawler explaining that the book has found its way to them for one purpose only, ending with the promise that together they will "burn it all to the ground."[3|7]

The 24th Edition identifies the book's full title and subtitle, which advertises potions, explosives, traps, secret societies, dungeon shortcuts, and (in its own words) much more.[3|8]

Originally generated into the system during the fifteenth season of Dungeon Crawler World, the cookbook passes only to crawlers who meet specific criteria and conditions. When a crawler inherits it, a second page is added to their scratchpad, a place to record discoveries, experiences, recipes, secret societies, thoughts, and anything else that might help future holders. The book frames this as the only price for its knowledge: to keep reading, an owner must pass their own discoveries on.[3|8]

The cookbook takes a new form each time it enters the Dungeon, usually tailored to its current crawler. Neither the audience nor the show's producers can see its contents, though the System AI is aware that it exists. The book breaks no dungeon rules, but if anyone at all comes to suspect that it is more than it appears, the crawler loses access to it, and it vanishes from their inventory upon death or retirement.[3|8]

==Appearance==
To Carl, the cookbook appears as a thick, dictionary-sized tome with a hard leather cover that lacks a title but bears a prominent anarchy symbol, an "A" within a circle. Its pages are gold-leafed, and the book catches the light with a distinctive glint.[3|7]

The visible text inside seems to contain only chicken and goblin recipes. When Carl opens it, his Escape Plan Skill activates with a haptic buzz and hidden text reveals itself on the otherwise blank pages.[3|7]

==Operation==
Carl accesses the true contents when his Escape Plan Skill activates while he reads, and the book also grants him an extra, secret page in his mental scratchpad. To keep access, the owner must pass on knowledge by writing on that page, which then becomes part of the next edition.[3|7][3|8]

Because the showrunners and viewers cannot see the hidden text but can observe a crawler's actions, the cookbook warns the owner to "become an actor": any recipe, secret, or strategy learned from the book must be presented to the outside world as something the crawler discovered independently, and the owner is cautioned not to spend too long staring at the pages. One note by Drakea, author of the 22nd Edition, advises reading in saferoom bathroom stalls, claiming the Syndicate is barred from observing toilet stalls (though not the rest of the bathroom), while admitting the author is unsure whether the rule is actually true.[3|8]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Carl hides cookbook-derived strategies by steering conversations so they appear to come from ordinary experimentation or from Mordecai. While experimenting with explosive recipes on the Fifth Floor, he notes that he is getting better at lifting recipes from the cookbook and making it look like Mordecai came up with the idea.[4|17]
The cookbook warns against using the Marketplace Interface while carrying it. Batbilge (12th Edition) records that the marketplace tags the book as a unique heirloom worth roughly 50 million gold, unlike the owner's normal inventory display, and fears the interface gives a third party too much access to a crawler's inventory; Allister (13th Edition) adds that this appears to be Batbilge's final entry and advises leaving the book behind before using the marketplace. Carl follows the warning, planning to set the book aside before checking item values.[4|3]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=Carl extends the hidden-collaboration idea using a method tied to the Coffee Shop Author's Kit, circulating compartmentalized planning notes among trusted allies leading up to the Butcher's Masquerade. He frames the notes as documentation for future cookbook readers, invoking Drakea's earlier request that authors describe their deadly plans in detail.[5|63]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Former owners can often recognize a current cookbook holder even after the book has moved on. Dante notes that it is obvious when someone has had it, and Volteeg observes that Carl's behavior makes his possession plain. By the Ninth Floor, former authors such as Rosetta, Tipid, and Justice Light are deliberately feeding Carl information with the clear intent that he record it in the cookbook.[7|Dante][7|Volteeg][7|49]}}

==Contents==
Carl describes the cookbook as organized into curated chapters. Documented categories include potion recipes, explosives, traps, secret societies, dungeon shortcuts, monster weaknesses, power supplies, portals, mapmaking, inks, and stairwells, alongside personal stories, warnings, coded messages, and commentary from previous crawlers.[3|8][3|13]

The final and largest section gathers notes that do not fit neatly elsewhere, arranged by edition. Some authors filled pages and pages; others left only a few lines.[3|8]

==Story==
Carl obtains the 24th Edition during a Prize Carousel event on the Fourth Floor, triggered when viewers vote for his Platinum Fan Box to let him choose his own prize. The host is Chaco, whose unexpected appearance prompts Mordecai to attack him and earn a seven-day saferoom timeout.[3|7]

Among the nine options, the untitled anarchy-symbol book catches Carl's eye with a faint glint, and he reasons that it may connect to his Compensated Anarchist class. The public description makes it look like a joke prize about chicken and goblin recipes, leading Princess Donut to complain that he should have taken the enchanted chaps instead. When Carl opens the book, his Escape Plan Skill reveals the hidden introduction; he confirms that a second tab has been added to his scratchpad and, unable to reveal the book's true nature, begins reading in private.[3|7][3|8]

Carl soon uses the cookbook to justify and develop the "backpack method" for Katia. By crafting an equippable open-top backpack and loading it with metal, he lets Katia add over a ton of mass to her transformation, while presenting the trick as something inferred from the armorer workshop menu rather than from the book.[3|13]

He also draws on the cookbook's NPC chapter after meeting Growler Gary, a gnoll bartender who regenerates after death. Remembering entries by Azin and Drakea about indestructible NPCs, Carl concludes that Gary likely has or knows something important; Gary's regenerating hands later become the key to activating the Interdiction Rapid-Response Railway Repair Carts during the Iron Tangle crisis.[3|26]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=On the Fifth Floor, after his party buys a Marketplace Interface upgrade for their personal space, Carl heeds Batbilge and Allister's warning and avoids using it while the book is on him.[4|3]
Carl uses the cookbook's recipes to infuse a Hobgoblin Smoke Curtain with an invisibility potion, creating a Hobgoblin Disco Ball, a multi-hued smoke device that conceals movement and reacts to light and sound. As always, he pretends to have stumbled onto the recipe through experimentation.[4|18]
He likewise applies Coolie's 19th Edition notes (on off-world program appearances, holographic admins, and lingering forcefields) to engineer the assassination of the dungeon admin Loita during an off-world taping. Afterward Carl writes his first known cookbook entry, thanking Coolie and dedicating the act to Coolie and Bonnie, and ends it with the word "One."[4|18]
Later on the Fifth Floor, Carl receives Drakea's Enchanted Kerchief of Disorder from a gold boss box, an item tied to Drakea, author of the book's 22nd Edition. Recognizing the name from the cookbook, Carl feels his bond with the book's lineage of past crawlers deepen.[4|28]}}

{{Spoiler|book=5|spoiler=During the lead-up to the Butcher's Masquerade, Carl adds a major 25th Edition entry describing the party as a trap and preserving his planning notes for future readers, explicitly citing Drakea's request as his reason to document every step. He names survival as the central goal but lists broader objectives: solving the Tina quest, killing Eva, saving the changelings and getting them into the castle, confronting Queen Imogen, killing Lucia Mar, and ensuring no hunter escapes the floor alive.[5|63]
These notes also preserve the ballroom mechanics his team worked out and the ruse they used to draw Queen Imogen out of hiding before the party.[5|63][5|64]}}

{{Spoiler|book=6|spoiler=On the Eighth Floor, during an interview, Rosetta uses a fabricated image and a false story about "milk and potatoes" stew to pass Carl a coded message. He links the phrase to Milk's cookbook entries and the toraline root vegetable supplied by his sponsors, concluding that the Open Intellect Pacifist Network likely employs multiple former cookbook owners. The liaison Harbinger cuts the interview short, bans Rosetta from future interviews, and threatens to remove both her and Carl.[6|13]}}

{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=On the Ninth Floor the cookbook becomes an active intelligence conduit. Using information from Rosetta, Carl records how first-time deity worship forms an invisible light connection carried by Bijou fairies, and his group puts the recorded theory to use in tracking how the warlord Vinata slips undetected into Club Vanquisher. By this point several former authors, including Volteeg, Justice Light, and Rosetta, have returned for Faction Wars and deliberately feed Carl material to record.[7|49][7|Volteeg][7|Justice Light][7|Rosetta]}}

{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=On the Tenth Floor, while interrogating the war mage Akuma, Carl learns that a former crawler named Herot, whom he recognizes as the author of the 16th Edition, is still alive. Akuma describes Herot as a skink who developed the Worn Path Method (which helps reused mobs and NPCs realize their artificial nature) and explains that the dungeon is a modular, reusable framework installed planet to planet before the macro AI is paired with the Primal Engine.[8|38]
Herot and another former crawler, Menerva, once gained permission to build the Seventeenth Floor, a deadly maze concealing the Pineapple Cabaret, a pocket-dimension refuge they built to shelter awakened NPCs and mobs. After the approving showrunner was murdered, the pair became forgotten ghosts in the system, quietly running the project for hundreds of seasons.[8|38]}}

==Trivia==
* The cookbook was originally generated in the fifteenth season and first awarded to Porthus on the Ninth Floor, disguised as a blank sketchbook.[3|8]
* The book's true contents are invisible to viewers and showrunners, but not to the current System AI.[3|8]
* The book warns that the owner must act as though they discovered its recipes and secrets independently.[3|8]
* Drakea wrote the 22nd Edition and died during the final Naga-run season. Carl notes that only two other crawlers held the book between Drakea and himself, and finds Drakea's hate-filled notes oddly comforting.[3|8][3|13]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=Allister's version of the cookbook was not a book at all but a large deck of T'Ghee playing cards.[4|3]
The book's ordinary inventory value and its marketplace value differ dramatically: Carl's normal interface shows it as nearly worthless, while the Marketplace Interface tags it at around 50 million gold.[4|3]
To protect the book's secrecy, Carl must constantly pass off his complex strategies as either his own inventions or ideas suggested by Mordecai.[4|17]}}

==Speculation==
* Because the System AI is aware of the cookbook's true nature, it may intentionally generate the book for crawlers whose classes, skills, or anti-authoritarian playstyles suit the goal of causing maximum chaos for the showrunners.[3|8]
* The glint that drew Carl to the book on the Prize Carousel may have been a deliberate intervention by the System AI, or a latent magical feature meant to ensure the book reaches someone able to use it.[3|7]
* The warning that the book is legal but self-erases if discovered suggests the System AI is exploiting a loophole in the rules rather than openly breaking them.[3|8]

{{Spoiler|book=4|spoiler=The marketplace warning may imply that the cookbook's hidden status depends not only on viewer censorship but also on avoiding outside economic and inventory systems that could flag its true value.[4|3]}}
{{Spoiler|book=7|spoiler=Because former owners can recognize one another, the cookbook may leave behavioral, strategic, or ideological traces even after it disappears, making it as much a social lineage as an item.[7|Dante][7|Volteeg]}}
{{Spoiler|book=8|spoiler=Herot's survival and the existence of the Pineapple Cabaret suggest that cookbook authors may have been building infrastructure-level resistance projects across many seasons, not merely sharing notes between crawls.[8|38]}}
