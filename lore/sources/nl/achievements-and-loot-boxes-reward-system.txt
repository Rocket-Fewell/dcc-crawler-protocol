Achievements and loot boxes: how the dungeon's reward system works
Anyone reading book 1 of Dungeon Crawler Carl sees a steady stream of notifications from page one: achievements, each one tied to a loot box. Together, they form the reward system the System AI uses to steer crawlers through the dungeon.
Achievements: more than just a pat on the back. Achievements are notifications generated by the System AI to reward, mock, or warn crawlers for specific actions. Most early achievements (starting with the very first, “You’ve Killed a Mob!”) mainly hand out a loot box and are designed to get crawlers experimenting with the mechanics quickly, so they can make an informed class choice by floor 3 at the latest. Others work as warnings instead: a heads-up that you’re on the wrong track, or a subtle nudge to remember a particular tactic.
Experience points: only after that first kill. Without the “You’ve Killed a Mob!” achievement, a crawler doesn’t even start earning experience points. Once that’s unlocked, party members share experience: whoever contributes most to a fight (including support roles like tanking or healing) gets the largest share, but simply being present during a battle already earns a small amount. Carl also suspects the System AI quietly tweaks things behind the scenes, sometimes to keep the game “fair,” sometimes to punish crawlers who exploit loopholes.
Loot boxes: dozens of types, six tiers. Loot boxes come in dozens of varieties, each tied to a different kind of achievement: ordinary Loot Boxes for achievements, Boss Boxes for defeating a boss, Quest Boxes for completed quests, and Fan or Benefactor Boxes sent as gifts from viewers and sponsors. Every box type also comes in six quality tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Legendary, and Celestial), with each tier dramatically rarer and more valuable than the last.
Celestial: the very top. Celestial is the highest tier, and that’s no exaggeration: across the entire history of Dungeon Crawler World, only 2,145 Celestial boxes have ever been awarded. Their contents are often tied to gods, sometimes granting god-like power, sometimes as a reward for a quest line a god set in motion.
