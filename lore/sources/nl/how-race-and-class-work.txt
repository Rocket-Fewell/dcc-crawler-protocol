# Race and Class: how the System AI's character system actually works
Spoilers through book 2. One of the first big System moments in Dungeon Crawler Carl is the race and class selection, the foundation a crawler’s whole run in the dungeon is built on. Here’s how it actually works.

When you choose. Only once the tutorial floors are behind them and a crawler reaches the third floor do they officially select a race and class. Level-ups are still tracked before that, each one grants three stat points, but those points can’t be manually distributed until then.

Class: dozens of options, three recommendations. The System AI builds a list of thirty or more class options per crawler, based on their stats, race choice, Desperado Club membership if any, certain gear, and other tutorial-floor choices. It then recommends three that fit best. With that many crawlers and that many possible combinations, a typical season of Dungeon Crawler World sees tens of thousands of different classes chosen in total.

Race: from human to something else entirely. Same structure here: hundreds of options, three recommendations, each with a short description, a summary of the stat benefits and drawbacks, and a spinning 3D model of what an average member of that race looks like. Roughly 80% of crawlers keep their original (human) race. Those who switch get a race that’s roughly balanced on stats, and where the stats don’t quite balance out, the AI often compensates with a skill, a racial spell, or a perk like night vision.

Carl as an example. After the tutorial, Carl picks the Primal race and a fully unique class: Compensated Anarchist, which the System AI describes as a “trap-layer, bomb-maker, and social media dynamo.” That season, he’s the only crawler to pick it: a good illustration of just how personal the combination of choices and AI recommendations can get.
