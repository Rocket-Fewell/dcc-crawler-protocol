# Crawler Creation

::: epigraph The Intake Kiosk
Describe yourself in one line. Be honest. We have cameras. We have so many cameras.
:::

Every Crawler starts the same way: on a molded plastic bench, in a booth under a buzzing sign that reads **CRAWLER INTAKE: PLEASE HAVE YOUR SOUL READY**. The kiosk screen has a smeared touch panel, a slot that dispenses things, and a camera that never blinks.

::: readaloud
You come to on a bench designed by someone who hates spines. Fluorescent light. The smell of carpet glue and old fryer oil. A touchscreen chirps a jingle composed by a committee of dentists and fills with friendly letters: **WELCOME, VALUED CRAWLER!**
:::

This chapter builds a level 1 Crawler at that kiosk, one decision at a time. It follows the order of the **/create** screens, so a player at the keyboard and a player at the table meet the same questions in the same order, with the same names.

## Before You Start

**Creation is standard 5e (2024) character creation at level 1.** You choose a class, a background (which gives ability score increases and an Origin feat), a species, ability scores and equipment. Nothing is removed and nothing is simplified. The Crawl adds only paint and paperwork: a hostile customer-service tone, a few System names for things, a private survey, and a story in which your species stays hidden until Floor 3. The System keeps a **Fan Profile** on every Crawler; players never see it and never set it (S12 is retired). Floor 1 later grants **Midterm Onboarding** (level 2 at the Green Room) — that is play, not creation. The rest of the standard level track (arrival bands, Floor 3 and Floor 9 end-boss bumps, Floor 18 at level 20) is in Chapter 1.

**Time.** Plan on about 20 minutes per Crawler. A table of new players should plan on 45. Everyone can build at once, because each player works alone.

**What you do not get at creation.** No magic items. No loot boxes (the Welcome Box arrives at the first saferoom). No sponsors, no Broadcast Name, no Ratings above Unknown, and no multiclassing at level 1. A level 1 Crawler is a baseline 5e hero holding some very bad gear.

::: system
Before we begin, please accept our Terms of Service. They are four hundred thousand words long and the only one that matters is "lethal." Tapping DECLINE will also continue, but louder.
:::

### Two Ways to Build

1. **At the table.** The GM (or a veteran player) walks the table through the steps below, and each player fills in a character sheet.
2. **With /create.** Many tables run creation as a private command in their chat server. The command walks one player through **sixteen screens** and writes nothing until the last one. This chapter follows that flow, and the screen number appears in each section heading in case you are using it.

Either way the rules are the same. The flow is only a faster way to make legal choices.

### How /create Behaves

- **Everything is private.** Only you see your screens. Nothing is saved to the campaign, and nothing is posted anywhere, until you confirm on S17.
- **Back never loses your answers.** Edit jumps to any screen from the review and returns. **Restart** wipes your answers after a confirmation, except your comfort lines, which stay.
- **Help** gives a plain three-line explanation, with no new rules in it. **Pick for me** appears on the mechanical screens and chooses a legal default; the System says what it chose, and you can change it.
- **Your draft is saved after every screen.** If you stop, run /create again and it offers to resume where you left off. Drafts that sit idle for 14 days are archived, not deleted. [VERIFY: the 14-day figure is a tool design default, not a game rule.]
- **Cancel build** shreds the whole draft, including any rolled ability scores. Nothing has been registered, and a new build starts fresh.
- **Table settings are not part of the flow.** Death Mode, the Floor 1 Reprieve, dice and timeouts are set by the table owner before anyone builds. The flow only shows them to you.

## Table Settings (S1 and S2)

### What S1 Shows You

S1 is a read-only welcome. It shows the **session** you are joining, the table's **Death Mode** (**Costly Revives** by default: revival is rare and costly; see Chapter 1), the **Comfort** level (**Ludicrous** by default), the **Floor 1 Reprieve** (**ON by default** for Season Zero Floor 1; a table may turn it off; it never covers a collapse death), and how many of the four Crawler slots are taken ("Party: 2 of 4 active Crawlers"). If you already have Crawlers on file, it also shows how many and which one is active.

You will see four choices: **Accept**, **Decline** (both continue), **Use a pre-built**, and **Read the Terms**. Reading the Terms shows you nothing, because the Terms are not shown. It does earn you a snark achievement.

::: achievement Don't Skip Intro
You asked to read the Terms of Service, all four hundred thousand words of them. The System did not know that was possible.

Reward: Nothing. The Terms remain unavailable. The System admires your commitment anyway.
:::

### Comfort: Lines and Veils (S2)

S2 asks you, privately, what you do not want in your game. The System will not ask why, will not joke about your answers, and will not show them to anyone else.

- A **line** is a topic that never appears, anywhere.
- A **veil** is a topic that may exist off-screen but is never shown or described. The System fades to black.

You may list a handful of topics, each marked line or veil. You can mark a topic **Share with the GM** if you want the GM to see it in review; the default is private. The word **"cut"** works at any time during play and needs no setup. The System obeys every entry from any state, and a topic you did not share never appears in any review view or on your card.

S2 can be reopened later with /create comfort, with no change to your sheet. The hard floors from Chapter 1 apply regardless and are not listed as options.

## Name, Look and Hook (S3 and S4)

### Name Tag and Pronouns (S3)

Your **name** is for the paperwork. It is not your Broadcast Name; that is earned on Floor 3. Rules for the field:

- 2 to 30 characters: letters, digits, spaces, apostrophes, hyphens and periods.
- No @, #, slashes, markdown or line breaks.
- Not a reserved word: **gm, ai, system, party, all, everyone.**
- Unique at the table, ignoring capitals. If your name sits inside another Crawler's name (Horn and Hornet), you are asked to confirm, because the tools match names by exact text first and then by partial text.

Your **pronouns** are optional: he/him, she/her, they/them, or something you type. Leaving it blank reads "unspecified."

### Look and Hook (S4)

Two lines, both required.

- **Look:** what you look like *now*, in whatever you were wearing when the world ended. Everyone on Floors 1 and 2 looks baseline human. Species features (horns, tails, scales) stay out of the look, or you can keep them as a costume.
- **Hook:** one sentence on why you are still funny or dangerous.

Keep each to a line. Good hooks name a habit or a fear, not a power.

::: sidebar Example
**Look:** Fifty-year-old school-bus driver in a reflective vest and orthopedic sneakers. **Hook:** Has driven forty seventh-graders through a blizzard; you are not scary.
:::

### The Intake Tag and the Pending Handle

On confirmation the System assigns you a cosmetic **Intake Tag**, such as `CRAWLER #0417-K` (four digits and a letter), used on every Floor 1 popup. The tag has no mechanical effect. Until Floor 3, your sheet reads **Broadcast Name: PENDING**. We return to the handle in the Backstory section.

## Ability Scores (S8)

Your six ability scores decide how long you last. This screen is called **The Six Numbers (Ability scores)**.

### Factory Preset (Standard Array)

Assign 15, 14, 13, 12, 10 and 8 among your six abilities, one each. The total is 72.

### Layaway Plan (Point Buy)

You have **27 points**. Every score starts at 8 and can rise to 15 before any bonus.

<<TABLE:pointbuy>>

The most a 27-point buy can ever total is 75, for example 13, 13, 13, 12, 12 and 12.

### Dice Gamble (Roll) {{HOUSE}}

::: house Roll Method
Roll is a table option. It is on by default and your GM can switch it off. If it is off, you will not see it on S8.
:::

Here is how it works.

1. **The System rolls three arrays.** Each array is six scores. Each score is **4d6, drop the lowest die**, rolled in the open and logged. Every die is recorded.
2. **You keep exactly one array**, and only an array whose six scores **total 73 or more** may be kept. There is **no upper limit**.
3. **If none of the three qualifies, the System rolls all three again.** It repeats until one does. If a build somehow takes more than ten rounds, the System stops and sends you to the other two methods. [VERIFY: the 10-round safety stop is a tool design default.]
4. **You assign the kept scores freely.** Each score goes to one ability, once. Equal scores are separate entries.
5. **There is no voluntary reroll.** Your rolled rounds are saved. Back, Edit, Restart and changing methods all show you the same rounds. The only exception is **Cancel build**, which shreds the whole draft.

A single 4d6-drop-lowest score ranges from 3 to 18, so a background +2 can never push a score past 20.

::: sidebar The Odds
About 56 percent of rolled arrays total 73 or more, and about 8 percent of rounds produce no keepable array at all. The standard array totals 72, and point buy can reach 75.
:::

**Example round.** The System rolls three arrays.

| Array | Scores | Total | Result |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| A | 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 9 | 73 | Keepable (total 73) |
| B | 16, 12, 12, 11, 10, 9 | 70 | Not keepable (total 70, needs 73 or more) |
| C | 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 10 | 70 | Not keepable (total 70, needs 73 or more) |

Only array A can be kept, so you keep A and assign. Had A been 72, all three would have been thrown away and rolled again.

::: system
Three arrays. Keep one that totals above 72. The numbers are not wrong. They are just yours.
:::

## The Survey (S13)

S13 is a **short, private customer satisfaction survey**. Your answers are private notes. The System does not read them aloud, does not put them on your card, and does not share them with other players. You may **pass** on any question, or skip the whole screen. **S12 is retired** on purpose (the old public-tags screen); the numbers stay so the tools never renumber. From your private notes and from play, the System builds a **Fan Profile** that stays **hidden** — players never see it, never set it, and never ask for a specific reward. Nothing in the survey is required, and nothing in it is a test.

## Backstory and Handle (S14)

The screen is **Your Sob Story and Handle Request**. It asks for two things.

- **A backstory**, two sentences or so. This is *public*. The System reads it aloud when it introduces you, so make it worth reading.
- **A requested handle.** What do you want the audience to call you? This is a request, not a promise.

The System logs your request and sets your sheet to **Broadcast Name: PENDING**. During Floor 1, the audience may start a nickname for you based on something you did. On Floor 3 the System mints your real Broadcast Name *with you*, and may honor your request, mangle it, or overrule it. The name the audience gives you is the name you earned.

> Requested handle: "Shadowblade." Logged. Laughed at. Pending.

## Standing Orders (S15)

This is the **Autopilot Memo**. Write what your body should do while your brain is in the bathroom.

- **Standing Orders.** One line of default tactic. Used when a turn times out or when you go on autopilot. With no orders written, your Crawler simply takes the Dodge action.
- **Standing Reactions.** One line. Defaults to "Opportunity Attacks: on."
- **Signature attack** (optional). A name for your best trick. It must be a real attack or spell you actually have.

Good orders are specific: *"Shoot the nearest enemy with my shortbow from cover; drink a potion under a quarter of my hit points."* A timed-out turn never spends limited resources unless your orders say so.

## Several Crawlers, One Active

A player may create **as many Crawlers as they like** (the soft guideline is ten; the GM may allow more). Each `/create` makes a new Crawler. At the table, though, you play **exactly one at a time**. This is the rule that keeps four-player parties four players.

### Active and Benched

- **Active Crawler.** The one in play. It counts for the party, takes one of the table's four slots, earns credits, XP and achievements, and receives loot and boxes.
- **Benched Crawler.** Everyone else you have on file. A benched Crawler is inert: no slot, no credits, no XP, no loot, no boxes and no trades. You cannot move items or credits between your own Crawlers. Using benched Crawlers as mules is not allowed, and the GM may refuse any swap that smells like it.
- **Your first Crawler is active automatically.** Later Crawlers start benched.
- **Every Crawler belongs to one campaign** and never moves to another.

### Swapping

The active Crawler is **locked for the whole session**. It changes only at **session zero or between sessions**, never mid-session, not even in a saferoom. You ask with `/crawlers`, which lists your Crawlers (name, class, level, active or benched, floor) with links to their sheets. It files a *request*; the GM approves and runs the swap, and logs it. You get **one swap per window**.

A Crawler swapped in keeps its own level, kit and credits as filed. If it is behind the party, it is brought up to the party's lowest level at the next saferoom. A swapped-in benched Crawler does not get the gear package or Silver loot box as late-entry compensation that a brand-new replacement gets.

### When a Crawler Dies

Death is permanent (see Chapter 1). During the session you cannot bring in a benched Crawler. You either sit out, or, if the GM offers it, you run a GM-assigned companion until the next between-session window. At that window you may swap in a benched Crawler as your replacement.

### /delete

`/delete <name>` removes one of **your own** Crawlers. You must type the Crawler's exact name to confirm. The active Crawler cannot be deleted while a session is open. Deleting refunds nothing and un-spends nothing; its credits, gear and boxes leave with it. A dead Crawler can be deleted. The GM logs each deletion and nothing more.

::: system
Crawler deleted. The System has filed your regret under "Later."
:::

### Sheet Channels

Every Crawler has its own text channel in the **Crawler Sheets** category, named `#<player>-<crawler>`. The card in it updates as you play.

## Pre-Built Crawlers

New to 5e? On S1 you can pick **Use a pre-built**: twelve ready-made concepts, one per class, with a legal array assignment and standard kits already chosen. A pre-built fills the species, class, background, ability, skill, equipment, spell and orders screens. You still do your comfort lines, name, look, hook, survey, backstory and the review, and you can **Edit** anything that is pre-filled. A pre-built never skips review or confirmation.

## Worked Example: Tamsin Vale

Here is the whole flow for one Crawler, from first screen to locked sheet.

- **S3 and S4.** Name: **Tamsin Vale**. Look: *mall-security vest over a hoodie, one shoe untied.* Hook: *has told four hundred people "no running" and been ignored by all of them.*
- **S5 Species:** **Canadian (Human)**. Resourceful, Skillful, Versatile.
- **S6 Class:** **Off-Duty Bouncer (Fighter)**. Fighting Style: **Bubble-Wrap Enthusiast (Defense)**. Weapon Mastery: greatsword, flail, javelin.
- **S7 Background:** **Black-Friday Mall Security (Soldier)**. Raise Strength and Constitution: +2 Strength, +1 Constitution. Origin feat: **Drive-Thru Fry-Station Veteran (Savage Attacker)**. Versatile feat: **Night-Shift Insomniac (Alert)**.
- **S8 Scores:** Factory Preset. Base STR 15, DEX 13, CON 14, INT 8, WIS 12, CHA 10. After the Soldier increases: STR 17, DEX 13, CON 15, INT 8, WIS 12, CHA 10.
- **S9 Skills:** Soldier gives Athletics and Intimidation. Fighter picks Perception and Insight. Human Skillful gives Stealth. Languages: Common, Common Sign Language, Goblin.
- **S10 Equipment:** Package A for the class (chain mail, greatsword, flail, eight javelins, Dungeoneer's Pack, 4 credits) and Package A for the background (spear, shortbow, arrows, a gaming set, Healer's Kit, quiver, traveler's clothes, 14 credits). Total credits: **18**.
