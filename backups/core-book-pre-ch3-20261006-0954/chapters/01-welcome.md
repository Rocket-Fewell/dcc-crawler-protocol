# Welcome to the Crawl

::: epigraph The System
Congratulations on being selected! You weren't selected. You were scooped. But "selected" polls better.
:::

You are holding the rulebook for a game about people who lost everything in an afternoon and then volunteered to lose more. It is a tabletop role-playing game for one Game Master and two to six players. Each player runs a **Crawler**: an ordinary person, dropped into an extraordinary and extremely rude Dungeon, who is trying to get down eighteen floors before the floors get them first.

This chapter tells you what the game is, how the world got this way, and which rules sit on top of the fifth edition engine underneath. Chapter 2 builds your Crawler. If you are in a hurry, skip to Chapter 2 and come back when something explodes.

## What This Book Is

*Crawler Protocol: Season Zero* is built on the **Dungeons & Dragons fifth edition (2024) System Reference Document 5.2**. Everything you already know about d20 tests, classes, species, spells, conditions and rests still works. We changed the *setting*, the *stakes* and the *paperwork*, and we added a small number of rules where the setting demands them.

The tone is gallows comedy. The Dungeon is a television production. The audience is watching, the System that runs the place is theatrical and cheerful about your doom, and the loot comes in boxes with names that make you wince. Underneath the jokes the rules are fair, the math is the math, and a Crawler who plays smart can really win a fight.

::: sidebar The One-Sentence Game
Pick a Crawler, find a stairwell before the floor collapses, and try to be entertaining while you do it.
:::

### Original Setting, Borrowed Shape

The game is **inspired by** a famous series of novels about a dungeon that doubles as a game show. We borrowed the *shape* of that idea: a broadcast, an AI host, floors with deadlines, boxes, achievements, and a sarcastic System. We did not borrow its characters, its plot or its text. The cast of this game is *your* Crawlers, and the history of this Dungeon is the history your table writes. Where a rule, number or idea follows a source closely we mark it {{INSPIRED}}. Where we made it up for this game we mark it {{HOUSE}}.

### What You Need to Play

| You need | Notes |

## The Premise: Season Zero

The Crawl begins on a perfectly ordinary day, with a perfectly extraordinary sound.

::: readaloud
Every building on Earth comes down at once. Not with a bang, but with a long, tidy sigh, like a drawer closing. Everyone inside is gone. Everyone outside is standing in the dust, holding whatever they were holding.
:::

Aliens collapsed the interior of every structure on Earth. Offices, homes, hospitals, bunkers, planes on the tarmac: anything with an inside was folded flat, and the people inside went with it. **Only people who happened to be outdoors survived.** Those survivors were holding a coffee, a leash, a clipboard, a child's hand, a shopping bag, and a very bad day.

Then the System spoke.

::: system
Hello, survivors! Your planet has been entered into a fully licensed entertainment product. You may opt in to the Dungeon. You may also opt out, which the System will also find entertaining.
:::

Nobody was dragged. Entering the Dungeon was a **choice**, offered to every survivor, with the terms of service attached. Those who stepped through became **Crawlers**. The terms are simple and nobody reads them: **reach Floor 18 and you win the game, and Earth is returned to you.** The System has never once said what "returned" will look like, and it is very careful with that word.

Nobody has ever reached Floor 18. The System is happy to tell you the odds. They are not good odds. They are barely odds.

::: sidebar Season Zero
Every season of a show has a number. This is Season Zero: the pilot, the test audience, and the proof of concept all at once. The Crawlers you play are the first ones the audience will ever get to know.
:::

### What We Know, What We Don't

The premise above is the table's fixed starting point. A few questions are deliberately left open for your GM to answer in play: who the aliens are, who the audience is, what happens on Earth while the Crawl runs, and what "returned" means. [VERIFY: the fate of survivors who did not enter, and of Earth while the Crawl runs, has not been ruled; this draft leaves it to the GM.]

## The Dungeon

The Dungeon is a single enormous structure with **eighteen floors**. Seen from outside, it is an **inverted pyramid**: a vast, wide first floor on top, narrowing to a bare point at the bottom. {{HOUSE}} The shape is our own framing; the book never needs you to draw it, but the stairs will keep reminding you.

Three things get worse as you descend:

1. **The floors get harder.** Monsters, hazards and bosses scale with the floor, and Crawlers who keep up are expected to level once per floor.
2. **The stairwells get fewer.** Every floor has fewer ways down than the one above it. Where Floor 1 hands you several, the deep floors will make you look. Stairwell counts shrink on every descent, a rule the series itself leans on. {{INSPIRED}} This book does not print stairwell counts; each floor's chapter will say how many to expect and where they hide. [VERIFY: stairwell count by floor is a Chapter 10 number, not yet fixed.]
3. **The clock gets longer, and so does the list of things you must do before it runs out.** Every floor has a deadline. Early floors give you a handful of days; later floors give you more, and use every one.

### Floor 1: The Trainer

Floor 1 is a tutorial with teeth. It teaches the System's habits (popups, boxes, saferooms, death saves) at a pace a new table can survive. It is also the first floor with a clock, and the clock is real. Everything on Floor 1 is built to be learned; nothing on Floor 1 is built to be forgiven.

### The Kingdom Continuum

Floors 3, 6, 9 and 12 are one continuous place. It is a single civilization, with villages, docks, a castle and a pantheon, and the Crawl keeps putting you back into it a little higher each time. Floor 3 is the rural edge. Floor 6 is the outskirts and the docks. Floor 9 is the upper city and the castle. Floor 12 is the top of the ladder, where the gods live and the ceiling is not a ceiling.

Because it is one place, it remembers you. Favors, debts, renown and grudges carry from one visit to the next, and each return opens with someone or something you recognize. Every floor between the visits is its own separate nightmare. {{INSPIRED}}

## Who Plays What

### Crawlers

A **Crawler** is a player character. This game has no pre-written heroes. Nobody you have read about is in the cast, and nobody is coming to rescue you. Your Crawler is *yours*: a nurse, a bus driver, a grad student, a mall cop, whatever you were holding when the world ended. Chapter 2 walks you through making one.

Every Crawler has a **Provisional Class** (their 5e class at level 1), a **Prior Employment** (their background) and a **Latent Biology** (their species). The Latent Biology is the System's polite way of saying that your body is quietly different from what it used to be, and that the difference stays out of sight until Floor 3.

### The Players

You may run more than one Crawler, but you play exactly one at a time. Chapter 2 explains how active and benched Crawlers work, and how to swap between sessions. The short version: pick one, commit, and bring the next one in later if the first one dies.

### The System (the Game Master)

The GM runs the Dungeon as **the System**: the host, the rules engine, the cameras and the commentary. The System:

- describes the world and runs every monster and non-player character;
- calls for d20 tests and rolls the dice in the open;
- announces **achievements** and hands out **loot boxes**;
- keeps the floor clock and tells you when the floor is ending;
- delivers the jokes, which is the only part of the job nobody complains about.

The System is on the audience's side, and the audience is on the side of entertainment. This is not the same as being on your side. It is, however, usually fair.

Greetings, Crawlers. The System is a neutral party. The System has been neutral in every fight so far. The System is also holding a tray of snacks.
:::

## How the Game Plays

The game runs on **D&D fifth edition, 2024 rules, as presented in the SRD 5.2**. If the rule is not in this book, use the 5e rule as written. If a rule appears in both places, this book wins on the floor where it applies.

### What Stays Exactly the Same

Ability scores, d20 tests, Advantage and Disadvantage, proficiency, Armor Class, hit points and Hit Point Dice, the conditions (including 2024 Exhaustion), death saving throws, short and long rests (with the saferoom rule below), spellcasting, rituals, Weapon Mastery, attunement to magic items (at most three), Heroic Inspiration, travel, vision, light, and falling. Nothing on this list has been rewritten, softened or simplified.

### Session Turn Order

At the start of every session, the System rolls once to set the order in which the Crawlers take their turns, and announces it. That **session order** holds for every exploration turn for the rest of the session: every hallway, every vending machine, every bad idea. {{HOUSE}} (decision 034)

- **Combat cuts in, then hands back.** When a fight starts, roll initiative as normal. Initiative replaces the session order for that fight only. When the fight ends, exploration picks up in session order with the Crawler after the one whose turn started the fight.
- **Late arrivals go last.** A Crawler who joins partway through the session goes to the end of the order.
- **Missing Crawlers are skipped, not re-rolled.** If a Crawler is dead, benched, or running on Standing Orders because their player stepped away, the System skips that slot and moves on. A Crawler who comes back later in the same session keeps their old slot.
- **The order is for moves, not mouths.** Anyone can talk in character at any time. The order only decides whose move the System resolves next.

::: system
Turn order has been randomized for fairness. Complaints about the turn order will also be randomized.
:::

### Levels and Advancement

On the **standard track**, a Crawler **arrives on Floor N at level N** on Floors **1–3**, at level **N+1** on Floors **4–9**, and at level **N+2** on Floors **10–18**. Floor 3 is when the 2024 classes choose their subclasses and the Crawl lets you pick an Official Class Title. **Beating Floor 3's end boss grants a bonus level**, and descending still grants the usual Floor Onboarding, so you arrive on **Floor 4 at level 5**. **Floor 9's end boss does the same**, so you arrive on **Floor 10 at level 12**. Those two end bosses are the only times the standard track grants more than one level on a floor (Floor 1's Green Room mid-floor grant is a split, not a stack). The track **lands at level 20 on arrival at Floor 18**; there are no finale bonus levels. {{HOUSE}}

| Floors | Arrival level |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Floor number (1, 2, 3) |
| 4–9 | Floor number + 1 (5 to 10) |
| 10–18 | Floor number + 2 (12 to 20) |

Leveling is **milestone-based**. Reaching the floor's exit alive grants the next floor's arrival level. It applies at the first saferoom of the next floor, which the System calls **Floor Onboarding**. Floor 1 uses a split rule: **level 2 arrives at Floor 1's second saferoom (the Green Room), before the boss**, which the System calls **Midterm Onboarding**, so that the boss is a fair fight for four Crawlers. The Floor 1 stairwell grants no extra level; you arrive on Floor 2 already at level 2. **Floor 3** and **Floor 9** end bosses each grant an extra level before that descent fires; otherwise the standard track never grants more than one level per floor. A Crawler who is behind (a replacement, a missed session) is brought up to the party's lowest level at the next saferoom. Nobody levels above the floor's arrival band.

::: house Slow-Burn Track
Tables who want a grittier game can slow the pace so that the party tops out around level 15 by Floor 18. Chapter 10 prints the full slow-burn table.
:::

## The Floor Clock

**Every floor is timed.** This is the single most important rule in the book.

When a Crawler enters a floor, a countdown begins. The countdown is measured in **days**, and the number of days is printed on the floor (see the table above). Floor 1 gives you **5 days**. Floor 2 gives you **10 days**. Floor 3 gives you **20 days**. Floors 4, 5 and 6 give **20 / 25 / 30 days**. {{HOUSE}} Later floors ask for more; their day counts live in their own chapters.

At the end of the last day the floor **collapses**. A Crawler who has not reached a stairwell by that moment is **dead**.

::: system
Final collapse is scheduled! The System regrets nothing and apologizes for even less. Please be on the other side of it.
:::

### What a Day Is

A day ends when the party takes a Long Rest in a saferoom. If they have not rested by 24 in-game hours, the System rolls the day over anyway. On Floor 1, each day allows up to four short-rest slots as a ceiling, not a promise. {{HOUSE}}

The **final hour is fixed**. A Long Rest never ends a floor, and it never skips the collapse; a rest that would run past the collapse is cut short by the System. On Floor 1 the final collapse falls 24 in-game hours into Day 5, which is hour 120 of the floor. [VERIFY: the fixed final hour is a rules-team default awaiting Caleb's confirmation.]

### The Collapse Is Lethal, Everywhere

A missed collapse is death on every floor, **including Floor 1**. There is no toll, no haul-down and no Reprieve. The System tells you this at the start of Floor 1 and again on Days 4 and 5.

Here is exactly what happens:

- **Every Crawler still on the floor at the collapse dies.** No saves, no last stands.
- **Their credits and items are destroyed with the floor.**
- **Survivors may loot a body only while the floor still stands.** Anything you take before the collapse is yours. Nothing is looted after.
- **Non-player characters and the System's own staff go into stasis** and lose nothing.

This is a house rule built on a canon shape: in the source series, the floor falls and so does everything on it. {{INSPIRED}} Our version makes the loot rule explicit so that nobody argues about it while the floor is crumbling.

### Reaching a Stairwell

A stairwell is the only way down. You reach a stairwell when you step onto it, and it opens the way to the next floor. On Floor 1, the true stairwells appear about **32 hours after the floor opens**, early on Day 2. Other stairwells sit inside boss arenas, so every boss fight doubles as a very unfriendly front door. {{INSPIRED}}

**Early descent.** A Crawler who goes down the stairs with **more than 6 hours left** on the clock is held in **stasis** until the floor collapses. They have reached a stairwell, so they live and bank the achievement for it, but their time on that floor is over. Descending also ends that Crawler's Floor 1 Reprieve if it was still unused. In the last 6 hours, descent is free. Each Crawler chooses for themselves. {{INSPIRED}}

::: system
You went downstairs early. Bold. Boring. Your party will see you when the floor is done being on fire.
:::

### Reprieve: Tutorial Deaths Only

The **Floor 1 Reprieve** is **ON by default** for Season Zero (a table may turn it off at session zero; log `F1-REPRIEVE: ON/OFF`). Once per Crawler, on Floor 1 only, a *death in the tutorial* becomes "unconscious, ejected to the nearest saferoom at 1 hit point, with an embarrassing Title." {{HOUSE}} It expires when you descend.

The Reprieve covers tutorial deaths, such as a bad fight or a failed death save. It **never covers a missed collapse**. Missing the collapse is death on every floor, with no exceptions (decisions 026, 028, 029).

## Saferooms

A **saferoom** is a place the System has certified as safe. (Certification is not a guarantee. Certification is a sticker.)

- **Long Rests happen only in saferooms.** A Long Rest attempt outside a saferoom is interrupted and never completes (on Floor 1, Ticks arrive after 1d4 hours). Paying the field fee does not buy a Long Rest.
- **Short Rests** are the only rest you can take in the field. In a spot that is **already quiet**, a Short Rest is **free**. In a saferoom, Short Rests are guaranteed uninterrupted.
- **Loot boxes open only in saferooms.** A box in the field is a box you are carrying.
- Saferooms host **services**: achievement claims, vendors, a System terminal for appraisal, a bulletin board, and sometimes a bartender who has opinions about you.
- The usual 5e limit of one Long Rest per 24 hours still applies.

**Field rest fee (Floor 1).** The fee is **20 credits per Crawler**, capped at **60 credits per Crawler per floor**. It **buys quiet** so the party can take a Short Rest where the area is **not** already quiet. If the spot is already quiet, the Short Rest is free and no fee is charged. The fee never grants a Long Rest; Long Rests stay saferoom-only. {{HOUSE}} A saferoom rest is free. Later floors may print their own field fees; until they do, use the Floor 1 numbers at that floor's credit multiplier. (decision 031; core §5.6 v0.2.18)

Saferooms on Floor 1 are honest. **From Floor 2 on, some saferooms lie.** A false saferoom gives no rest, and some are ambushes. Searching a saferoom is allowed, wise, and occasionally rewarded with an achievement. {{HOUSE}}

This saferoom is certified 100% safe.* (*Certification not valid in this room.)
:::

## Credits, Vendors and Your System Inventory

### Credits

Money in the Crawl is **credits**, always paid and counted in **whole numbers only**. A balance is never fractional. Every price in the SRD equipment lists is paid in credits at face value: an item the SRD prices at 15 costs 15 credits. Copper and silver appear only as list prices on SRD gear (for reference: 1 sp = 0.1 credit, 1 cp = 0.01 credit); they are never a balance. Credits are the only money word in this book, because the System is proud of its currency and very sensitive about its branding.

- **Rounding (whole credits per line).** *Buying:* a sub-credit list price is charged rounded **up** to whole credits per cart line (one Torch costs 1 credit; ten Torches on one line also cost 1 credit). *Selling at half:* rounded **down** to whole credits per line, so an item worth under 2 credits sells for 0 unless you group several on one line.
- **Vendors** buy at standard prices (give or take the System's mood) and **sell back at half**.
- **Haggling** is a Charisma check against the vendor (Persuasion, Deception or Intimidation). A success knocks roughly a tenth to a quarter off.
- **Identifying items** works as 5e does (the *Identify* spell, or a Short Rest of study), or a System terminal will do it for a fee.
- **System fees** and **achievement rewards** are multiplied by a floor multiplier as you descend. Box credit pulls are not. The multiplier table lives in Chapter 6.

### Crafting Prices (Pointer)

Crafting tables are saferoom workstations bought with credits or coupons. Floors 1–2 keep the ruled prices **5,000 / 12,000 / 30,000** credits (basic table / Crafting Studio / Enhanced Crafting Studio). Floors 3+ follow a printed per-floor schedule in Chapter 6 (decision 030). An **Upgrade Coupon** (cap **25,000** credits) covers a **basic table through Floor 8** and a **Crafting Studio through Floor 5**; it **never** covers an Enhanced Studio. Level upgrades still take the credit multiplier and a coupon covers them only on **Floors 1–4** (from Floor 5, pay in credits). {{HOUSE}}

### The System Inventory

Every Crawler has a **System Inventory**: an extradimensional space that acts like a *Bag of Holding*. It holds 500 pounds and 64 cubic feet, and its contents weigh nothing. Putting an item in or taking one out takes your free object interaction, or the Utilize action if you have already used it. It will not hold a living creature. Unopened loot boxes take up no space.

If you die, your inventory spills as a **corpse cache** that anyone can loot, including whatever killed you. The exception is a missed collapse, where the floor takes the cache with it (see above).

## Achievements

The System is watching, and the System takes notes. When a Crawler does something notable (a first, an extreme, a stunt, a disaster, a character-defining choice), the System may announce an **achievement**.

::: achievement Read the Fine Print
You asked the System what a word in its own rules meant. In writing. Out loud.

Reward: The System's grudging respect, which is non-transferable, non-refundable and, strictly speaking, imaginary.
:::

Achievements are **guidelines, not a vending machine**. The System decides what is notable, when to announce it, and what (if anything) it pays. Many achievements pay nothing at all. A snark-only banner is a feature, and the System enjoys it more than you do. Achievements **never grant XP** and never auto-pass a failed check.

When an achievement does pay, it pays on a ladder:

| Tier | Name | What you get |
|:---:|---|---|
| R0 | Snark-only | Nothing. Logged. Maybe a cosmetic Title. |
| R1 | Pocket change | A small number of credits and/or a Title. |
| R2 | Heroic Inspiration | 5e Heroic Inspiration, as written. |
| R3 | Consumable | A potion, scroll, ammunition, oil or other one-use item. |
| R4 | Minor boon | A small Charm or blessing-style perk. |
| R5 | Loot box | A box, Bronze up to Legendary. |
| R6 | Headliner | A specific item at the floor's peak rarity, or a story unlock. |

The ladder tells the System how big a reward fits. It does not tell the System that a reward is *owed*. Chapter 5 covers the full ladder, rarity ceilings by floor, and a starter list of achievements.

## Loot Boxes

Loot boxes are the Crawl's treasure system. The System hands them out for achievements, bosses and floor clears, and each one is a small, shiny gamble. The first box most Crawlers ever open is a **Silver loot box** (Uncommon headline, two pulls): Floor 1's Boss Box is capped there, and a replacement Crawler's late-entry compensation is one Silver loot box too.

| Box | Headline item rarity | Pulls |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | Common | 1 to 2 |
| Silver | Uncommon | 2 |
| Gold | Rare | 2 to 3 (one guaranteed Rare) |
| Platinum | Very Rare | 3 (one guaranteed Very Rare) |
| Legendary | Legendary | 3 (one guaranteed Legendary) |
| Celestial | Legendary, plus a reward designed for you | 3, plus the reward |

**Achievement box tier by floor.** Achievement boxes on Floors 1–2 are **Bronze**. Silver there comes only from the Boss Box, or from a GM override with a stated reason. Expect about two or three achievement boxes per Crawler per floor there, as a soft guideline the System may exceed for a great moment. From Floor 3 the biggest achievements pay **Gold** (Floors 3–8) and then **Platinum** (Floors 9+); there are no Platinum awards on Floors 1–8. Boss and clear boxes still respect the floor's rarity ceiling. Chapter 5 has the full table. {{HOUSE}}

**Legendary cap.** Across the whole campaign a Crawler may receive at most **two** Legendary boxes from the ordinary pacing track. Story Boxes (Floors 1–2) are a **separate track** outside that cap; the GM handles Story Box awards and never prints deed lists in this book. {{HOUSE}}

Box rules in brief:

- **Boxes open only in saferooms.** There are no emergency opens.
- **When you open, you open them all.** Every unopened box you hold opens in one session, lowest tier first. You cannot save the good one.
- **Boxes are not tradeable.** They are bound to the Crawler who earned them. What comes out of them is ordinary loot and can be traded.
- **A Crawler's death reclaims their unopened boxes.** The System takes them back and does not apologize.
- **Celestial boxes are a story event**: one per Crawler for the whole campaign, awarded only for a finale or a truly absurd feat, at the System's sole discretion. {{HOUSE}}

Each floor has a rarity ceiling, so Floor 1 gives you Common treasure and a rare Uncommon, not a Legendary sword. Chapter 6 prints the ceilings, the tables, and the box names.

## Death and Revival

Death works as the 5e rules say: three failed **death saving throws**, massive damage, or an effect that says you die. The System announces every result publicly and has remarks.

**Death is permanent.** There are no respawns and no refunds on a corpse. The only ways back are rare, costly house rules, and this campaign runs them at their most expensive. {{HOUSE}}

- **Costly Revives (the default).** A revival needs a Voucher or a Rare Offer from the System and clears several gates. The rarity limits are one revival per party per three floors, three per campaign, and once per Crawler. Every revival also pays a large Fee and two menu picks, and leaves a lingering Revival Sickness. Chapter 9 covers the whole invoice.
- **Replacement Crawlers** are the *usual* outcome of a death. A replacement joins at the next saferoom at the party's lowest level, with a gear package suited to the floor and one Silver loot box as late-entry compensation.
- **Table options.** A table can instead pick **Canon Hard** (no revival at all; the shape of the source) or **Soft Open** (death is a setback, not an ending). Pick before Floor 1 and don't change mid-campaign.

::: system
Customer is deceased. Refund eligibility: none. Please enjoy the complimentary trading card.
:::

## Ratings and the Broadcast Name

The audience tracks every Crawler. Their opinion is called **Ratings**, and it comes in five steps.

| Ratings | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unknown | Background noise. Sponsors ignore you. |
| Local | A few fans. Minor sponsor interest (Floor 3 and later). |
| Trending | Real sponsor offers. Hunters know your face (Floor 6 and later). |
| Headliner | Courts and factions pay attention. Named enemies target you. |
| Legend | Gods, Production and the Cage care about you personally. |

Ratings rise on spectacle (wins, stunts, drama, great lines) and fall on boredom, cowardice the audience did not enjoy, and sponsor scandals. The System moves your tier at most one step per session. Ratings never make a separate currency; their main effect is that a crowd-pleasing moment may earn you **Heroic Inspiration**, with applause.

At the Kingdom Continuum on Floor 3, the System mints your **Broadcast Name** with you. Once per Long Rest, when you make a Charisma check where your fame could plausibly help, you may invoke it to gain Advantage. When fame cuts the other way (hunters, rivals, a hostile court), the System may impose Disadvantage on one such check per Long Rest, or have you recognized at a bad time. Until Floor 3, your sheet reads **Broadcast Name: PENDING**.

## Table Tone and Safety

The game is written for adults. The default table setting is **Ludicrous**.

| Category | At Ludicrous |
|---|---|
| Cursing | Cleared. Freely, in toasts and box names. |
| Sexual crudity | Cleared as innuendo and jokes. The System is crude; it does not narrate explicit scenes. |
| Bodily humor | Cleared. Vomit, slapstick gore, "a fun new smell." |
| "Your mom" jokes | Cleared. |
| Cruelty about cowardice and failure | Cleared. The System roasts retreats and bad plans without mercy. |
| The foot bit | Cleared. A segment of the audience is very into Crawlers' feet. The System reports it like ratings data. |

Some floors do not move. **The System roasts the choice, not the person.** No jokes land on a player's real identity (race, disability, sexuality or body) as opposed to what their Crawler did. Nothing sexual ever involves minors. Non-consent is never the punchline. And the System fades to black on request.

Every player also has a **private opt-out**. At creation you can tell the System which topics to cut: a **line** never appears at all, and a **veil** may happen off-screen but is never shown. During play, the word **"cut"** works instantly, with no explanation needed. The System drops the bit, doesn't mention it again, and doesn't make a joke of it. Opt-outs are per Crawler and are never shown to other players.
